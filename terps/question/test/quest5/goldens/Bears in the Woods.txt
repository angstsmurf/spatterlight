It's raining outside, but you are cuddled and warm. Your heartbeat rebounds off of your lover’s ribs, mingling rhythms. The radiator hums, warding off the cold. I’ve been seeing Grizzly bears swirling in the dark brown well of my morning coffee. They bound and splash after salmon, slurp and swallow, backstroke over the caffeine rapids. I see them everywhere—galloping down the wooden grains of my apartment floorboards, sleeping in puffs of pumpernickel bread at the bakery next door, rearing up from the muck of decomposed leaves in the garden. I think they know I can see them. When I leave for school in the morning, covered in books and worries, they nod their bear heads knowingly and return to their ritual berry licking in the red bricks by the door. Yesterday, I found a caribou flank on my stoop, a bloodstained Hallmark card tacked to the flesh:

“Thank you for your hospitality.

We are enjoying our stay.

Our only request—

that you cook the meat less.

Regards,

The Bears”

I folded the note carefully and transitioned my dinners from medium to a bloody rare. But I still see them growling in the furrow of my lover’s brow as he slices potatoes, and I watch them float languorously in the foam of his thick brown beer at dinner. They seem to like him, his unkempt nature. They nuzzle in his beard at night, tickling his nose with their big bear paws as he snores. Sometimes, when it’s dark and he is asleep, they lumber across my fingers while I write, lingering on my pinkies, reading everything. They love poetry the most. I’ve found comfort in their presence and in their familiar wild smell. They are so like us. How odd we must seem to them, oblivious to the taste of sweet grass, always searching for our life’s purpose when there is life all around us.
THE END.
