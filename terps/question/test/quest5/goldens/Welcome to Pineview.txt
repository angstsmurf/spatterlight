You're driving home late one night.

Your phone buzzes. It's a number you don't recognize.

You ignore it.

Your phone buzzes again. It's the same number.

You answer it. There is no one on the other end.
You look up to see yourself flying off the road and down a steep ditch. There is nothing in front of you but trees.
You don't remember much after that.

You drift in and out of conciousness.

A number of days pass.

You blink and open your eyes. The flourescent light above you is flickering slightly. You try to reach up, to wipe your face and rub your eyes, but you can't. Your arms are strapped to the bed.

"Mr. Adams?" The voice is unfamiliar.

You twist your neck towards the noise and mutter deliriously "Who?"

You try to sit up and a blinding pain shoots through your stomach. White light fills your vision and you pass out.

You have strange dreams.

The smell of fire. Of cooking meat. The sounds of something...fireworks, explosions, you can't tell.

You're lost in the woods. Some sort of shadowy figure dances in front of you.

It appears to be laughing.

Your feet are torn and bloody.

You fall to your knees.

You can't get back up.

You wake up. Sunlight is streaming in through the windows. You sit up in the bed, rubbing your eyes. Your arms are free.

You're in a hospital room, a fairly clean one, and someone has scrawled "Have a great day!" on the dry erase board on the far wall. You stand up and stretch. There is a small soreness in your stomach, but other than that, you feel great. You can't remember the last time you slept so well.

Actually, you can't remember the last time you slept. You can't even remember going to sleep.

Come to think of it, you can't remember anything.

The door opens suddenly and a small elderly woman in a nurse's uniform enters.

"Hello," she says. "I see you've woken up."

You shake your head. The elderly lady in the floral shirt smiles. "A little confused still? Don't worry, all of our patients feel that way when they come back from the anesthesia. It's our own special blend, you know?"

"I'm sorry," you say. "Where am I?"

"Why, you're in Pineview Hospital, silly!"

That means absolutely nothing to you. "What?"

"Pineview Hospital." The nurse looks at you a little more seriously, cocking her head to the side and narrowing her eyes. "Are you feeling okay, Mr. Adams?"

You shake your head again. "I'm sorry," you say. "Mr. Adams?"

She sighs deeply. "The doctor was worried about this. Are you experiencing memory loss?"

You nod.

"That's to be expected," she said. "The doctor told me to tell you to take it easy for the next few weeks. Don't try and push the memories, they will come back naturally."

"And what if they don't?"

"They will." The nurse looks down at her clipboard. "How are you feeling physically?"

You shrug. "Like new, I guess. I can't remember ever feeling better."

"Well, that's wonderful!" The nurse's worried look vanished as quickly as it started. "The doctor has informed me that everything went well with the surgery, and you can go home whenever you want." She smiled. "Just try and take it easy for the next couple of weeks. Keep the activity light. Don't worry about anything."

You want to ask more, but she turns quickly and walks away. You look around the room and spy a backpack in the corner.

Opening it, you find a set of clothes and a small identification card from some local college. The name on the front is Casey Adams, and by admiring your reflection in the window, you can tell that the picture is definitely of you.

"Casey Adams," you say out loud. The name is unfamiliar on your lips. You shrug and slide the ID card into the back pocket of your new pants.

PINEVIEW UNIVERSITY ID CARD added to inventory.

The nurse returns with a few forms that you sign quickly, and soon you are wheeled to the front door of the hospital.

"Thank you," you say to the orderly with the wheelchair.

"Whatever," he responds, and closes the door abruptly. Confused, you turn around and begin walking.

Notes on the River Palms Epidemic:

The River Palms Epidemic of 1862 was an onslaught of mass hysteria that occurred in and around the small town of River Palms, now a part of Moon River County. The epidemic began on January 30th, 1862 at a small Christian-run boarding school for females a distance outside of the city limits. It spread randomly and slowly at first, only affecting two or three students a day, but soon 95 out of the 159 pupils were incapacitated with the disease. There were no adults involved in this first outbreak. Students were reported as being ‘unable to focus’ on their lessons, and the school was forced to close down by mid-March.

The epidemic was soon spotted in Michelsville, home to several of the homebound schoolgirls. Throughout the next few months, symptoms were reported in 217 members of the community, including children and young adults. The school was reopened on May 21st and closed again at the end of June, at which point the epidemic also spread to Pineview.

The original school in River Palms was sued by multiple families and closed its doors for good the following year. Other school and cities were also affected. Eight months after the River Palms Epidemic began it immediately ended at 4:43PM. No one knew why.

Symptoms included respiratory problems, fainting, rashes, excessive eye watering, nerve pain, alien hand syndrome, and, of course, excessive, uncontrollable laughter.
