Once there was an old King who had three children. The eldest was a boy, brave and handsome, and of him the King was very proud. The next child was a girl, and the King was no less proud of her, for she was clever and resourceful and skilled in many arts. But the youngest child seemed to have no talents of any kind, and idled away long days in reading the ancient Bestiaries, which were full of strange stories about incredible animals living in far-away places. His youngest child was the despair of the old King.

One year, a terrible blight struck the land. The corn went rotten in the fields, the cattle fell sick and died, and even the fish in the sea dwindled in number. The King's Chancellor called the wise men of the country together, and they scratched their heads and read all the books they could find, but not one of them could say why the Blight had come, nor how it could be brought to an end. The King grew angry, and would have had all the wise men put to death, but the Chancellor pleaded with him, saying: "These men are not to blame; perhaps the answer to the Blight is not to be found within our country, so how should we expect these men, who have lived all their lives here, to find it?" And at last the King agreed with him.

...

Then the King's eldest child spoke. "If the answer to the Blight lies outside our country, then I shall go out and search for it, and I shall not rest until I have found it or perished in the attempt." The King was very pleased, and gave him a horse with a golden bridle to carry him in his travels. And all the people of the city gathered to watch him as he rode through the streets and set out on his journey. But the days passed, and the weeks, and the months, and still he did not return.

Then the King's second child said: "I too shall go and search for an answer to the Blight, since my brother has been unsuccessful." And the King was very pleased and gave her a fur-lined cloak to protect her. Once again the people of the city gathered to watch her as she set out on her journey. But the days passed, and the weeks, and the months, and still she did not return.

Then the King's youngest child said: "My brother and my sister have been unlucky, it seems. But tomorrow I shall set out to follow them, and surely I shall find an answer to the Blight." At this the King did not know whether to laugh or to cry. How could the hopeless one succeed where the better had failed? But at last he gave his consent.

...

The youngest child stood in the audience chamber of the palace, expecting a gift such as the King had given the others. But the King only said: "You already have a small hunting knife; that can be your weapon. I gave you a copper penny yesterday; that will be your cash. And your own two legs will, I think, be more then able to carry you for all the distance you are likely to get, my little one." And with that, the King and the Chancellor went out of the chamber, leaving the youngest child alone...
