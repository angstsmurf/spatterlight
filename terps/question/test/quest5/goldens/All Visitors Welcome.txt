All Visitors Welcome

by Bitter Karella

You startle awake -- just in time to see the last sliver of sun dip below the tree-covered horizon.

How long were you asleep?  Granted, you don't see much traffic up here at Sloane Rock State Historic Park during the off season. You don't think you've seen a single visitor all week.  But it still wouldn't be good if one of the rangers happened by and saw you snoozing on the job.  You need this job.

When you saw the ad on Craigslist for a "part-time seasonal interpretive specialist" at a state park, you thought it sounded pretty exciting.  But it turns out that Sloane Rock is pretty far off the beaten path, way out in the dense, mountainous pine woods of the high Sierra and only the most dedicated tourists make the trek during the summer.  In the autumn, it's dead.

Which is presumably why they leave park maintenance to an underpaid peon like you.  All you really do is unlock the doors in the mornings, clean the bathrooms, occasionally give directions to motorists who get lost on their way to something more exciting, and take passive-aggressive abuse from the rangers whenever they stop by on their rounds. Oh, and chase away those pesky teenagers.

You stretch and check your watch.  7:00 p.m. The day shift ended two hours ago.  Where is Roy? He was supposed to relieve you so that you could finally go home.

At your training, the Head Ranger was really adament that the visitor center never be unattended because of some incident a few years back when a few punk kids spraypainted graffiti all over the outside walls.  Whatever.  You haven't seen another soul all day.

So where IS Roy?

> ask binder about lazarus sloane

> ask binder about lazarus sloane
Little is known of the early life of Lazarus Sloane. He made the overland journey to California in 1849 after news arrived from the west of the gold discovery at Sutter's Mill on the American River.

By all accounts, Sloane was an ambitious man not shy to use any means to get ahead.  After arriving in California, Sloane spent two unsuccessful seasons mining in Coloma.  Local newspapers mention that Sloane was involved in several attacks on Chinese and Chilean mining camps.

After growing disillusioned with mining, Sloane traveled north, where his luck would change.  He eventually befriended and partnered with a Dutch immigrant named Mureen van der Wald who had acquired a 2200 acre Mexican land grant called Rancho de Piedra Grande.  The relationship was reportedly stormy, the two men often coming to blows over differing opinions for how to develop the ranch. Van der Wald was mauled to death by wild animals in 1853, leaving his land to Sloane.

Sloane mounted several expeditions to eradicate the local Pombo Indians before razing more of the forest on the lower acreage to make room for expanded animal husbandry operations. Left alone, Sloane manifested an until now unknown aptitude for management, eventually turning the newly christened Sloane Ranch into a successful meat production empire that sold beef, pork, mutton and other meats all over the country and the world.
> ask binder about jaskakno

> ask binder about jaskakno
Jask'akno is a mythical figure associated with Sloane Rock in the folklore of the Pombo Indians. Since the Pombo left no written records, very little is known about it -- Jask'akno is mentioned several times in Lazarus Sloane's diaries. In one instance, Sloane demanded that a Pombo brave tell him about the secret details of the tribe's religious ceremonies, but was rebuffed as it would anger Jask'akno for a white man to know such things.   It is believed that the petroglyphs on Sloane Rock might be intended to either summon or ward away Jask'akno.

Folklorist D.E. Holt identified Jask'akno as the trickster in the Pombo legend 'How the Dark Started.'
