The Swan
Part I - The Descendant of the Swan

by Nomad

One lazy morning, two girls sat upon a bridge. One was fishing, the other sunbathed, if such a thing was possible on so gloomy a day.

"Mara, I just don't understand what your problem is," said the girl with the fishing rod in her hands.

"Loya, you will do!" replied the other girl.

At sixteen summers, Mara was one year older than Loya. The girls were talking about future grooms. Loya had no reason to think about such things yet, but seventeen was the traditional age at which yellow-gown girls got married. Loya, though, was not a traditional yellow-gown.

"The only reason yellow-gown girls have always got married when they're seventeen is that they've always got married when they're seventeen. Honestly, I'm never going to get married. I'm going to see the caves of Kantara, the cliffs of Eaht, and the peaks of Nara," said Loya.

"Yeah, right!" said Mara. "Yellow-gown boys can become farmers in their villages, and we can marry a farmer from our village. We've got no other options."
"You're all dog ugly anyway," came a voice. Loya turned. A blue-gown boy called Vin was now on the bridge. Loya stood up. He might have been from a higher caste, but how dare he speak to her like that. Loya had never understood why the blue-gowns had rights the yellow-gowns didn't!

"Get out of my way worm!" he exclaimed. With that, he shoved Loya. She stumbled into Mara, knocking her friend off the bridge in the river. Loya's fishing line also flew into the river and drifted away almost as quickly as her friend did. The boy sauntered off to the west, his hands in his pockets and with no concern for that yellow-gown girl who was now being swept out to sea.

Loya wondered what to do. Should she dive into the river to save her friend, who could not swim, or should she try to teach the boy a lesson? Her rage told her the latter option was the better!

Loya entered the canoe. From nowhere, gallumphed the fat monk. With a guffaw, he said, "My beamish girl, callooh callay," he said. "Snicker-snack, your vorpal stone hast bound the bandersnatch. You have helped the The Order of the Swan prevent actions most manxome taking place, for the Orders of the Eagle and the Dragon seek to cause uffishness, and only you can prevent it.

"Great Demon speed you to Maranga, from whence seek Professor Zorek! He is a Swan most trusted and most frabjous."

The fat monk chuckled once more. "For the moment, our fate moves to the hands of girl named Yina, the whiffling child you met in the mansion."

Then he chortled again. "Fear not, my girl, for you shall soon be the focus of this adventure once more. No fear not, for you shall set eyes upon the caves of Kantara, the cliffs of Eaht and the peaks of Nara, for it is written in the prophecy."

With that, the fat monk pulled a white stone from his pocket and began whispering, "'Twas brililig and the slithy toaves did gyre and gimble in the wabe..." No sooner had he got to the part of the rhyme where the momeraths were outgribben, did Loya's canoe begin to float towards the open seas.  Her canoe glided swiftly the waves of the river until she found herself on the open seas sailing towards the blockade and beyond to the magnificent city of Maranga.

Moments later, the descendant of the Swan heard a huge bang. As she turned, she saw her beloved island in flames.
