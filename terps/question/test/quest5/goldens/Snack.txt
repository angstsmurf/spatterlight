My name is Aedan, and I don't know where I am.

Let me back up.

I grew up on the streets of Touboro, a small commune along the eastern border of Cameroon. It was hot, dry, and quiet. In a word, paradise.

Now don't get me wrong, I've lived a life far from perfect. My father died when I was six months old, and the locals like to joke that my mother didn't even stick around for my birth. Had I been born into a colder climate I probably would've died before I learned to crawl. As it is, however, I learned to raise myself, and over time I've discovered three rules for surviving on the streets:

   1. Stay warm
   2. Eat slowly
   3. Keep your head low to the ground

If you keep to these, there's nothing on earth that you can't squirm out of. Unfortunately, as I've discovered, there are other things to worry about.

Last night I laid under the stars, the world around me calm, still. Even the lark stays its cry come nightfall, and only the southern river separates a minute from an instant. But ashes to ashes, as the proverbs say, and peace soon came to pass. A bright light appeared in the sky above me, and a shapeless black mass lifted me from where I'd lain. Slate gray walls enclosed around me, and the world went black.

Time went by. Perhaps minutes. Perhaps days. Sleep and wake blend together when the world disappears. Eventually the walls were lifted, and I woke up here.

My name is Aedan, and I don't know where I am. But I do know that something brought me here, and that it's going to come back for me. I can't stay here. It's coming.
