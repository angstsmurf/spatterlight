INTRODUCTION.
It's a tragic day for your quaint little town. Your favourite childhood attraction, the Earth of the Future Exhibition, is being torn down. Yes, it's just a single convention hall full of flashing lights, shabby robots and inventions that seemed cool and futuristic in about 1985, but you loved it and are crushed that it's going to be demolished. Those behind it, led by the eccentric Professor Patrick Pending, had tried to find support but with his untimely death, about a year ago, that had come to nothing.

The day before the exhibition is due to go down, you call them and ask if there's any chance you can go in for a last-minute private walk through. To say goodbye.

They say yes.

You arrive in the early evening and walk into the building and towards the exhibition hall, writing a eulogy for the place in your head as you go. You enter through an automatic door at the south end of the room, slightly left of centre. As soon as you do, your feet land on a travelator, a moving walkway. It slowly moves you around the room, rolling you past the exhibits on your left and right.

A heavy sense of loss settles into your stomach. Okay, the entertainment value isn't great when you look at it with objective eyes, but some of the technology is amazing. The creators have some incredible talent. It's crazy that they've gone so broke the place has to be destroyed. There must be something, something, that's worth the world's attention. Something life-changing. Something that can save this place, and the livelihoods of its designers.

You think about it as the travelator conveys you towards the exit, when suddenly, the whole thing jerks to a halt. You almost lose your footing. Once you've stabilised yourself, you roll your eyes – well, at least it waited until you were finished to break down! Guess you have to use your feet to get to the door. You walk up to it... but it doesn't open. You try the other door, the entrance one; again, it doesn't open. Well, this is frustrating. You glance up at the security cameras – five of them, interspersed along the ceiling between exhibits – and give them an urgent wave, trying to get a staff member's attention, but after several minutes nobody has come to your aid.

Look, in all likelihood, some janitor somewhere will come in here and find you. But you have no idea how long that will take. And this exhibition is being shut down, demolished tomorrow... maybe there aren't any janitors working here anymore. You should probably find a way to get those doors working yourself – it may be the only way to 100% guarantee your safety!

Exhibition Hall
With trepidation, you examine your surroundings...

To the left of the door through which you entered is an exhibit labelled 'The Family of the Present', with half a dozen statues in it representing, you guessed it, a modernish family of today. Then, on the west wall, there are two exhibits displaying two different time periods of the past, showing off their greatest achievements and inventions. The first is an 1800s exhibit, and the second is 1900s. The entire north wall is covered in a mural. The right side of the room is a mirror image of the left, only with different exhibits. Against the right wall are exhibits for the 2000s and the 2100s, containing models of the designers' predictions for what great inventions will come in the future.

In the centre of the room is a huge square exhibit that's been split into four sections. On one side are the Bedroom of the Future and Kitchen of the Future. On the other side are the Sports of the Future and School of the Future. On the south wall, to the right of the entrance, is the automatic door through which you were expecting to exit, and beside it another exhibit, this one labelled 'The Family of the Future'.

Around the ceiling there are five security cameras.
