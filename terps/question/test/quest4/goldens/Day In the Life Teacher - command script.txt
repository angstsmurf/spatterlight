Day In the Life: Teacher -- Jordan, ASL 350
===========================================

Mr. Smith teaches 7th grade, and the game is his timetable: wake up, find the
car keys, remember your lunch, teach nine periods, grade the papers, go back to
bed.  The whole thing is a linear chain of `show`/`hide` on hidden objects, with
exactly one thing that can go wrong.

It is also a QDK Pro 3.53 game with no compass directions at all.  Every exit is
a `place <Room>`, so movement is GO TO <ROOM>, and the two floors of the house
are joined only by two hand-written room commands:

    define room <Upstairs Hallway>
        command <go down stairs> goto <Downstairs Hallway>
    define room <Downstairs Hallway>
        command <go up stairs> goto <Upstairs Hallway>

The descriptions tell you so, which is just as well, because GO TO DOWNSTAIRS
HALLWAY does not work from upstairs.

TAKE does everything
--------------------
There is no custom verb in the game.  Every interaction is the object's `take`
script, whatever it actually represents:

    take light             turn on the closet light  (reveals the car keys)
    take Refridgerator     open the fridge and pocket your lunch
    take Car               drive to school (only `if got <car keys>`)
    take Papers            collect the ungraded papers at the final bell
    take The Car           drive home from school
    take The Bed           go to sleep -- `take playerwin`, the ending

and TAKE RECEPTIONIST gets you "No way! I don't want to touch HER!".  Teaching a
class is SPEAK TO instead: each period is a hidden object whose `speak` script
prints one of three shared `define text` blocks (LA 1, Science, Math -- reused
three times each), hides itself, shows the next period, and stamps the clock.

The clock
---------
    define variable <The Time>  type numeric  value <8>  display <It is ! o' clock.>

It is a plain number, not a time, so the status pane cycles through "It is 8 o'
clock", "It is 830 o' clock", "It is 915 o' clock", "It is 10 o' clock", 11, 12,
1245, 130, 215, 3, 345, 6, 630 and finally "It is 9 o' clock" for bedtime.  Every
period sets it directly; nothing ever reads it.

The one way to lose -- and the two ways not to
----------------------------------------------
Lunchtime is the only branch.  In the Teacher's Lounge:

    define object <Other Teachers>
        speak if got <Deluxe Cuisine> then { ...chat, show <2nd Math>... }
        else { msg <You sit down and realize-- you forgot your lunch at home!!!!>
               playerlose }

So you must be holding the Deluxe Cuisine before 12 o'clock, and there are two
ways to get it:

  1. TAKE REFRIDGERATOR in the Kitchen before leaving home.  This is what the
     lose text tells you to do ("Next time remember to check the fridge at home")
     and it is what the script below does.

  2. Forget it, and in Room 101 TAKE DESK, which yields a Package addressed to
     Mrs. Swoon; SPEAK TO MRS. SWOON in Room 102 and she recognises it as her own
     lunch, gives it to you, and keeps her PB&J.

The two are mutually exclusive, deliberately:

    define object <Desk>
        take { if got <Deluxe Cuisine> then msg <You attempt to pick up the huge
               desk, but since you didn't do well in Gym, you strain some
               muscles.|n"Ow."> else { ...give <Package>... } }

Once the fridge lunch is in your hands the desk is just a desk, so route 1 closes
route 2 for good.  (And if you have already given Mrs. Swoon the package, taking
the fridge lunch too is impossible -- you were never home again.)

Order of periods
----------------
The chain is fixed and each link is only reachable in one room, so the walk is a
lot of trips through School- Hallway.  Room 101 is Language Arts, 104 Math, 105
Science, and the Teacher's Lounge holds both lunch and the papers:

    Room 101  speak to 1st Class      8:30 -> 9:15
    Room 101  speak to 2nd Class      9:15 -> 10:00
    Room 105  speak to 1st Science   10:00 -> 11:00
    Room 104  speak to 1st Math      11:00 -> 12:00
    Lounge    speak to Other Teachers  12:00 -> 12:45   (needs the lunch)
    Room 104  speak to 2nd Math      12:45 -> 1:30
    Room 101  speak to 3rd Class      1:30 -> 2:15
    Room 105  speak to 2nd Science    2:15 -> 3:00
    Room 105  speak to 3rd Science    3:00 -> 3:45, and the Papers appear
    Lounge    take Papers            3:45 -> 6:00, and The Car appears
    Front     take The Car           6:00 -> 6:30, and you are home
    Dining    use Papers             -> 9:00, and The Bed appears
    Bedroom   take The Bed           playerwin

MY SCHEDULE, lying on the front-hall floor, is the in-game version of that table
(USE MY SCHEDULE prints it), and it is accurate apart from listing "6:00-
Departure" as the last entry.

The toilet trap
---------------
Nothing else in the game can be got wrong, but one thing can waste half an hour:

    define object <Toilet>       (Bathroom)
        take if ask <Do I really need to go?> then {
            timeron <Toilet>
            destroy exit <Bathroom; Upstairs Hallway>
            hide <Toilet> }
        else say <No, I don't need to.>

    define timer <Toilet> interval <30> ... create exit <Bathroom; Upstairs Hallway>

Answer YES and the door out of the bathroom is destroyed for thirty turns, with
no exits at all and nothing to do; the timer then flushes and rebuilds it.  It is
the game's only timer and its only `destroy exit`.  This script stays out of the
bathroom.

Other scenery: the Living Room TV is `take choose <TV>`, a `choose` pointed at a
prose `define text` rather than a list of options, so it opens a menu with
nothing in it -- avoid.  The Lunch Lady in the cafeteria and Mrs. Swoon's
no-package greeting are flavour.  The Garage has no `place` exits whatsoever, so
once you walk in the only way out is to drive to school.
