YOU ARE A TIGER (YOU ARE A TIGER.cas, QDK 4.1.5, asl-version 410)
=================================================================

This is not a winnable game; it is the opening scene of one.  The whole file is
a single room -- SWEET-ASS BEDROOM -- with no exits of any kind, no score, no
playerwin, and nothing that ends the game.  You wake up hungover as a rap
mogul, you are told "Didn't you have something you needed to do today? Better
figure it out!", and then there is no way to leave the bedroom and do it.  Two
of the counters the author set up (Oontz Completion, AcheQuotes Found) exist
only to be incremented; nothing ever reads them.  Sam is waiting in a living
room that was never written, and Eazy's backing track can be found on the
computer but never finished or sent.

So this script is a furthest-reachable-state transcript rather than a
walkthrough: it visits everything the room contains, plays all six voicemails,
and then empties both consumables, which is the last state change the game has
to offer.  The win marker is the burrito's "You ate the whole thing" line -- the
end of the content, not a victory.

What the script does, in order, and why that order:

  * LOOK AT BED, SPEAK TO BED, then LOOK UNDER BED.  "look under" is one of the
    game's own verbs (`verb <look under>`), and the bed's `action <look under>`
    is what produces the Jack Daniels: the bottle is defined `hidden`, and the
    action does `give` + `show`.  Until then there is no whiskey to drink.

  * LOOK AT COMPUTER before the voicemails.  Looking at it sets the TrackFind
    flag, and Eazy's message branches on it: with the flag you get the "so that
    unfinished track on your computer is for Eazy..." follow-up, without it just
    "Did you finish that track before you went out last night?".  This is the
    only piece of state in the game that two different scenes share.

  * LOOK AT PHONE followed by a bare "1".  Both LOOK AT PHONE and the game's
    own CHECK VOICEMAIL command run `choose <voicemail>`, and a selection eats
    the next line of the script as its answer -- the answer is the 1-based index
    of the choice, so the six numbered lines here are Girlfriend, Sister, Eazy,
    Sam, Dad, Unknown Caller.  (SPEAK TO PHONE is a plain property, no menu.)

  * DRINK JACK DANIELS three times, EAT BURRITO three times.  Both objects start
    at `properties <volume = 30>` and shrink with
    `property <obj; volume = { #obj:volume# - 10 }>`, i.e. Quest's inline-brace
    arithmetic, which only exists from ASL 3.91 on.  On the third go the volume
    reaches 0, the `if is <#obj:volume#;0>` test fires, the object hides itself
    and prints its closing line.  Note the whiskey prints "Looks like you're all
    out of this stuff!" *before* "Ahh, mother's milk" -- the author put the test
    ahead of the message -- and that the burrito's blank `msg <>` is why there is
    an empty line after each bite.

  * LOOK AT MIRROR twice, once at the start and once at the end.  The mirror is
    a `select case <%abs%>` over eleven exact multiples of ten; abs starts at 50
    and each bite of burrito knocks off 10, so the first look gives the abs-50
    text and the last the abs-20 one.  Any value the cases do not list would
    print nothing at all, which is why the script eats exactly three times.

Nothing else in the room does anything: the desk, and every lib verb in the
QDK-generated header, only print refusals.
