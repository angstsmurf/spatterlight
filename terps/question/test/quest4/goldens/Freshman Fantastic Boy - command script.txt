Freshman Fantastic Boy
Freshman Fantastic Boy.cas, v1.0, ASL 410, "Created with QDK 4.1.5", author Marilyn

An adult game set at Fantastic University, a low-gravity space-station college.
Seventeen rooms, ten women, and a single number to optimise.  There is no
`playerwin` anywhere in the file: the win condition is an `afterturn` block that
fires when ten flags are all set *and* the score is exactly 10.  Everything
below is bookkeeping -- who gates on what, and which conversational branches
give you the point rather than just the flag.  124 turns, every room visited,
score 10 of 10.

The prose is pornographic and the script below runs through all of it; nothing
in this file reproduces it.


THE WIN CHECK, AND THE TRAP IN IT

    afterturn {
        if flag <faesatisfied> and flag <teressasatisfied> and flag <smithsatified>
           and flag <lizbethsatisfied> and flag <elindasatified> and flag <bethsatisfied>
           and flag <renesatisfied> and flag <janesatisfied>
           and flag <thistlethimblesatisfied> and flag <dimplebottomsatisfied> then {
            if is <%score%;10> then msg <...You Stud!...> else msg <You didn't manage to...>
        }
    }

Note `smithsatified` and `elindasatified` -- the author dropped the s in both,
and since Quest flags are created by first use the typos are simply what those
two flags are called.  They are spelled the same way at both ends, so they work.

The trap is the `else`.  Seven of the ten conversations offer a branch that sets
the flag but does not `inc <score; 1>`:

    elinda2  "feel her up"                    flag, no point (and no elinda3)
    elinda3  "no"                             flag, no point
    rene2    "no"                             flag, no point
    lizbeth3 "no"                             flag, no point
    jane2    "Sex" / "A thank you would be nice"   flag, no point
    jane3    "no"                              flag, no point
    thistlethimbl2  revenge / cop a feel       flag, no point

Take any of them and the game becomes unwinnable without saying so: once the
tenth flag lands, the `afterturn` starts printing "You didn't manage to sleep
with everybody" after *every* subsequent command, forever.  That repeating
message is the only failure notice in the game -- there is no `playerlose`
either, and `define text <lose>` is never referenced.

Beth is the exception: her third selection sets no flag at all on the wrong
branches, so a mistake there is recoverable -- SPEAK TO BETH again.


THE HOTNESS ECONOMY

Every gift to a woman is guarded by `if is <%hotness%;gt;N>`; note `gt`, not
`gt=`, so you need N+1.  Hotness starts at 0.

  entrance form, Major   +2 for P.E., Space Law, Medicine or Spaceship Piloting
                         +1 for Cosmic Art, Robotics or Spaceship Design
  USE COLOGNE            +1  (once; the bottle hides itself)
  USE MACHINE in the gym +1  (once, `usedequipment`)
  USE SHOWER in the gym  +1  (once, `showerused`)
  each conquest          +1

That is 5 points of non-conquest hotness, and the ten gates are:

  Fae            >1   give her the student compass she lost
  Teressa        >2   give her the lunch Spaulding hands over
  Elinda         >2   give her the tea pot Spocter has in his pocket
  Beth           >4   checked on the *conversation*, not a gift
  Rene           >5   give her Beelfetter's medicine
  Lizbeth        --   no hotness gate at all, only `flag <mapfixed>`
  Dimplebottom   >7   give her the bra from the girls' dorm dresser
  Dr Smith       >8   give her the repaired cleaning robot
  Thistlethimble >9   give her the rare painting
  Professor Jane >10  give her the case transcripts

Since each conquest is worth a point of hotness, the gates are satisfiable in
exactly the printed order with only the Major bonus, but the script takes the
cologne, the machine and the shower anyway -- they buy three turns of slack and
they are two of the three things RATE, EXAMINE and USE exist for.  Order matters
only in that you cannot do Jane before nine others; the script's route is
chosen to keep the walking down, which is why Teressa (gate >2) happens at
hotness 6.

RATE <woman> reports her `sexy` property, i.e. the number you must beat.  The
command is defined as `command <rate#@object#>` -- no space before the
parameter -- and matches RATE FAE regardless, in Quest and here.


CREDITS

The Job selection is the only source of starting money: Towel Boy 150, Library
Monitor 100, Janitor 50.  Teressa's shop sells cologne 50, calculator 10,
flowers 20, towel 20 and a map of the galaxy 20 -- 120 credits for the lot, so
Towel Boy buys everything at once.  Dimplebottom's "Ask about your work study
job" adds 10 credits and can be repeated as often as you like, so no choice here
is fatal; it is just slower.

Only the map is load-bearing (Lizbeth).  The flowers buy the Book of Poems from
Samuel, which is where the answer to Beth's riddle comes from.  The calculator
and the towel buy hints: Tom names Dimplebottom's bra fetish, Bud Dearborn tells
you about the machine and the shower.


THE SUPPORTING CHAIN

  SEARCH                 a game command, hard-keyed to two rooms:
                           in `passage`  -> the Key
                           in `closet`   -> the Rare painting
                         anywhere else it prints "you don't find anything".
  EXAMINE DRESSER        girls' dorm; `action <examine>` shows the Bra.
  EXAMINE MACHINE        gym; `action <examine>` shows the case transcripts.
  USE KEY                needs `exists <box>`, so carry the tool box out of
                         classroom 101; it `remove`s the Tool from the box and
                         shows it in the room, so TAKE TOOL afterwards.
  USE TOOL               needs `exists <cleaning robot>`; sets `robotfixed`.
  GIVE MAP TO ALDRIN     teacher's lounge; sets `mapfixed`.  Giving Lizbeth an
                         uncorrected map just hands it back.
  GIVE FLOWERS TO SAMUEL passage; hands over the Book of Poems, whose first line
                         is "Love is like a fish that yearns for yarn".  Beth's
                         `enter <poemword>` wants exactly `yarn`.
  SPEAK TO SPOCTER       classroom 102, third choice: the tea pot.
  SPEAK TO BEELFETTER    his office, second choice ("Rene is sick"): the
                         medicine.  It tests no flag, so you can collect it
                         before you have ever met Rene.
  SPEAK TO SPAULDING     cafeteria, third choice: the lunch.


GETTING AROUND

The corridor and the passage use `place` rather than compass exits for five
rooms -- passage, classroom 101, classroom 102, Professor Jane's Office,
Professor Beelfetter's Office -- so the command is GO TO PASSAGE, GO TO
CLASSROOM 101 and so on.  The offices and classrooms also have `out`, which is
shorter.  GO PASSAGE and ENTER PASSAGE both fail.

Six of the conquests end with a `goto` that teleports you: Fae, Dimplebottom and
Elinda drop you in the girls' dorm, Teressa in the closet, Beth, Thistlethimble
and Jane in the corridor, and Rene all the way back to the orientation room.
The script's route is built around those, which is why it walks the
hallway--store--corridor spine so many times.


AUTHORING ODDITIES (all harmless)

  * `command <>` sits in the passage room definition -- an empty user command
    with an empty name.  It matches nothing and does nothing.
  * The lunch and the robot both `hide` themselves on success and then `give`
    themselves back on the next line, outside the branch the author meant; you
    end the game carrying two invisible objects.
  * The hallway's description says the boys' dorm is north and the girls' south;
    the exits are the other way round.
  * Doctor Spocter's `speak` guards on `flag <done>` -- the flag the tea pot
    branch sets -- so after you have the pot he only says his greeting.
  * Professor Beelfetter and, oddly, Jim, Bud Dearborn and the exercise machine
    are all `displaytype <Object>`; only the women are `Character`.

Nothing in this game needed an engine change: it loaded, parsed and played
correctly on the first attempt, including the `place` navigation, the
`rate#@object#` pattern, the `enter <poemword>` string compare and the
ten-way `and` chain in the `afterturn`.
