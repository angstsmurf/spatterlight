Green Light
Carlii Cortez, 2002, v1.0 "Prereleased", ASL 310, "Created with QDK 3.1"

Green text on a black background, Verdana 8, and a game blurb that warns you it
is "long and often complex and challenging" and begs you to save often.  It is
in fact a single tower with fourteen rooms in it, and the whole thing is one
lock-and-key chain plus one cooking recipe.  You play Jem, and the ending tells
you what you are.


THE THREE SLOT CARDS

The tower room you start in has three blank panels and a grate in the floor, and
every card opens one more of the tower's hidden doors:

  card A -> panel A   creates  tower room --south--> lab
  card B -> panel B   creates  tower room --southwest--> bedroom
  card C -> panel C   creates  bedroom --south--> library   (the endgame)

Card A is under the grate and needs the crowbar.  Card B is in the kitchen
cupboard.  Card C is in the storeroom, behind the monster.  Each `use card X on
panel X` does `create exit`, `lose` and `hide`, so the cards are consumed.

Your starting inventory arrives by the QDK 3.1 idiom of putting objects in a
room literally called `inventory` -- the crowbar is defined there and you are
holding it on turn one.  There is a second bookkeeping room, `spare room`, that
holds every object the game will later conjure into your hands (card B, the cup
of water, the dough, all the bread stages).  It has a `west <tower room>` exit
and a description about a huge window, but nothing anywhere leads into it, so it
is limbo with a door.


THE CUPBOARD

`x cupboard` in the kitchen opens a nine-drawer selection, one drawer per look:

  drawer 4  card B          (guarded by `if got <card B>` -- see below)
  drawer 7  1 gold coin     (guarded by the string variable #goldcoin#)
  drawer 8  a cup           (guarded by the string variable #cup#)

and the other six are "nothing :(".  Drawer 4's guard is a `got` test rather
than a one-shot flag, so once you have spent card B on panel B the drawer
cheerfully produces another one.  Nothing needs a second card B, but it is the
kind of thing that tells you which drawers the author tested.

The cup is the one that matters: it becomes the cup of milk, and the milk is
what gets you past the monster.  Do not use the fountain room's
`fill cup with water` command on it -- that swaps the cup for a cup of water,
which the milking procedure does not accept and which the monster (twice stated
to be allergic to water) would not drink anyway.

The `coins` variable that drawer 7 and the vase feed is displayed in the pane
and never read by anything.  The vase pays `$rand(3;5)$` coins, so the number is
different every run.


THE FIGARI BREAD

The recipe is printed on the kitchen's cooking book (`read book`) and this is
the whole middle of the game:

  flour  =  wheat -> grain -> hopper -> flour, carried in the pot
            (cythe from the shed to `pick wheat`; `take flour` needs the pot)
  water  =  `use jug on fountain`, jug from the potions room
  clove  =  drop the giant's vase in the bedroom
  sugar  =  `use vial on sack of sugar` in the pantry, vial from the potions room

  use jug of water on pot with flour      -> dough
  use Clove of Figari on dough            -> figari dough
  use figari dough on baking dish         -> uncooked Figari bread
  use uncooked Figari bread on oven       -> Figari bread
  use vial of sugar on Figari bread       -> sugared Figari bread

Each step is a procedure that loses one object and gives the next, so the chain
has to run in exactly that order.  The vase is `take`-then-`drop`: taking it
warns you it is delicate, dropping it smashes it and spills the clove and the
coins.  (Taking the pieces of ceramic afterwards is optional and does nothing;
the game just likes that you tidied up.)

Milk is separate: `milk cow` in the wheatfield needs the cup *and* the gloves
from the shed, per the sign in the field.

Getting to the wheatfield at all needs the gold key from the balcony, and the
balcony needs the whole bedroom sequence: climb the giant's bed, `pull string`
(which drops a ladder and dumps you back on the floor), climb the bed again, then
`climb ladder`.  `use key on window` in the fountain room creates the place exit
out into the field.


THE MONSTER

The eight-eyed monster stands in the fountain room's east exit, which is a
script that refuses to move you while it is `here`.  Note that it has to be
named in full every time: it is `define object <eight-eyed monster>` with
`alt <8-eyed monster>` (green_light.asl:372-373) and nothing shorter, and the
game is ASL 310, so a bare "monster" resolves to nothing at all.  Quest only
matches a word taken from the middle of a name for ASL 391 and up, and only
while abbreviations are on (Disambiguate, V4Game.cs:4762-4772; see
fixtures/abbrev.asl and fixtures/abbrevoff.asl).

It will not take food until you have talked it round, and the conversation is
four nested selections deep, one correct answer each:

  speak to eight-eyed monster -> monster   1  "Hello. How are you?"
                              -> monster2  2  "...you mustn't get much company."
                              -> monster3  1  "I feel sorry for ya."
                              -> monster4  3  "...bring some food from the kitchen?"

Only that last choice sets `#fmonster#`, which is what the monster's `give`
handler needs.  Every other branch is a rebuff, and speaking to it again after
`#fmonster#` is set just gets "Where's my food?" -- so the four numbers have to
be right the first time through.

Then `give sugared Figari bread to eight-eyed monster` (it asks for something to
wash it down with) and `give cup of milk to eight-eyed monster`, which hides the
monster and `create exit east <fountain room; hallway>`.  Hallway east is the
storeroom and card C.


ODDS AND ENDS

The game block carries a leftover debug cheat: `command <a> give <card C>`.
Typing a single "a" hands you the last card and skips the monster, the bread,
the milk, the cow and the key entirely -- that is to say, most of the game.
This script does not use it.

`define procedure <read-book>`, which opens a "which book would you like to read
in the library?" selection with "The Art Of Potions" in it, is never called from
anywhere.  So the three potion recipes it prints (a transformation potion needs
eight human eyes; an explosive potion needs a Figari clove) are unreachable, and
so is the potion bottle from the potions room, which nothing in the game uses.
The two `command <eat ... Figari bread>` handlers in the game block are the same
sort of tease: they answer "You feel it serves some sort of purpose..."

The library's north exit is `north msg <The door has been slam shut behind you.>`
-- once in, you stay in.  That is fine, because `speak to Mervil` ends the game:
the giant tells Jem he is one of Mervil's experiments, made with a formula
called Green Light, hands the formula over, panics, and the procedure calls
`playerwin`.  The win text advertises a sequel, Green Light: Gold Expansion.

The lab prints "You are in the the labratory" -- the room has `indescription
<You are in the:>` and `prefix <the>` and an `alias`, and all three land in the
same sentence.  Quest does the same; it is the author's doubling, not ours.

Every transition is padded with `pause` (up to 4500ms in the bedroom), which the
runner ignores.
