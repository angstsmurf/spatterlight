Ghost Light -- ASL 410, QDK Pro 4.1.1.  The file is called asdarknessfalls2.cas;
the game inside it calls itself Ghost Light, and nothing in it ever mentions
darkness falling, so the title used here is the one in the define game block.

You are an investigator of the supernatural, walking in through the gates of a
coastal manor house to find out what has been killing people there.  The gates
slam behind you and never open again -- 26 rooms, one win, four ways to die, all
four of them in the last five turns of the game.  It is a well built and
genuinely fair puzzle box: everything needed is findable, the two number codes
are both properly clued, and the endgame is spelled out in a manuscript you can
read beforehand.

IT MUST BE RUN WITH --tick.  Not for flavour: the only source of salt in the
game is boiling a pot of sea water dry over a campfire, and the boiling is done
by `define timer <boilaway>` with `interval <20>`.  Without a timer tick there
is no salt, and without salt the demon dodges the chandelier and kills you.
Three more timers matter -- `pentime` (20) is how long the laser pen stays
balanced on the tripod, `demontime` (20) is how long the demon lets you think,
and `pausetime` (70) is the reprieve the salt circle buys -- so the whole
endgame is on a clock as well.

THE TWO CODES.  Under the guillotine in the gate house is "Opening the safe is
as easy as A,B,C", and the three letters are scattered: A=7 is scratched on the
matchbox found among the gravestones, B=8 is scrawled on the wall by the
bricked-up front door, and the desk in the gate house is etched "C=AxB", which
makes C 56.  So the safe is 7856, and it holds the telescope.

The electronic lock on the door into the manor wants 4190, and that one is a
homophone riddle.  The door's warning reads "Beyond the door feeds Ageroth the
Demon.  Only the noble can pass beyond"; the gravestone the statue's spear
points at belongs to Sir Cedric De Margain, a nobleman; and scrawled on it in
blood is "FOR ONE I KNOW".  Read it aloud as digits -- FOR is four, ONE is one,
I is the ninth letter, and KNOW is no, which is nought.  4190.

Nothing in the game checks whether you have found any of that.  Both codes are
typed into `enter <code>` prompts and compared with `if is <#code#;7856>`, so a
player who knows the answers can walk straight past the whole telescope chain.
The script below does it the intended way anyway, because it is the only place
the second code exists in the game and because the chain is the best puzzle in
here: attach the telescope to the tripod in the front loft, look through it to
see which way the statue's spear points, take it off again, stick the putty to
the tripod, balance the laser pen on the putty, and then run -- rear loft, out of
the window, down the trellis, across the driveway and into the graveyard -- and
chalk the grave the red dot is sitting on before `pentime` drops the pen.  Name
it in full: "grave" also abbreviates the several gravestones standing beside it,
and Quest stops to ask which you meant.  Seven
moves and one CHALK, against a twenty-turn timer.

EXAMINE IS NOT LOOK AT.  The game block defines `verb <examine;x> msg <Examining
that reveals nothing more>` as a catch-all, so EXAMINE on any object that keeps
its secret in a `look` script -- the gravestones, the pile of rubbish, the
cooking pot -- answers with that brush-off, while LOOK AT the same object runs
the script and finds the thing.  Objects that define their own `action <examine>`
(the statue, the hedge, the shelves, the bin, the boxes, the seaweed, the altar,
the sign) work either way.  Sorting out which is which is most of what makes a
first playthrough slow, and the script uses whichever form the object actually
answers to.  The one that costs a game if you get it wrong is the altar: LOOK AT
ALTAR only describes it, and EXAMINE ALTAR is what turns up the chisel that frees
the spear.

THE KEY IN THE DRAIN.  The gate house is locked and its key is down the drain in
the lower driveway, out of arm's reach behind a grill.  It is not there when
you arrive: `drainkey` only shows it after the plastic brick found in the seaweed
has been dropped down the *upper* drain and a potful of sea water has been poured
after it to wash brick and key down to the lower one.  Then the aerial from the
loft boxes, with the sticky putty off the back of the DO NOT ENTER sign on its
end, fishes it out.  That one puzzle is why the first third of the route is a
long loop out to the cove and back, and it gates everything else: the gate house
holds the string, the telescope and the lever that opens the garage.

THE COOKING POT.  `action <climb>` on the trellis refuses outright while you are
carrying the cooking pot, and the trellis is the only way into the house.  Since
the pot is needed twice -- once to flush the drain, once to boil down the salt --
the route is built so that the second use, which ends with `use cooking pot on
A-frame` taking it out of your hands for good, happens before the last two
climbs.

THE CANDLES.  The machine in the workshop under the garage wants wax, wicks and
118 degrees.  Wicks are 20cm lengths of string -- the length comes from the
manual, and the guillotine will not cut the string until the manual has been read
-- soaked in the bottle of turps in the garage after a sachet of vinegar found in
the rubbish has been poured in.  118 comes off three dials: the first adds 110,
120 or 130, the second 0 or 5, the third 2 either way, so it is 120 + 0 - 2, and
`tempdial` resets the readout to zero on a wrong guess.  Then PUSH BUTTON.
`makecandle` branches on whether blood has been poured into the machine, so the
white candles must be made first: they are what goes into the candelabra on the
north balcony, which when lit shows the draught that reveals the secret button,
which opens the store where the blood pack is.  Only then can the blood candles
be made, and the wax, the wicks and the dials all have to be set up a second
time because the machine resets them.

Also note the blade.  Removing the guillotine blade with the screwdriver is what
cuts the chandelier rope at the end, but the blade has to come off *after* the
string is cut, since a bladeless guillotine cannot cut anything.

THE ENDGAME, in the order the manuscript in the secret store gives it: secure the
demon in a circle of salt, bind it in iron with blood candles surrounding it,
then pierce its heart with stone.  In practice everything has to be staged before
the summoning, because SUMMON DEMON starts `demontime` and locks the door back to
the loft:

  * lower the chandelier from the south balcony, go down to the hall, put the
    blood candles in its five pentagram slots and light them, then come back up
    and raise it again;
  * go down to the hall and SUMMON DEMON, which throws you back onto the east
    balcony as Ageroth crawls onto the altar;
  * MAKE SALT CIRCLE, which needs the jar of salt and the `onbalcony` flag -- set
    by entering the *west* balcony and cleared only by going back into the front
    loft, so it is still on -- and which swaps `demontime` for the longer
    `pausetime`;
  * cut the rope on the south balcony, dropping the raised chandelier onto a
    demon that can no longer dodge;
  * THROW SPEAR.

Each of those has a death waiting behind it.  Cut the rope with no salt circle
and the demon steps aside; cut it with no lit blood candles in the chandelier and
it bursts back out of the iron; go down the stairs into the hall at any point
after the summoning and the game asks whether you really mean it and kills you if
you say yes; and let `demontime` run out at any stage and Ageroth takes you
apart.  The spear must also not be thrown early -- `action <throw>` outside the
`almostdone` state just loses it.

No engine divergence came out of this game; it is here for the timer plumbing,
the two `enter <code>` prompts, the three `define selection` dials, and as the
suite's only test of a game whose win depends on a real-time timer expiring on
schedule.

Verified: 235 turns, --tick, `--win "YOU HAVE JUST KILLED THE DEMON"`, seed 1.
