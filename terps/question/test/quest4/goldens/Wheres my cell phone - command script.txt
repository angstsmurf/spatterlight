Where's my cell phone (where's my cell phone.asl, QDK Pro 4.1.1, asl-version 410)
================================================================================

A five-room scavenger hunt around one house: your phone rings, it is not in your
pocket, and you have to find it.  It is winnable, in eight moves if you know
them, and the win is OPEN PEN in the office -- the phone is defined as a child
of the pen, which is a "container" whose open script is `playerwin`.  Quest
prints the game's `define text <win>` section on playerwin (FinishGame ->
DisplayTextSection("win"), V4Game.Part2.cs:7424-7428), which is the
"Congratulations you found your cell phone and you win!" line after the author's
own stray "win" prefix in the open message.

This script is the full tour rather than the eight-move path, because almost
everything in the house is a joke, a red herring or a note from the author, and
the tour is what the game is actually made of.  Things worth knowing:

  * The two locked doors are both opened by pushing an *invisible* object, not by
    using a key.  The kitchen's north door is unlocked by PUSH BUTTON (the
    `button` object's `action <push>` does `unlock <kitchen; north>`) and the
    bedroom's east door by PUSH SQUARE.  Both objects are `invisible`, so they
    are never listed and never described -- you are told about them only in the
    descriptions of the two keys ("...unlocks the bedroom when you push the
    button on it").
  * USE BEDROOM KEY does *not* unlock anything, even though it says "you should
    now be able to unlock the bedroom door".  Its script is
    `unlock <kitchen; bedroom>` followed by two copies of `unlock <; >`: the
    second parameter of `unlock` is a direction, and "bedroom" is a room, so
    nothing matches.  The script keeps the line anyway, immediately followed by
    the NORTH it does not enable ("The exit is locked.") and then the PUSH BUTTON
    that does -- that pair is the point of including it.  The office key is the
    same kind of ornament; PUSH SQUARE is what opens the east door.
  * Several objects are `invisible` children of furniture and only appear in
    prose: the cell phone case (in the bed, mentioned by X BED), the drawer and
    the door in the dresser, the desk drawer, and the email on the computer.
    They can all be referred to regardless, which is how OPEN CELL PHONE CASE
    and OPEN DESK DRAWER work with the object never having been listed.
  * X and LOOK AT are different verbs here.  The author put the real content in
    `properties <examine = ...>` on the bed, the candy bar, the two keys and the
    jacket, while `look <...>` holds a plainer description -- so X BED finds the
    phone case and LOOK AT BED does not.  Likewise READ (notepad, record book,
    dream game book, email) and DRINK (pepsi) and SIT ON (toilet) are all
    properties, reached through the `verb <...>` lines in the game block.
  * Dead ends the script walks into deliberately: the apple is "stuck inside the
    fridge. Hee hee" and cannot be taken; the candy bar has a pin-hole in the
    wrapper and belongs in the waste can ("you put it into file number 13"); the
    cell phone case is empty; the jacket's pockets are empty; and the email
    offers to mail the author for hints.
