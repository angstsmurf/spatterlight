The Seat of Rulership
   Situated in the very centre of the Royal Palace, this is the Seat of
Rulership: this is where you give out the commandments which affect the lives
of countless millions, where delegates from foreign courts come to pay homage
to you (and, on more than one occasion, attempt to kill you), and where
enough money has been spent to refurbish the homes of every peasant in the
kingdom a dozen times over. It used to be referred to by the less prestigious
title of Throne Room, but High Chancellor Verenor felt that this ill
reflected your true status and so, after beheading the person to come up with
the term 'throne room', he had it renamed to its current title.
   The Seat of Rulership is a vast chamber: large enough to fit a small
village inside with ample room for expansion. Your throne - or Seat - is
located in the dead centre, claims High Chancellor Verenor, to allow the
adoring masses to see you as the centre of the universe. Suits of armour line
the outer wall, reputedly those worn by your loyal guardsmen who have
selflessly given their lives to save yours in the past. You can't help but
notice there are rather a lot of them.
   Passages leads away from the Seat of Rulership in the four cardinal
compass directions: north to the armoury; east to your personal chambers;
south to the palace archives; west to the palace gardens.

12.00 noon. You have a busy day ahead of you and, as it might well be your
last, you'd better see about setting your business in order. No doubt High
Chancellor Verenor will call you when your kingly duties are required so you
don't need to worry about that, but with the impending spectre (in the most
literal sense) of the Grim Reaper and midnight hanging over you, you wonder
if perhaps there is something you should be doing in the meantime. Can Death
be cheated? Can you be given a third chance? Maybe something in the archives
will inform you of this.

Naturally, time is of the essence today. You should avoid any unnecessary
delays and simply concentrate on preparing yourself for the midnight night as
best you can. And when you're ready - or if you think you're ready - 'wait
for midnight' will bring about the inevitable showdown between yourself and
the Grim Reaper.

The Palace Archives

   The palace archives: the entire knowledge of the kingdom dating back as
far as written history are contained here. The smell of old paper and
parchment... well, it's enough to make the nose curl to be honest. You tried
to get the place some proper windows years ago, but were informed by the High
Curator that that would possibly destroy some two thousand years of knowledge
and he even threatened to kill himself if you went ahead with it. So the
archives remain the same now as they were then: stuffy, somewhat smelly,
cramped... but still an interesting place to visit when you're in the mood
for some literature from a bygone age.
   Not just official palace records are kept here, but pretty much everything
the scribes have been able to lay their hands on: novels, manuscripts, old
scrolls on the summoning of demons, devils and whatnot. All attended by
short, balding scribes in patchy white robes who oversee the archives with
the kind of mindless devotion to duty that you have often seen from High
Chancellor Verenor where you are concerned. Someone attempting to torch this
place would have a thousand and one ink pots lobbed at his head the moment
the idea occurred to him.
   Where you're standing now offers an excellent view of a huge pile of
scrolls, stacked haphazardly on top of each other and swaying alarmingly from
side to side. There are paths through the piles to the southwest and
southeast while the Seat of Rulership lies back to the north.
Bartimony the Learned (chief scribe of the archives) is here.  Hrolf, Strug
and Bark enter from the north.

You start to move east towards your personal chambers, but something, some
little niggling thing in the back of your mind, tells you to stop. You're not
sure what it is... but it might have something to do with that heavy weight
hanging from the ceiling.

remarkably thin piece of wire. At a rough guess, you'd say it weighed about
10 tonnes and, if it fell on you, you'd hate to see what would be left of you
afterwards. 'Flat' just wouldn't describe it.
   You summon High Chancellor Verenor and inform him of the weight. After
promising to have the person who cleans the palace ceiling executed, he
arranges to have some workmen come round later in the day and remove it.
   "But for now, Your Highness," he advises, "it might be an idea to tread
carefully. Who knows what other manner of traps might be waiting for you?"
   On that unsettling note, he departs and leaves you, your guards and a huge
weight hanging precariously above you.

Preface

There recently came a time of great sadness in my life. My father, the noted
historian and playwright, Sir Arthur Montforth-Adams IV, after a long and
protracted period of illness which put great strain upon the entire family,
was still not dead. Many were the times we would gather around his deathbed,
where he lay, grinning maliciously at us as to his remarkable constitution,
waiting for the old codger to damn well hurry up and die, but he, as always,
had the last laugh and, indeed, was laughing at us as we sat around hoping
for a sizeable portion of his estate in the form of his will. He was an old
man and yet, as we all knew, Death just didn't seem interested in him.

I began to ponder this at length. Why is it that some people manage to defy
Death with such success and others, like my dear drug-sniffing
alcohol-swilling friend Janos, succumb to his icy embrace while doing nothing
more dangerous than taking a midnight swim in Suredeath River? I set out to
discover if there was a reason for this, and, in doing so, find out if there
was a totally legal and above board way of speeding along the demise of one
old codger who really should have cleared off this mortal coil a long, long
time ago...

How To Spot When Death Is After You

I conducted a great deal of research to discover the signs that the Grim
Reaper is after you. They are many and varied, and some are ridiculous while
others are deadly serious. I have made a list of the common ones as follows:

l        A small black cat begins to follow you around.
l        Flowers and weeds wither and die in your presence.
l        You have a mysterious feeling of being followed yet find no one
behind you when you turn around.
l        Milk sours when you try to drink it.
l        An unpleasantly bony fellow with a scythe and red, glowing eyes
follows you around and makes frightening comments on how you are 'soon to
die'.
l        An unpleasant smell emanates from you which reminds everyone of a
dead relative left to fester for several weeks.
l        Children see you and burst into tears.
l        Religious folk make the sign of the Devil at you.
l        You have a sudden desire to eat cheese which is ever so slightly
moldy.

And many, many more...

101 Tricks To Employ To Avoid Death

While I discovered no definitive way to permanently avoid Death, I did find
several ways of delaying the inevitable. My father, bless the old swine,
seems to have used them all. They are not, I should hasten to add, 100%
effective (except where ageing playwrights are concerned of course) but they
seem to delay Death's approach by a certain amount. Employed correctly, they
could give the unfortunate as much as an extra year of life.

l        Become unpredictable. Death has a lot of work to do and can't keep
tabs on everyone. The more you stick to your normal daily routine, the easier
it is for him to keep track of of you.
l        Carry around as many religious items as you can find. Death doesn't
like them and while they won't keep him away forever, they'll certainly make
him think twice.
l        Cease washing. Seriously. Death lacks a nose but he can smell all
the same. Bad smells seem to disagree with him.
l        Make sure you avoid anyone dying around you. This attracts Death and
if he's already got his eye on you, you don't need more reasons for him to
target you.
l        If possible, have your home refurbished. Knock walls down, move
corridors, install new doors. Death can walk through the walls if he has to,
but every little thing that slows him down can work to your benefit.
l        Be friendly to priests and other religious fellows. Their very
presence is often enough to make Death decide to leave you for another day.
l        Practice hiding. Death can't kill what he can't find. (Strictly
speaking, he can. But the harder it is for him to find you, the harder it is
for him to kill you.)

Armoury

   Weapons: that's what this place is about. Big weapons, small weapons, fat
weapons, thin weapons, weapons that kill from a distance and weapons that
kill up close. Lots and lots and lots of weapons. Your country, being forever
at war, is in constant need of huge amounts of weapons and this is where they
are stored.
   The armoury is a huge room: ten times the size of your personal chambers,
with a ceiling so far above your head that you swear you've seen clouds up
there some days. The weapons which give the armoury its purposes are stacked
on every surface you can see: tables, chairs, benches, shelves, hanging on
the walls. A single clerk oversees the entire place with the kind of
permanently harassed look which would indicate he really wishes he was the
clerk of somewhere else instead.
   To the west is the Palace Meeting Room, guarded by several rugged
sentinels, and to the south is the Seat of Rulership.  Hrolf, Strug and Bark
enter from the south.

Meeting Room

   Sporting the largest table known to man (or this man anyway), this is the
Palace Meeting Room: a huge room whose far reaches are always hidden in murky
shadows and whose roof is the subject of much debate as to whether it
actually exists or not. The table itself is situated in the centre of the
room and has enough space at it to seat almost five hundred people. Meetings
here tend to be very long-winded affairs, mainly on account of the distance
from one side of the table to the other being so great that everything has to
be conveyed by a series of runners who seldom remember more than ten words at
a time. Portraits of your former ancestors - an ugly bunch and no mistake -
line the walls and stare down at you with thinly-disguised contempt.
   High Chancellor Verenor, a squadron of elite palace guards, several army
captains and the High Warlord are present.
   The exit is to the east and leads you back to the armoury.

"Greetings, Your Highness," says High Chancellor Verenor. "I have gathered
the High Warlord and the army captains here today so that we can discuss how
best to combat the menace of the barbarian hordes of Vancour-"
   "Er, begging your pardon, sir," says one of the army captains who, by
interrupting High Chancellor Verenor, clearly has far more bravery than
common sense, "but we beat them the other week."
   "Then who are we discussing how best to defeat?"
   "The goblins of Yaun?" someone suggests.
   "No, we signed a peace treaty with them," someone else says.
