Introduction...

You are Stavros 'The Bulldog' McGrogan, the hardest man the SAS ever
produced. While it's perhaps untrue to say that bullets bounce from your
skull, daggers bend when stabbed into your ribs and bombs just plain run away
from you, it is true to say that you once disarmed a terrorist by tearing his
arm off with your teeth  and then cracking his head (through his shock
resistant armour) with your nose. You're mean, nasty, vicious and downright
tough. They call you in when someone needs dealing with quickly and
painfully. When terrorists threaten the safety of the world, they send out a
message for The Bulldog. And may God forgive any terrorist who doesn't get
out of your way…

[MORE]

So it's perhaps understandable that when you get the call to visit a small
cottage in the charming part of the English countryside known as Pleasant
Woods, you're a little surprised. When you see it's an actual cottage,
surrounded on all sides by other cottages, and not a disguised terrorist
base, you're even more surprised. But that doesn't prepare you for your
briefing with Sergeant Twiddles…

”Yes,” says the Sergeant, fiddling with his moustache as he strokes his small
black and white cat. “We're in a spot of bother and no mistake.”

[MORE]

You grunt. (You are a man of few words after all.)

”It's old Mrs Moog, you see,” Twiddles goes on. His cat emits a purr of
satisfaction. “She's only gone and fainted due to the sudden heat wave we're
experiencing. Frightful business.”

You grunt. (The grunt says: “and you called me here to tell me some old dear
has passed out?”)

”Mrs Moog,” Twiddles goes on, “is head of our Nuclear Research Facility.”

You nod. You remember the name now. 112 years old, can barely walk, half
blind, three quarters deaf, likes coffee creams and cats, mad as a bat… and
yet a dab hand at constructing nuclear devices capable of tearing the world
apart a dozen times over.

[MORE]

”She was working on a crucial design for a new bomb,” Sergeant Twiddles says.
He frowns. “And then when she didn't show up at work today, we decided to go
ahead without her. Only that's when we hit a slight problem.”

You frown. (This is your other mode of expression. It works as well as a
grunt but is more visual than verbal.)

”You see, nuclear bombs are complicated things,” explains Twiddles. “Mrs Moog
is great at them. Her staff… well, they're not quite so great. Or 'useless'
might be a better term to apply to them.”

Grunt.

[MORE]

”Yes. I agree. Anyway… her staff went on without her and started a… what's
the word?” Twiddles fumbles in his pocket and pulls out a piece of paper. “Ah
yes. A 'nuclear meltdown'. Apparently if the nuclear reactor isn't shut down
in the next six hours, it'll explode and blow the world to pieces. Or split
it in half. Or turn it into one great big lump of burning rock floating
through space. Whatever. It'll be a trifle unpleasant around here.”

Frown.

”Yes, you've got it in one,” says Twiddles. “We need you to get into Mrs
Moog's house and get her out of there in one piece. Only there's a slight
problem with that.”

You figured there might be. After all, they don't call The Bulldog in unless
there's a problem.

[MORE]

”Mrs Moog is paranoid about security,” says Twiddles, “and has rigged her
house up with the most fiendish collection of anti-burglary and anti-theft
devices known to man. I've sent in four men already and…” He nods to a bucket
off to one side of him, in which can be seen what looks suspiciously like an
arm. “Well, it wasn't very nice. So we need you, Mr Bulldog, to head in there
and- ugh!”

You lean forward and say, “it's The Bulldog. Not Mr Bulldog.” Then you smile
coldly and release his throat.

Time, you decide, you got to work.

Outside Mrs Moog's Cottage
A gnome standing by the side of the quaint path which leads to the front door
(currently closed) of Mrs Moog's cottage is giving you the evil eye. The
cottage itself - small in appearance, but probably larger inside than you'd
otherwise guess - looks cosy in the kind of way only a cottage in the heart
of the English countryside can look. There are two floors, but only a single
window is visible. From the bars across it, you doubt getting in there is
going to be possible. A fence encircles the property.

A metal bar lies on the ground.

>You smash the gnome to pieces, scattering it into a thousand and one pieces
across the ground… and revealing something that was previously hidden inside
it. Brushing aside shards of shattered gnome, you pick up… a bug.
   Not the kind of bug you're likely to find in the English countryside but
some kind of high-tech surveillance device.

>The fence is made from carved pieces of wood, each of them about two feet
long and sharpened to a fine point. One of them looks a little out of place.

>You tug out a piece of the fence and now have a mighty fine spike.

>The window is small and pokey. Even if you climbed up the side of the
cottage, you doubt you'd have much chance of getting in there. Not to mention
the fact that there are bars across it and-
   Hang on. What's that blinking red light you see behind the window?

>You're guessing it's some kind of camera but it's difficult to tell for
certain.

>You take aim and throw. The spike hits the bars and bounces back, falling
onto the ground at your feet.

>You pick up the spike.

>You throw the spike again. This time, it actually clatters against the camera
but as the blinking red light continues to blink at you, you're guessing you
haven't caused it any actual damage.
   The spike drops at your feet.

>You pick up the spike.

>You throw the spike-
   And there comes the sound of something shattering in the room beyond the
window.
   The spike lands at your feet a moment later.

>You pick up the spike.

>The door stands closed before you. Very closed. If doors could be said to be
things that had various degrees of closedness, then this door would be
crowned king of closedness stuff.
   It's otherwise perfectly unremarkable aside from a letterbox halfway down.

>You peer through the letterbox and see-
   A bomb.
   Or, more precisely, a wire leading to a bomb that Mrs Moog has rigged up
just inside the front door in order to surprise unwary burglars.

>You jab the spike through the letterbox and nudge the wire.
   Something beyond the door makes a high-pitched whining sound, then a
thunderous explosion is triggered inside the cottage.
   But you? Aside from a slight burn on your fingers, you're otherwise okay.

>You open the door, revealing an unremarkable hallway beyond it.

>You pick up the metal bar.

Hallway
A short hallway lies before you, the walls decorated with portraits of the
lady of the house. The carpet underfoot has an unpleasant feel to it, as
though it conceals something… unnatural. At the northern end, the corridor
splits into two branches, one heading west, the other east while from the
ceiling above you hangs an ornate and elaborate chandelier. At your back (to
the south) is the front door via which you entered the cottage.

There is a basket here.

>You pick up the basket.

>The basket… something about it strikes you as wrong. It's made of metal for a
start. A metal picnic basket? And since when does Mrs Moog go on picnics? She
designs bombs and often…
   And suddenly you remember an article you read about her a while back: she
often brings her work home. And when you work with bombs all day, you need
something suitably bomb-proof to transport them home on the bus, taxi or back
of your pushbike.
   You check the basket and notice, sure enough, the blast marks that scar
its inside. There's even a clasp on the side for the basket to be attached to
something, no doubt to keep it steady while it's being transported somewhere.

>There are four portraits, which you like to think of as the first portrait,
second portrait, third portrait and fourth portrait. Helps to distinguish
them that way.

>A charming shot of Mrs Moog stroking a cat as she attaches a guided missile
to its back. The cat doesn't look unduly bothered by it; probably been
promised a bowl of milk afterwards if it behaves.
   There are a series of strange marks on the ground beside the cat.

>The marks look like letters: B/S/L/B. Hmm… you wonder what they mean.

>This isn't a portrait as such, but instead a lever, a button and a switch.
The lever appears to be Mrs Moog's nose, the button one of her eyes (you
guess she ran out of buttons or just fancied a portrait of her minus an eye)
and the switch her chin. You can't help but feel that another switch for her
mouth would be a good idea.

>You push the button.
   Somewhere deep in the ground there is a creaking sound.

>You flick the switch.
   You hear another creaking sound from down below.

>You pull the lever.
   A third consecutive creaking sound is heard.

>You push the button.
   Nothing happens. Until you hear a creaking sound from somewhere down below you and another
creaking sound from the room off to the west.

Mrs Moog's Study
A desk of vast proportions occupies just about the entirety of this otherwise
smallish room, its edges actually bending the wall in places and the floor
fairly groaning beneath it. A small window in one wall, barred, displays a
desert scene beyond the cottage. Lurking in one corner is a single bookcase,
alongside a cabinet and a wardrobe (a wardrobe in a study?) A panel in the
far wall has opened, revealing a small cavity.
   A doorway, minus a door, leads back to the hallway to the east.

>It reveals a winter scene and…
   A winter scene? Yet wasn't it sunny when you were outside just a few
minutes before?
   You look again. A winter scene alright, complete with a snowman,
snowflakes falling and even some heroic figure skiing down a slope, wiggling
from side to side as he makes his way down…
   And that's another strange thing, you realise. You can actually see the
figure moving, and the snow falling, and the snowman is, of all things,
smoking a pipe. Little trails of smoke float away from the pipe.

>The way they curl through the air strikes you as strange, almost as if
they're being directed and…
   And it almost looks like there might be words written there in the smoke.

>The words in the smoke twists this way and back but they look like: "noroc".
   You wonder what they mean.

>It's a strange bookcase in that while it could hold perhaps a couple hundred
books, or maybe even half a thousand really thin books, it only holds a
single, slim volume.

>You take the book from the bookcase.

>Now you've removed the only book that was on the bookcase, all that remains

in often enough, and her own house keeping skills leave a lot to be desired.

>You wipe at it, but the dust seems pretty ingrained into the surface of the
bookcase and you just succeed in smearing it into… a very peculiar shape.

>The dust seems to have been smeared into a word. Or, rather, there was a word

something Mrs Moog is working on in her nuclear research role?

>A-ha! There's a magnifying glass lying on the floor beneath the cabinet. You
guess Mrs Moog must have dropped it there and never remembered to pick it
back up again.
   You ferret it out and wipe the dust off it. A bit smudged but still ought
to magnify okay.

>You peer through the magnifying glass and make out the words from the first
page:"Well done, you're a burglar with a brain. Congratulations! I do so

   Bet you're wondering how to get into the room opposite this one, eh?
Without the chandelier dropping on your head and killing you outright?
Well..."
   That's all that's written on the page.

>You turn to the next page in the book and read: "Try using the wardrobe door
as a shield."

>You turn the page and read: "But try to disarm the bomb first. Throwing
something bookish against the wardrobe door ought to do the trick."

>You lob the book at the wardrobe and there is a tremendous explosion as the
wardrobe explodes!
   (Well, strictly speaking it's the bomb inside the wardrobe that explodes

technically correct.)
   You wave your hands to dismiss the smoke, and finally get to see the
