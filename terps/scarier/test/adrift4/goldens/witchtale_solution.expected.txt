So, ready then?

Originally I wanted to become a painter. Something about the etching of
lines, the blending of shades, the outpouring of soul... In actuality, I
can't paint a thing. I won't even try. With my luck I'd drown in the paints
or choke on the brush. Since I can remember, I've been doomed with my
klutziness and general ability to create wild turmoil out of the most
peaceful situations unintentionally. Everyone tells me it's because of my
purple eyes.

Things didn't quite turn out on the path of the artist, so after a long and
arduous debate with myself I concluded that I was destined to take any job
that came my way. So - I became a waitress.

Surely I looked ridiculous as I wobbled under the large stack of plates and
silverware that I had balanced carefully on my head; with my hair falling
down past my eyes and my hands unable to leave the stacks of china without
sending it all down in a tumble. I blew a breath and my hair parted to create
a little window so that I could see again.

That was when I tripped. My heart jumped and I almost managed to catch myself
until my hair dropped back over my face and I teetered off to one side,
sending the enormous stack crashing down in a symphony of rattles and bangs.
I fell against the wall, which caused a shelf of glasses and cups to smash on
the floor and set off a rather interesting chain reaction of mass destruction
that I thought was utterly totally completely impossible, leaving the entire
pub in the nastiest state imaginable.

I brushed the hair out of my face, surveyed the damage, and winced.

more >

Needless to say, there was trouble. The rosy-cheeked and hen-shaped owner,
Lenna, had shed her fictional feathers and transformed into a rhinocerous
with her rage. A great shouting ensued after which it was decided that my pay
would be docked, I'd have to stay overnight to clean it all up, and I would
still rather be a painter. Characteristic of my relentless streak of  bad
luck, and probably caused by my cursed corneas.

By the time the ruckus had subsided and I was left alone, a storm outside had
kicked into full gear. I was in the pub with a crushing load of cleaning to
do and in low spirits. On this night something peculiar will happen to me, as
you will find out. It's a wonderful thing to happen, too, and really it
changes everything! But anyway... where was I? Oh, yes -

Lenna's Pub
I'm in the pub in the wake of my latest mistake. Broken dishes are scattered
across the floor. Shelves have unhinged from the walls and lay haphazardly
among the tables, which along with the chairs are also upturned and spun
around every which way. The floor is wet, too - soaking actually. I don't
remember exactly why... something to do with a water leak in the kitchen, I
think. The worst of it though is the silverware. Somehow silverware is
everywhere, even in places I won't dare to look. I didn't think that much
silverware could ever be contained in a kitchen, but apparently I'm wrong.

> clean floor
Between the patting drops of rain I hear a distinct knocking at the front
door. How unusual. I wait hesitantly, and there it is again – a distinct rap.
I go to the door, hear the knock again, and promptly open it.

At first I couldn't tell exactly what was standing in front of me. It looked
like a lump in the dark, slightly shorter than myself. As my eyes adjust I
see it's a short, stocky figure wearing some sort of cloak.

“Hello, child,” the figure rasps in a croaky female voice that sounds like an
old frog. “May I step in for a moment and dry off?”

The figure raises her head and I see that it is indeed a woman, and an old
one at that. A large, stocky nose protudes from a weather-worn face thats
wrinkled like a prune. Her eyes seem small and I can't see her other features
through the dark of the night and curtain of thick raindrops.

I thought about it and decided I had nothing to lose: “Sure.”

more >

She comes inside and removes her cloak. I can see her face now, her large
nose protruding from weathered skin is her greatest distinction, but its her
eyes that catch my attention – they're purple. Like mine.

“I apologize, dearie,” the old woman says, “But that rain is falling hard for
a old hag like me.” She looks at me for a moment and continues, “Ah, the
hexenmark is strong in you, but let me not get ahead of myself. My name is
Ragoria Hobben. May I sit and rest my legs a moment, dearie?”

She sits on one of the tables while I introduce myself and explain to her my
hopeless situation. I stop mid sentence and, confused, ask her about the
hexenmark.

"Ha ha!" Ragoria bursts, "If you had known, I tell you what, then you would
not be working at this boring old place!" Her laughter subsides, and I'm
still baffled. "Well, someone has to tell you, dearie, and it may as well be
me. I'm not crazy, and you are a witch."

more >

"It's that deep night in your eyes, the purple - It's witch's blood inside
you. And I know, dearie, because I am one myself," Ragoria finishes proudly.
I am still dumbfounded as she reaches into one of the many folds of her
poncho and withdraws (of all things) a tiny lizard.

"A skitterling," she explains, and quickly tosses it at me. I swing my arms
wildly in clumsy attempt to dodge the whizzing thing and it hits the broom,
bursting like a firecracker into a cloud of smoke. The broom, highly
motivated by this act, began to work on sweeping the floor as if possessed.

Just like that the situation went from downright dreary to literally magical.
In a few moments, the kitchen tools were working on their own accord while
Ragoria and I established a meeting the following day. I was going to become
a witch!

> ask ragoria about magic
"Magic has been around since the world itself. It is a part of the world, in
fact, tied to it just like the trees and mountains and sky. Much is known
about magic now, but it was not always so. It took generations for it to
emerge, and the first ones who figured out its existence became known as the
magi.

"The magi understood that the forces of nature could be controlled, summoned
and bent to their will. It took many years of practice, but the magi began to
use their knowledge to summon rain, speed the growth of crops, and heal the
sick. It continued this way for many generations, with the older magi
becoming more adept with their power.

At first, it was not known exactly why certain people could use magic while
others could not. Eventually it became understood that there was something
inside the body, something that was used up after casting spells. This,
dearie, is mana - some of us have it, and some of us don't. Those who don't
couldn't do magic no matter how hard they tried. Now this mana runs through
you like blood, and it is this mana that makes you a magi."

more >

"It was always a mystery in the olden days why magi would die earlier and age
faster than the common folk, at least until the discovery of mana. The more
magic you use, the more of your mana stock is drained. This mana is a part of
you just like your heart and mind, so as the mana is drained it will take a
toll on your body. Basically, in those days magic was a double edged sword -
it's powers could cause wonderful things, but the price was a strain on life
itself. And then, dearie, the magi discovered that mana was in everything -
just like magic! Mana could be harvested and used, just like drinking or
eating. In fact, the liquid in this cauldron here is pure mana."

"The magi," Ragoria continued, "Could now recharge themselves, and so the
magi lived more healthily and continued to explore their powers. One day, a
magi was born with deep purple eyes. Her name was Deneb, and she became the
most powerful magi of all. Deneb studied magic furiously, and stumbled on a
new discovery - instead of spending years learning a handful of spells, she
was able to craft magic into objects and potions and such. That was the
beginning of witchcraft - the art of crafting magic into a physical, tangible
form. Deneb was the witchmother, the ancestor of all witches today. That is
why we have these lovely purple peepers, my dear.
