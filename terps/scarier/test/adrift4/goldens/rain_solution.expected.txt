Bringing the Rain
It hasn't rained in almost three years.

Times are desperate, people are getting hungrier and hungrier, and in the
village proper there's been a great deal of muttering against the mayor.

Rain used to come from the cloud-wreathed Feather Mountain, to the east, but
something has changed. Something has gone terribly wrong. People fear that
they've angered God, or at least angered the storm spirits. All the villages
and surrounding farms receive now are dry lightning storms and dust and hot,
terrible winds that destroy what little remains in the parched fields.

You are still not technically old enough to be considered a man, but you've
decided to leave home to take some of the burden off your parents, who have
your younger siblings to think of. Furthermore, you've had enough of this
drought; you're going to the top of Feather Mountain to find out the cause.

Village Square
Fitful winds swirl around your feet and all about the cobblestoned square in
the center of town, coating every available surface with dust from last
night's storm. A scrawny chicken pecks half-heartedly at the ground next to
the town's main well, long dry; people wanting water have had to travel miles
to what's left of the river for nearly half a year now.

The road to the mountains now turns and leads east out of town, past the
dejected looking houses, all withered boards and cracked and peeling paint.
The only home that has escaped this fate sits to the south and belongs to
Melda, the self-proclaimed witch who insists she can end the drought if only
the mayor would give in to her demands.

Road to Mountains
Before the village was even out of sight to the west, your clothes and boots
were filled with the ever-present dust. Before the last of the parched, empty
fields were behind you, your lips were dry and cracked, and the occasional
small sip you allowed yourself from your bottle of precious water did little
to assuage your thirst.

But you pressed on and now, here you are, almost at the very foot of the
towering Feather Mountain itself, the narrow, ancient path that leads to its
peak clearly visible as it starts its ascent to the east. Though you can't
see the top from here, you've been watching it somewhat apprehensively as you
approached--from the rumbles of thunder and occasional flash of lightning,
another terrible dry storm is brewing, and you'll be walking right into it.

> e
You're continuing up the path when the thickening clouds ahead, no longer
content with subdued flashes and rumblings, split the sky with a terrific
bolt of lightning, followed a fraction of a second later by a deafening CRACK
that rattles you to your core. Clapping your hands over your ears, you almost
drop your beautiful feather--taking a more secure hold on it, you notice with
some alarm that it has begun to shimmer softly and quiver in a way that can't
be explained by the wind.

Absorbed by your contemplation of the apparently magical feather, you don't
at first realize that the rumbling you hear is not all from the gathering
storm. Something makes you look up, however, just as a portion of the
mountain further ahead suddenly seems to just collapse, shattering and
sending up a cloud of choking dust that rolls down the path to engulf you,
though thankfully the rubble itself doesn't reach so far. When the air clears
enough so that you can stop gasping and coughing, you stare around in dismay.

Road to Mountains
You are at the very foot of the towering Feather Mountain, the narrow,
ancient path that leads to its peak now clogged with a pile of heavy stones
that block all access to the east. Above, dark clouds are gathered, letting

The feather in your hand seems to flicker and pulse with power in time with
the storm. If there was ever any doubt in your mind that the thing was
magical, its long gone now...yet the way the rising winds tug at it so
eagerly makes you nervous, and you have a sudden urge to grip the feather
tightly to keep it from flying away.

> grab the feather
Closing your hand around the end of the magic feather you gasp and then laugh
out loud as a quick, light burst of energy goes through you. The sensation is
cool and wonderful and somehow liquid; all your weariness vanishes in an
instant.

A gust of wind tugs at the feather, the feather tugs at you, and then you are
leaping, tumbling...no, flying through the air, up and around in a dizzying
spiral, dropped with the dying of one wild gust and then caught and spun
around by another, until near the end of one spectacular swoop, low to the
ground, you accidentally lose your grip, and the feather continues flying on
its own, sending you crashing to the ground with an "Oof!"

Mountain Path
Heart still pounding from your wild flight, you look around breathlessly; you
are safely on solid ground now, though the wind howls just as loudly in your
ears. Whether through sheer luck or because it somehow sensed where you
wanted to go, the magical feather has deposited you on the other side of the
blocked path, well away from the piles of rocks and rubble left by the
avalanche. From here the path takes a steep turn upwards, shortly before
winding out of sight.

Top of Mountain
Fighting the increasingly strong winds all the way up, knowing that any
misstep could lead to a plunge to your doom, you finally reach the place
where the steep path levels out, at the very top of the mountain, and behold
your destination and the place where you hope to find relief for your
village. Tall stone pillars that look as ancient as the mountain itself form
a sort of long, roofless hall directly to the east. At the very end of it
shines a bright light, and somewhere there is a low, deep rumble you can
almost feel in your bones.

> e
Shielding your eyes to keep from being blinded, you stand as close as you
dare to the source of the intensely bright light--a long, low form resting on
a stone dais. To its right is another dais, this one empty, and further still
is a third. As your gaze falls on this last one you startle and feel your
heart skip a beat, your eyes only just now adjusting enough to notice the
large, dark creature with woolly fur and long, grey feathers that silently
regards you--a gryphon!

"You've finally arrived, I see." You peek out from behind your hands at the
strong, clear voice, watching two broad streaks of light, like sails suddenly
bloom out from the back of the bright one--wings, you realize, in the brief
moment before they settle back against him. Even that was to much for you,
however, and you squeeze your eyes shut again, the image seemingly burned
even on the backs of your eyelids.

The voice continues. "Very few humans have the dedication to make it this
far, which is good, because there are even fewer that we wish to see.
But...we can tell that you at least intend no evil, so you will not be
harmed, so long as you do not linger."

He pauses then, seeming to notice your discomfort. "Here, let me veil myself
so that you may see."

Opening your eyes just the tiniest crack, you realize gratefully that the
light is no longer so painful, and gradually you can actually make out the
shape of a gryphon, though it's still more like you're looking at molten gold
in a smithy than a living creature with fur and feathers.

"I am Lightning," the gryphon continues, his voice now somewhat muffled, as
though it came from a distance. "And the other here is my brother Thunder.
You have come to ask us a question? Speak, then."

Hall of the Storm Lords
Lightning rests here on his stone dais, still bright enough to make you
squint. To the right of him is another empty dais, and further still is a
third. On this last one is the dark, woolly gryphon that must be Thunder,
silently regarding you. The pillars form a circle around the gryphons, and a
line of them leads westward back the way you came.

> ask lightning about drought
Awed and frightened as you are, you remember the importance of your mission,
and somehow manage to stammer out a question about the drought.

Lightning is silent a long moment. "Our sister Rain, she has been taken from
us," he finally says with a sigh. "A mortal strong in the dark arts holds her
captive, that much we sense, but we don't know who or where. And even if we
did, there are...certain rules, which would keep us from interfering too
much."

"The lands below yearn for Rain's return almost as much as we do, we know
this, but without her we can do nothing to help, even if we wished it...as
heavy as our hearts are, even all our tears will not be enough to bring life
to your crops. So I'm sorry, but I'm afraid you've wasted your time in coming
here."

"But here...you faced a difficult journey in reaching us, so let me at least
make your return a little easier." As the gryphon finishes speaking, he
raises one wing and twists his head around to pluck out a long feather, the
movement of his bright beak leaving an afterimage hovering in the air.
Reaching out, you gingerly take the proffered gift, and it shines brightly in
your hand.

"There is enough power in that feather to grant you the gift of flight, for a
short time. If you come to an obstacle, just grip it tightly and think of
where you would like to go. Be careful though, flight can apparently be a
bit...overwhelming for those who have never experience it."

Deciding not to mention that you already know this first hand, you thank
Lightning with as much gratitude as you can muster, your mind elsewhere as
you contemplate what you've learned about his sister Rain.

The gryphon straightens to stand on all fours and shakes herself joyfully,

flashing within them. Only instead of another dry storm whipping up the

You waste no time in clambering out of the cellar and running out into the
street, knowing you need have no fear of the lightning. All around you in the
village, lamps are going on, and people are rushing outside in their
nightclothes to join you, talking excitedly, hugging each other and laughing
with joy...

...all except one person. Melda rushes out of her ruined house, her face
twisted in fury as she screams obscenities at the sky, swearing revenge on
whoever freed Rain after 'her three years of hard work, all for nothing'.

Everyone has stopped now, staring, and in that moment there is a blinding
streak of brightness from the heavens, almost too quick to follow (indeed,
most witnesses said afterwards they only had the briefest impression of
wings) and the witch's body is left smouldering on the ground, a deep wound
as though from a fiery spear--or beak--later to be found in her heart, as
Lightning bolts away to Thunder's earsplitting roar of triumph.
