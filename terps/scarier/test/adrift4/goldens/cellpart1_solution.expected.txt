Cell C

Cell C

A harsh acid-blue glare burns down from a pair of high-intensity fluorescent
bars sunk into a pill-shaped recess in the poured-concrete ceiling.
Thankfully, the ceiling has not been painted the same dog-piss-yellow color
as the walls of this perfectly cubical room, three meters on a side. This
crumbling institutional paint is flaking off in great, filthy scales from the
walls, pushed up by a bruise-colored mold that is spreading in long, rootlike
fingers from the corners. Some wit has carpeted this tiny room with
Wal-Mart-brand plastic astroturf the green color of comic book toxic waste so
new and so bright that it could be ugly in the dark and it glares cheerily
back up at the ceiling. A slab of sunset-orange industrial packing foam,
thick and narrow like a giant French-fry, sits and quietly rots in the
corner, intended to serve as a makeshift bed. Directly opposite the pallet is
a blue plastic painter's bucket apparently meant to act as a toilet, judging
by the streaky sort of dun-and-brown stains on its sides. The north wall is
inset with a short, wide door which is, in turn, inset with a palm-sized
window of green glass.

It is very, very cold.  The room stinks of sulfur and smoke. This new smell
only partly overlays the older, stronger reek of vinegary piss, though.

The cell door has been bowed in and knocked askew by some sort of impact.

Cell Block 2

The unpainted poured-concrete ceiling of this short, broad east-west hallway
is very low and is studded down the center with shallow oval recesses
containing pairs of high-intensity fluorescent light bars. The actinic glare
from these lights makes the scabby avocado paint on the walls look
particularly wet and puky. The lights are dully reflected by the cracked
linoleum tile on the floor. Four impenetrably strong steel doors open onto
the hallway, two on the north and south walls respectively. A much less
sturdy swinging door stands at the east end of the hall.

The floor is littered with chips of concrete and stinks of sulfur and
cordite.   Also here is a beautiful young woman.

Cell Block Common Room

Cell Block Common Room

The common room of the cell block is a square block ten meters on a side with
a three-meter ceiling of unpainted poured concrete. A thick layer of
institutional green paint approximating the shade of avocados thoroughly
masticated by rabid goats is liberally daubed in centimeter thick layers on
the walls to contrast the badly cracked linoleum tile. A line of recessed and
chicken-wire guarded lights runs across the center of the ceiling to connect
two doors opposite one another on the east and west walls, throwing the room
into gloomy shadows and a gate of chain-link fence guards a stairway in the
north wall leading upwards. In the center of the room is a brushed-steel
autopsy table overhung with a set of powerful halogen lights. A line of tall
steel lockers stands against the south wall.

Near the table, a dead man in a heavy black canvas jumpsuit and workboots is
lying facedown on the floor in a slowly spreading pool of blood. The new
smells of cordite and blood overlay an ingrained stench of disinfectant in
this desperately cold room.
Genevive is here.

Isolation Chamber

Isolation Chamber 3

The isolation chamber of Mercy Memorial Hospital is a perfect cube of
blinding white plastic five meters on a side. The floor is gently sloped down
to the center and fitted with a wide, mesh-guarded drain in the center to
facilitate hosing down both the interior and the occupant. The ceiling is
guarded by a sheet of clear lexan through which a cluster of intense halogens
burn straight down into the chamber, flooding the little room with achingly
bright light. The north wall is fitted with an oval airlock door, featureless
and smooth on the inside. A camera on an articulated arm peers down through
the lexan barrier above.

It is pleasantly warm in the chamber, though it smells strongly of rubbing
alcohol and powdered aluminum.
