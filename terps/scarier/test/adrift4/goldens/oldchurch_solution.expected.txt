The Old Church

It's a hot summer's day and you are out driving. Along the road you see an
old church. The church-yard looks peaceful with old trees giving shadows and
coolness. You stop to take a break.

Churchyard
You stand in front of an old stone church. The front door is open. Old
gravestones are scattered in the grass. Under an oak in the corner of the
churchyard there is a stone bench. It seems perfect for a picnic. You keep
your provisions in a white plastic bag.

> in
You move in.

Church Porch
The porch is a cool room with stone floor and one small window. Here you meet
a man repairing something in a corner. It's the sexton. To the north there is
a toilet. You can go up to the tower, east to the nave and out to the
churchyard.

> u
You move up.

Tower
You stand in a low room between the porch and the bells. The only light comes
from a small window. Unfashionable or damaged furniture and movables are
stored here. A door to the east leads to the gallery and the stairs down to
the porch.

> e
You move east.

Gallery
The gallery is a balcony in the west part of the nave. From here you have a
good view over the church interior. The gallery is dominated by a beautiful
old organ for church music.

> examine organ
You read a sign telling: "Gift from Axel Gyllenpil 1684". Then you hear the
sound of someone playing the organ. You didn't touch it - and there is noone
else there.

> w
You move west.

Nave
The nave is a rather small room with walls plastered in white. Chandeliers
are hanging from the vault. The pews and the pulpit are painted in blue with
gilded decorations. Beside the door, under the gallery,  there is a table
with souvenirs. To the east you see the choir. The door do the porch is to
the west. There's also a door to the north.

> e
You move east.

Choir
The small choir is a narrow room dominated by a large altarpiece above the
altar. The picture represents the crucifixion of Christ and is painted by an
italian artist. It was donated to the church by Axel Gyllenpil in 1684,
according to a small sign on the wall. From here you can go west to the nave
or south to a chapel.

> s
You move south.

Chapel
You are in an chapel with aristocratic weapons and epitaphs. There's a
tombstone lying on the floor. It's a 17th century tombstone made of
limestone. A relief shows a man and a woman. Between them you can see an
aristocratic shield showing two crossed arrows. In a corner there is a steep
staircase.

> examine tombstone
A semi transparent creature appears - a ghost. It's a fat man in oldfashioned
clothes. His embroidered shirt has a lace collar and he wears high leather
boots. The ghost says: "My name is Axel Gyllenpil. Please help me to find my
sword." (Before you ask anything else, it would be appropriate to ask him
about family matters).

> d
You move down.

Crypt
The crypt is situated beneath the chapel. The room is filled with coffins
piled up on each other. It's dark and musty here, but comparatively clean and
tidy.

Also here is a sword.

> take sword
You take the sword. By accident you drop the plastic bag and a piece of
cheese falls out, the remains of your food. You take up the bag and the
cheese too.

Vestry
The vestry is a kind of office in the church. Here the priest changes clothes
and waits before the sermon begins. You see a table and chairs, and some
chests and cupboards for storing silver and textiles. A mouse is sitting on
the floor.

> give cheese to mouse
You offer a piece of cheese to the mouse. She comes towards you and eats the
cheese. Now she starts to talk:

"My parents told me that our ancestors remembered Axel Gyllengrip as a cruel
man. Once he killed a servant with that damned sword. He can't stand the
sword after that. It reminds him of the servant he once killed. That's why he
haunts our church. Now he treats me and the staff in the church as his
servants. So I put the sword in the organ, but Axel found it and throw it
into the crypt. He wanted to get the sexton's attention, but got none. He
needs blessing and forgiveness.

 [Press any key to continue]

The vicar and the sexton can't handle this. I must do something. Maybe I
should tell the vicar everything. We could have a sermon in the chapel for
Axel and the servant. Axel shouldn't see the sword anymore. We could send it
to the Historical museum, where it could be kept forever. Then Axel might
find peace at last."

"What an excellent idea!", you say.
"Then, please give me the sword!", the mouse says.

> give sword to mouse
You give the sword to the mouse. She says: "Thank you. Let's hope for the
best. You will hear from me soon."
You leave the church and go home. Some times later you get a letter and read:

Dear church visitor,
Axel Gyllenpil has come to peace after a sermon held in the chapel. We have
sent the sword to the Historical Museum. Thank you for your kind help.

Sincerely yours
The church mouse
