With a gorgeous wife to your left and beautiful son to your right, your
breakfast feels rather heavenly. You cannot help but smile. Susie returns the
smile. Your son, Terrence, is too busy downing his cereal to notice, but you
forgive him for being nine. When he has licked the bowl clean, he says, "Dad,
I saw the weirdest thing last night."

"Oh?" you say, raising an eyebrow.

"Yeah." He fiddles around with his spoon. "I got up and looked out the window
- there was some weird house out there. I thought there weren't any houses in
the woods so I checked this morning and it was gone!"

At first your conscious mind dismisses his story as childish imagination. You
let out an instinctive laugh. Then, however, your subconscious mind breaks
through a barrier it had been building for the past twenty years. The stern
look on your wife's face only strengthens the intrusion. You swallow and say,
"Well that's silly." But you can feel the lie coming out through your words.

~

Once Terrence has left the house to play with neighbourhood friends, Susie
brings up the breakfast conversation. "I think I know what it is," she says.
"And I know you have some idea."

You nod, unable to find words to respond.

"Uncle is calling me back, John. It can't mean anything else."

You sigh. "Yes, that would make sense. Will you go?"

"Of course I will. I've missed my poor old uncle. It's good to know he's
still alive, and I'd love to see him again."

"All right. Can I go with you?"

"Yes, of course."

~

Night has fallen. You and Susie tuck Terrence into bed. "You know that house
you talked about?" you ask him.

He nods.

"Well, your mother and I are going to visit it. We want you to stay in bed,
O.K.?"

He nods again. You and Susie take turns kissing his forehead and then shut
the door behind you. Equipped with flashlights, the two of you trek out into
the back yard. Destination: the Forest House.

Ending IV: Sacrifice

You wake to the steady sound of a heart monitor. Tubes are being fed into
you. Susie is there, holding your arm and smiling as your eyes flicker open.
You are lying on a hospital bed, clothed in hospital sheets. Your head feels
a little numb and you have trouble formulating thoughts, let alone words.

"Hi," Susie says. Her tone tells you she has not said that to you in some
time. Tears well up in her eyes and she wipes them away. "I was worried about
you."

"Wha-" you say. The full sentence formed in your brain, but your body refused
to respond.

"Shhh." Susie puts a finger to your lips. "The doctor says you'll need rest
even after you wake."

You nod and close your eyes. It seems the best thing to do, or so your body
tells you.

When you wake some indefinite time later, Susie is gone, so you sleep again.

The third time you wake, she's at your side again. Terrence is standing next
to her, his face expressionless. "Hey," you say to him when you can. Then to
Susie, "How long was I out?"

She clears her throat. "It's been several weeks. The accident... well, we're
lucky that all of us survived.The truck driver lost control when he hit a
sheet of ice. We just happened to be in the wrong place at the wrong time."

You nod. Funny how life goes full-circle sometimes. The last thing you
remember is going to sleep last night. Or, several weeks ago, as Susie said.
This incident reminds you of one in your childhood, though. Accidents can
cause terrible things, and no one is at fault.

~

Months later, with a gorgeous wife to your left and beautiful son to your
right, your breakfast feels rather heavenly. You cannot help but smile. Susie
returns the smile. Terrence, is too busy downing his cereal to notice, but
you forgive him for being nine. When he has licked the bowl clean, he says,
"Dad, I saw the weirdest thing last night."

"Oh?" you say, raising an eyebrow.

"Yeah." He fiddles around with his spoon. "I got up and looked out the window
- there was some weird house out there. I thought there weren't any houses in
the woods so I checked this morning and it was gone!"

Your first instinct is the feeling of deja-vu, as though you had heard this
before. You swallow and say, "That's interesting, Terrence. When I was your
age, or maybe a little older, --"

A knock at the door interrupts you. "I'll get it," you say. "Wonder who it
could be this early..."

You open the door to find a familiar, elderly man standing on your porch.
"Don't tell Susie I'm here," Uncle says. "We need to talk."

You look behind you to make sure Susie can't see from the kitchen and nod.
"Come in," you say, inviting him upstairs. You take a moment to tell Susie
and Terrence that a business call has come up and you need to talk to this
man, then go up to meet with him.

"You were dead," you say, flooded with memories you could not have had.

Uncle says, "Was I? Or did you merely perceive that I was dead."

You ponder this for a moment, but give no response.

"To tell you the truth, all of this was your making. I think you understand
this now."

"Yes. My buried emotions caused it. But how?"

He sighs and begins pacing. "Like I told you long ago, the infinite
vibrations of the multiverse create infinite dimensions. However, these
vibrations do not go unaffected by certain fifth-dimensional effects."

"Fifth dimension?"

"Yes. The realm of emotion, idea, and thought. These things cause strong
inter-vibrational communications, sometimes changing the events of another
dimension."

"So you're saying my buried emotions caused something in another dimension?"

"More than caused." He stops pacing and looks at you. "Your emotions created
something, namely, the dark twin of you which invaded my home."

"Right. So I killed the Master, which brought you and me back."

"Again, more than you say. Time flows differently between the vibrations, so
you have made it such that the Master never existed. Anything he affected has
been undone."

"So Terrence..."

"Never saw the house. Well, at least until I decided to visit."

"And Susie?"

"She never saw me die."

"So then, how did I see you die? Why do I still have these memories?"

He sighs again, longer this time. "You are the one who caused this chain of
events. In the time continuum of what is you, these events have a certain
permanence which cannot be erased. Your time, however, must still match up
with the rest of your dimension."

"Hence, the coma."

"Precisely. All of this is a direct result of your sacrifice. Rather than
choose between those you love, as only an Evil man would, you chose to
sacrifice yourself." He falls silent for a moment. "So then, have you all the
answers you need?"

"More than, I think."

You see him off secretly, Susie still unaware of his visit. You return to
breakfast.

There, between your gorgeous wife and beautiful son, you find that you are
happy.
