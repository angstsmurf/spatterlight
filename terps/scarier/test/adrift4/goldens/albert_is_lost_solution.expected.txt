An Adventure in Real Life
Written by Tiberius Thingamus using ADRIFT 4.

[press almost any key to continue]

     They were just about to have their picture drawn by a sketch artist at
the Annual Fall Festival in Oldeland, Ohio when Tiberius's littler
brother-type person, Albert (also of the clan Thingamus), disappeared!

     Now Tiberius must uncover treasure & the long-lost secrets of magic in
order to discover whence his brother hath hid and rescue him from potential
danger by evil wizards!

The Sketch Artist's Booth

sketch artist.  She sits by her easel, playing idly with her drawing
instruments.  A trail south leads to a little hill where buskers sometimes
entertain people eating lunch, and a less-used one leads east to a secluded
section of the festival.   Also nearby is yon stout bush.

Fortune Teller 5000
Tiberius has now ventured into shadows unexplored by the common
festival-goer.  Under the near-black, sun-killing shade of four tarps in a
little alley between the empty, cobwebbed stalls of long-forgotten vendors
stands a monolith from another age-- the Fortune Teller 5000.  Nearby is a
waste barrel for fortunes cast aside in bygone years, decades, maybe even
millenia, and next to that standeth a change machine.  Two barely discernible
paths lead west to the sketch artist's booth and south towards the sugared
grease smell of a food vendor's trailer.

> x teller
A cabinet covered in glowing lights with a coin slot and ticker tape
dispenser.  Garish-coloured writing in an oversized font adorns the machine
in a semi-circular pattern.

"Forecast ye fortune, ONLY 1 Quarter!  Ask me ANYTHING!"

> open pouch
Tiberius unclosess the belt pouch.  A quarter is inside the belt pouch.

> get quarter
Tiberius picks ups the quarter from the belt pouch.

Wally the Stray slinketh in from the south.

> put quarter in coin slot
Tiberius puts/drops/feeds/inserts the solitary quarter into the Fortune
Teller 5000.

The cabinet lights up and makes some fanfare.

"WHAT HO!  A customer!" it printeth out in ticker tape.  "Looketh around and
ye shall findeth something in ye scenery hath changed (See Footnote 2).
Whate'er 'tis that hath changed may helpeth ye find ye little brother.  Feel
free to asketh me any other questions ye liketh and I shall do my best to
answereth."

When he finishes reading it, Tiberius disposes of the ticker tape fortune in
the conveniently nearby barrel.

> ask teller about strange
Tiberius glances over his shoulder and then whispers, "Say, O mighty fortune
telling box, doth ye noticeth any strange out-of-placeness here?"

Fortune Teller 5000 prints out: "My reply is nay."

Tiberius tosses out the fortune after reading it.

> w
Tiberius wanders west.

Trailer-O-Foode (sic)
Oh, the joy of Oldeland festival foods.  If vendors could deep fry pure sugar
in the essence of sodium, they would slather it in cheap barbeque sauce and
sell sticks of it for $6.50 a piece.  It's entirely unlike the organic
markets, where local farmers sell their produce, composting materials, and
delicious homemade salsas on the weekend.

Over there is the vendor's trailer, next to a low billboard.  North is a
shady part of the festival and the luncheon hill is to the west.
A grumpy-looking vendor sits behind the trailer window, scratching and
snarling.

"LOOKFROTHO!" invoketh Tiberius...

From out behind yon bush cometh a magical pwoof! and out bursts Albert who
shouts, "PIRATE SHIP!" for no particular reason (see Footnote 3) and bounds
into Tiberius's arms.

"Glad to findeth ye safe, little buddy," says Tiberius.  "Now let's getteth
yon mighty portraiture sketched."

Tiberius may now wish to ask The Sketch Artist about business.

> ask artist about business
"Milady," says Tiberius.  "My brother and I are now ready for the creation of
our portraits!"

"Alright," says the artist.

"Can they not be done as caricatures, but as real-life pictures?"

"Sure."

And so she drew them, and Tiberius and Albert went home happily, with many
tales to tell of their exciting adventures that day.
