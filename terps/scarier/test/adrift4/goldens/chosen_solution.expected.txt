   The Third Great War had been a foul and bloody affair.  For so long,
people had feared nuclear apocalypse...  But the truth was that come the

and tanks had been sent in by all three participating economic superpowers to
engage in a vicious, inch for inch meatgrinding slaughter.  You spent all

South Americans had used chemical weapons against the English trenches you'd
been in command of, killing three hundred soldiers in the single attack,
their skins burned away from their screaming faces...
   Luckily, the Western Alliance, England and the USA, had finally defeated
both the Middle East/South America allies and the Japanese, though at a
terrible cost.  The latter had been entirely wiped out, shelled non-stop for
fifteen months following your government's victory until only three percent
of its population still lived.
   But that's in the past now.  The world is at peace, a new age of
prosperity has arrived, and following the war, the fascism of the Southerners
and the desperate poverty of the middle east is dead, and the world is
finally united.  But still, watching this sunset, even with Sarah at your
side, you cannot help but remember...
   Pushing the thoughts from your mind, you turn and kiss her, and she
responds, knowing that you need distraction from your bloody past.  The names
of dead comrades, the blood of the squaddies you commanded, the agonised
stares of the Mexicans as you buried them alive in their own trenches all
fade from your mind as you sink to the soft, warm grass atop the hill,
beneath the darkening sky...
   But then, looking down into Sarah's eyes, you see her gazing past you, up
into the sky, a look of fear on her pretty face, across which falls a dark
shadow.  You react on instinct, battle-honed reflexes flipping your body up
and round, your fists swinging up ready to kill if need be...  But all you
see is a brilliant white light, and as you leap backward, your legs fail to
touch the floor.  For several seconds, you seem to be falling.  Then, with a
slight jolt, you land on your feet.
