Ravine

A narrow wooden bridge, perhaps six inches wide, spans this ravine. The wind
rushes past you, threatening at any moment to blow you into the ravine and
send you hurtling down, down, down to its bottom, all of a thousand feet
away. There might be a river down there or there might be cold stone. Either
way, it's a long fall. A small bird, the sort of thing you might use for
target practice on another day, is fluttering around.

On the other side of the ravine, the mountain fortress of Baron Grishtak
rises imposingly into the sky.

North takes you across the ravine, south away from here.

> give mackerel to bird
It goes against every instinct you have, but you suppress your desire to grab
the bird by the throat and throttle it, and instead offer it the mackerel
still clinging to your fork.

The bird eyes you suspiciously. You've never seen this one before, but your
reputation as someone to be avoided by the bird community has no doubt spread
to even these remote parts. But then greed gets the better of the bird's fear
and it flies over, snatches the fork in its beak, and flies away again.

You watch it go.

Suddenly there is a twittering sound from above you and you look up to see a
flock of birds descending. Before you can even think abou throttling the lot
of them (a risky enterprise even for you), the birds latch onto you, dig
their claws into your uniform... and lift you up off the ground!

You struggle, trying to break free... but then happen to glance down and see
you're now over the ravine. All thoughts of breaking free, and what might
happen if you did break free, vanish from your mind and you stay very, very
still.

The birds carry you across the ravine, depositing you on the far side with
less of a bump than the time you had to crashland a Concorde after you threw
the pilot out of the door at 32,000 feet believing he was a terrorist.

One bird, it looks familiar, gives you a quick nod and then they all fly off.

Approach To The Fortress

Before you rises the steep, almost unclimbable wall of the mountain peak upon
which Baron Grishtak has built his fortress. It is lost somewhere in the
clouds above, hidden from view.

Right before you, perhaps sixty feet high, is the outer wall of the fortress
itself. Comprised of huge stone blocks ten feet in size themselves, the wall
has an immovable look about it. There is a gate situated partway along, to
one side of a tower, currently closed tight (the gate, that is, not the
tower).

Back along the path to the south is the ravine, the gate is to the northwest
and upwards lie the mountain side.

> x path
The path leads back to the ravine, which looks every bit as uncrossable from
this side as it did from the other. There is a stone lying by the side of the
path.

> get stone
You take the stone from the side of the path. As far as weapons go, it's
hardly an M16 but it ought to do the job of giving someone a serious headache
if you throw it hard enough.

> x tower
The tower is perhaps forty feet tall. Wisps of smoke drift from an upper
window, indicating that there is someone within. Guards perhaps.

> throw stone at window
Your skill with throwing stones isn't the same as your skill with rifles (you
once shot the ears of a fleeing suspect at six hundred yards), but you're a
dab hand at it all the same. You cock back your arm, weigh the stone, wait
for your moment and then - just as a silhouette passes across the window -
throw the stone.

It sails through the air, through the window and there is a dull, thudding
sound followed by a louder, screaming sound followed ultimately by the sound
of someone falling over.

> open gate
You approach the gate and give it a hefty push, shoving it open several feet.

No alarm bells go off. No sirens sound. No one tries to shoot you. And no
attack dogs come bounding over to tear you apart.

All well and good.

You slip through the gate and make your way along a winding path leading to
the grounds of the fortress.

The Fortress Grounds

The fortress of Baron Grishtak - an old castle, converted into a modern
castle by the look of things - rises majestically before you, its upper
reaches hidden in the very clouds. Between you and it lie the fortress
grounds: several hundred feet of seemingly innocent looking flowers, weeds
and bushes without a soldier, guard dog or security camera in sight.

Paths lead north to the main door of the fortress and northeast and northwest
around the sides of it. A further path leads away to the south.

As you're standing there, staring at the fortress, you suddenly realise
you've lost the photo of Princess Leonora. Somewhere between the ravine and
here, you must have misplaced it.

You hope this isn't an ill-omened sign for your mission.

some kind of garden keeper's hut, although judging from the state of the

particularly good job.

A path winds past the hut over to the northwest while southwest takes you
back to the fortress grounds.

Behind The Fortress

A bleak waste stretches away behind the fortress, eventually reaching an end
at a deep chasm which has split the ground. This waste is home to several
mouldering corpses and a number of recently filled graves.

The rear of the fortress is far more empty than its front, lacking doors and
windows. The only sign of ornamentation, in fact, seems to be a carving of
Baron Grishtak's face that someone has made here.

Paths lead away around the fortress to the southeast and the southwest.

> bury corpses
The ground is too hard to bury a corpse in and you lack any kind of digging
tools, but you feel better for considering it all the same.

As you're mulling over one of the corpses, a handkerchief tumbles from the
corpse's pocket and drops to the ground.

> get handkerchief
You take the handkerchief.

> x corpses
The corpses are long dead. Some of them are missing a limb or two whereas a
couple are missing just about everything but a limb or two. It's a gruesome
sight, but nothing that you, The Bulldog, haven't seen before. The main
difference being that generally when you see lots of corpses like this, it's
you that made them corpses.

A glint catches your eye and you move closer to one of the corpses: a tall,
handsome fellow (even with half his face missing). He has a watch on his
wrist that fairly gleams. After a second's careful consideration, you take
the watch off his wrist.

> x watch
A secret agent's watch. You can tell it's a secret agent's watch by the
S.A.W. motif on the side. And by the fact that the time it keeps doesn't seem
to be the same kind of time as the rest of the world (probably on account of
all the nifty secret agent devices cunningly hidden inside).

There is an indentation in the side of the watch, about the size of a
fingernail.

Inside A Ramshackle Hut

The interior of the hut is full of smoke. Thick, cloying smoke that threatens
to choke you at every turn. There is precious little here in the way of
anything useful and it looks like the hut has just been used to store all
manner of junk over the years.  A window on one wall, dirty and begrimed,
shows the side of the fortress but nothing more interesting than that. A
doorway, where there previously was a door, leads outside.

> x junk
A pile of junk, thrown haphazardly into the direct centre of the hut. You
can't help but feel that the way it's there is somewhat deliberate, as if it
has been put there for a reason.

The junk comprises a pole, a large rock and a tennis ball.

On A Straggly Path

You're on a straggly path running around the western side of the fortress.
Here, the fortress wall shoots straight up into the air, so smooth it's
almost like someone has polished it to prevent intruders climbing up it.
Thick bushes crowd the path, their branches poking painfully at you as you
struggle past.

Partway up the wall, previously hidden by bushes, you spy a wide open window.

The path leads further round the fortress to the north and southeast.

> x bushes
The bushes are thick and... well, bushy. You look into them, noticing nothing
that you wouldn't expect to see in a bush growing at the side of a fortress
run by an evil warlord-

And then you catch sight of something you didn't expect to see: a cudgel.

You snatch it up from the bush, relieved to have found a weapon at last. It's
no anti-tank gun but it'll certainly come in handy in a tight corner.

> use pole
You've never been much of an athlete, but you know the basics of what needs
to be done. Armed with the pole, you back up several metres, take a deep
breath, and then run forwards.

At the last moment, you jab the end of the pole into the ground and vault
over the area immediately before the fortress, coming to a rest neatly before
the front door and springing to your feet.

All in all, a pretty nifty bit of pole-vaulting, though you say so yourself.

Pity that you had to let go of the pole to get here but you probably won't
need it again anyway.

Front Door Of The Fortress

The front door of the fortress rises before you, larger than any door really
has any right to be. It is pitted and scarred with age, but still looks more
than sturdy enough to keep an army from battering it down. There looks to be
no means of opening the door from here; the controls are no doubt on the
other side.

A security camera has been mounted on an outcropping of stone just above the
door and its lens follows your every movement. There is a stone gargoyle next
to it, mouth permanently open in a perpetual growl of anger.

The closed door of the fortress lies to the north while the fortress grounds
are back to the south.

Entrance Hall

Judging by the sign hanging over archway to the south - WELCOME TO CASTLE
VUURGHEIM! - you assume this must be the entrance hall of the former Dark
Ages castle. Baron Grishtak clearly hasn't made much effort to tidy the place
up and there is dust covering every surface, from the old desk to the counter
running along one wall.

Torches line the walls, a throwback to the time before electricity, and these
cast flickering shadows over the proceedings.

There are passages to the north and east, and a flight of steps lead
downwards to the wine cellar.

> x desk
This clearly isn't a feature of the original Dark Ages castle that the
fortress once was. This is a newer feature, although why it has been dumped
down here in the entrance hall you can't imagine.

It's an old school desk, complete with graffiti and chewing gum even now. The
top can be opened.

> read graffiti
The graffiti is pretty much what you'd expect to find on an old school desk:

I ate the teacher!
Maffs suks!
Keeley 4 Simon!
Letter 2 is E!
eye dunna ned 2 atend skool as eye kan alredy spel grate neway!
Codeword: blogmore

You frown at the last of these: 'codeword: blogmore'. Does that mean
something? Does it bear some relevance to your current mission? Or, more
likely, is it just something that some school kid wrote a long time ago and
doesn't mean anything?

> open desk
Your old desk at school generally had an assortment of weapons in it: knives,
bayonets and maces being your favouritesssss. The owner of this desk clearly
wasn't of the same temperament as you, though, as all you find inside are
some ancient flowers (now withered away), a bible and a small, empty bottle.
After a moment's hesitation, you take the bible and the bottle, and leave the
flowers where they are.

Circular Chamber

What purpose this perfectly circular chamber has you cannot guess. The walls
are curved slightly, allowing the chamber to form a circular shape instead of
the more normal square or rectangular shapes of other rooms.

In the middle of the chamber there is a pit which appears to lead down into
the very depths beneath the fortress. An unwholesome stench emanates from the
pit, as well as a keening sound, somewhat like a cry.

Passages lead east and west away from here.

> e

Curving Passageway

On either wall of this curving passageway, tapestries have been hung
depicting the fortress when it was a Dark Ages castle. The tapestries are old
and tattered, fluttering slightly in the light breeze that blows along here.

A glowing panel is set into the ceiling, perhaps twenty feet above your head.
Luminous liquid drips from one side of it.

The passageway curves away to the west and northwest.

> x tapestries
By the look of things, these tapestries depict the former rulers of the
fortress, back in the days when it was a Dark Ages castle. There are a dozen
tapestries in total but most of them are so tattered and decayed with age
that they are more holes than tapestries. However, the third, sixth and tenth
tapestries are in better condition, albeit more than a little rugged
themselves.

> x tapestry three
This depicts a great bear of a man standing on a blue field and carrying a
halberd. About him are strewn the corpses of many fallen enemies.

A little plaque beneath the tapestry indicates this man to be "Gulgrin
Smersh, 5th Lord of Castle Vuurgheim".

> x gulgrin smersh
Smersh looks to be the kind of brutal fellow you'd imagine to see in charge
of a Dark Ages castle. The halberd he carries has the look of a weapon that
has seen a lot of action, and you suspect Smersh himself was a dab hand at
swiping heads off with it.

> x halberd
The halberd is soaked with gore which drips onto the blue field around it.
Something about the tip of the halberd looks a little off.

> x tip
What you're trying to look at isn't important enough to warrant a
description.

> x tapestry six
This tapestry is badly burnt, but you can still see the man shown there: a
snake-like fellow with beady eyes, clutching fingers and hollow cheeks.

The plague beneath the tapestry says his name to be "Eccles Montoron, 8th
Lord of Castle Vuurgheim".

> x eccles montaron
Eccles has the look of an evil-minded sociopath. That or a politician. You're
not sure which. The way he is depicted makes it evident that even the
tapestry didn't hold any clear liking for him. There looks to be a bulge
around his head, perhaps some kind of abnormal growth or distortion.

> push bulge
You push the bulge and it sinks into the wall slightly. Somewhere in the
fortress there is a click!!! sound but nothing else happens.

> push bulge
You push the bulge again. It sinks a little further into the wall but there
is no click!!! sound this time.

Kitchen

M
any, many years ago, before you were even known as The Puppy, this would have
been the kitchen where meals for the occupants of the fortress were prepared.
The great iron stove in the corner - a monstrosity large enough to fit both
Hansel and Gretel in at the same time and still have room left over for an
evil witch or two - still seems to emit heat, indicating that the kitchen is
perhaps not as deserted as it appears.

Rotten food has been scattered across the floor and dozens of rats fight for
it. A large pile by the west wall emits the most pungent stench and has the
largest rats fighting for it.

Passages lead out of the kitchen to the southeast, northeast and west.

[MORE]

As you step into the kitchen, senses alert for trouble, the sound of a door
opening and closing somewhere to the west can be heard. At the same time, you
hear a voice that you thought never to hear again:

"Bulldog - that you, ol' buddy?"

If that isn't your imagination playing tricks with you (unlikely as you don't
have an imagination), that's the voice of none other than Tiberius 'The
Tiger' Tugmore, your arch rival from your days at the SAS Training Academy.
And a man you thought was long dead.

But then into the room steps none other than The Tiger. He looks... not good.

[MORE]

The years clearly haven't been kind to him: he's lost one arm and had it
replaced with a metallic one; the left side of his face is a scarred ruin;
and he doesn't speak he booms. Probably due to the weird metal contraption
he's got bolted to his throat.

"Damnit, Bulldog," he says, "but you're still every bit as ugly as you were
back when I first laid eyes on you."

You grunt. You don't need to use a lot of insulting words; enough is carried
in that one grunt to get your message across.

Something flashes in The Tiger's one good eye. "That's a low one for sure,
Bulldog. Damn, if I wasn't already planning to kill you anyway, I'd do it
just for that."

[MORE]

You grunt a question.

"Damn straight, ol' buddy. The Baron put out a message that he was looking
for some muscle to help him organise a terrorist plot and there I was,
recuperatin' in some crappy little hospital while they tried to sew my body
back together after that little accident I had, and I thought 'hey, what a
swell idea'. So here I am." He taps his metallic arm. "And I got a few toys
besides." He taps the arm again. A bright flame starts to burn on the metal
tip, where his hand should be. "Oh man, I'm gonna enjoy this!"
