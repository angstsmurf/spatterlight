        _____________________________

        "A Record of Anatomical & Behavioural Mutations in Local Apiform
Life"
            Dr. Malcolm J. Fitzwold, Sarkalouga University

            This study addresses an indigenous species of bee, the Common
Sarkalougan (Apis Sarkalougous Mellifera, bred at the university) and its
hybrid with an exogenous species, the Africanized Honey Bee or "Killer
Bee"(Apis  Millifera Scutellata, imported from Brasil).  These species have
been crossbreeding since the early days of Sarkalouga University's
establishment,  and have produced normal offspring with two eyes & six legs.
Notable amongst samples of both species at the university is the relatively
high rate of giganticism.  The anomaly is noteworthy; non-behaviourally
affective & harmless by itself in original bee species as well as subsequent
hybrids, the presence of certain pollutants may otherwise explain the strange
behaviour recorded in my previously published article (see Apiary Chronicle,
Issue 42, Vol. IV) .

            To summarise the previous article, a series of events introducing
trace amounts of klarthotium into the local atmosphere & soil have produced
three distinct mutations in local apiform life.  The first in 1972 caused
larvae to develop a third compound eye as they entered adulthood.  The second
in 1974 caused larvae to develop only two hind legs and a pair of pincers
mounted on the abdomen.  In 1975, a mutation created three-eyed,  two-legged
bees (whereas previous mating of three-eyed bees with two-legged bees had not
been able to produce viable offspring).  Exposure to klarthotium appears to
have incited aggression and hostility by conjectured mechanism of klarthotium
on stem cell development in larvae and influences on neurotransmitter
activity in adulthood.

            EMERGENCY.  Experiment cannot continue… mass contamination, all
specimens affected… bees have revolted!  The KING OF ALL EVIL has led the
mutants into the old salt mines-- for fear of my life I dare not follow-- yet
it is only a matter of time before they rise up and seek their revenge!

        Publication:                The Atmospheric Observer
        Article:                        "Unidentified Objects Lands in the
Mountains South of Sarkalouga!"
        Author:                 Joachim Hakim.
        Release Date:                09/14/85

     Astronomers observed a large object falling through the atmosphere last
night at 6:05 AM, Eastern Standard Time.
     Astronomers say the object is massive enough and sustained little enough
damage during re-entry that search parties should find it intact just south
of the mountains surrounding  Sarkalouga, Georgia.  The object remains, as
yet, unidentified.
     A majority of astronomers interviewed support the prevailing skeptical
claim that it was likely some form of meteorite.
     "I was actually the first one to catch sight of it," says Dr. Philton
Begelski. "I'm not entirely sure how no-one else at the station saw it, but
these things happen."
     According to Begelski, the object could not have been an alien craft, as
it "didn't have the right shape."  He also added: "I deal with this sort of
thing everyday.  I'm far past the point of getting excited every time I see
something fall down to the Earth.  I wish this sort of thing got less
publicity so I could[…] quit getting hassled about little, green men every
five minutes."
     Some groups, however, reject Begelski's meteor and insist on the UFO
theory.
     "This supposed meteor traveled the entire distance through our
atmosphere without sustaining any  damage," says John Colland, a member of
Aliens Are Among Us.  "Terrestrial metals don't have that kind of structural
integrity.  What we are dealing with is not a random cluster of rocks-- this
object has to have been intelligently crafted, and that can only mean that it
is a craft of some alien origin."
     Begelski insists that this is "screwy logic."
     Although many are eager to begin inspection of the object's landing
site, the debris thrown up by the object's planetfall has obstructed paths
into the mountains.  Sarkalouga police have barricaded all but main entrances
into the mountains for security reasons while police decide how to  handle
this situation.
     Local construction crews hope to have the debris cleared away within a
week.
