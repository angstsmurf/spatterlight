The Farthest Shore
An Interactive Reverie by Stewart J. McAbney

   Hungry, confused, desperate, you curl up in the dinghy looking out into
the bleak emptiness. Too tired for questions, your mind drifts through
imagination after torrid imagination as you seek a warm thought to steady
your mind, a reason for optimism.

In a dinghy
   Water splashes in as the wind encourages the sea to spill over the
fluorescent flaps of the rapidly deflating vessel. Fog, wrapped around the
sky, conceals the seascape. Nothing is here, nothing but the shadows of
unseen entities, and they soon dart from sight as you turn on them.

>   The cymballine crash of the sea played by the percussionist wind mixes
disturbingly with whispers of unknown origin.

>   The metallic tang of the sea fused with the stench of salt and dying
greenery tease you.

>   Although your mind is numbed of sense, you decide against stepping out of
the dinghy and into the sea.

>Time passes...

   Unexpected by even yourself after days of drifting, the dinghy lands
roughly upon a reef of stones - land leads east into the unknown.

>Time passes...

>You move east.
On a grey beach
   A lone tree weeps at the edge of the beach, its roots firmly planted in
the grey sand. The wind toys with its fronds, a vicious unseen harpy, as the
sea washes upon the sand, the sting of icy spray hanging in the air.

   A hill leads up into the fog and your dinghy sits on the rocks to the
west.

>   The tree hangs its fronds in shame, its black trunk holding it steadily to
the ground against the torment of the wind.

>You move up.
Atop a hill
   With its summit firmly lost in the mesh of grey cloud and dirty fog, the
hill offers only a distressed signpost pointing south and west.

   Paths, or ragged lines of stones, lead off in said directions; another
path leads down to the beach.

>   A wooden signpost firmly planted in the empty ground, rotten and plagued
by a dark moss.

>You move west.
By a ruined cabin
   Built from rough stones and rotten driftwood, a small cabin takes a
beating from the wind. A ragged curtain, tugged by an unseen force, slaps
itself aganst the shack's stone face then floats effortlessly before cracking
itself against the cabin again.

   A barely discernible path leads east into the fog.

>   A gruesome blend of harsh stone blocks stacked within a frame of gnarled
driftwood, the cabin is a small outpost, the home of unseen entities.

>You move in.
Inside the cabin
   Empty but for a small table, the cabin appears long forgotten - rotten
beams struggle to hold it together and a basket of withered wicker cowers in
the corner.

   A doorway leads out to the filthy unknown.

>   A block of rough stone - crudely chiselled - topped with a collection of
wooden sticks, moss covered and rotten.

>   You are wearied by starvation and the onslaught of the elements - you
couldn't hope to lift the basket.

>   You try to take one of the turnips but it is so rotten that it almost
fades in your hand, the soggy flesh crumbling into the basket.

>You take the leek from the basket.

>   You nibble the root of the leek, its foul taste matching its foul stench.

>You move out.
By a ruined cabin
   Built from rough stones and rotten driftwood, a small cabin takes a
beating from the wind. A ragged curtain, tugged by an unseen force, slaps
itself aganst the shack's stone face then floats effortlessly before cracking
itself against the cabin again.

   A barely discernible path leads east into the fog.

>You move east.
Atop a hill
   With its summit firmly lost in the mesh of grey cloud and dirty fog, the
hill offers only a distressed signpost pointing south and west.

   Paths, or ragged lines of stones, lead off in said directions; another
path leads down to the beach.

>You move south.
A barren field
   Guarded by fenceposts without any beams, you can only guess as to how vast
the field is - the fog blocks your vision beyond the first few metres. Grass,
a dirty grey variety, grows in ragged patches around the field.

   A barely discernible path leads north into the fog.
   Tugging at the barren ground, a cow wanders aimlessly through the gloom.

>   The cow's bones audibly creak as it turns to sniff the leek. Tentatively
it nibbles on the root before collecting the full vegetable from your hand.
Finished chewing, it turns to you in expectation of another leek but ignores
you when it realises no more offerings are forthcoming.

>You move north.
Atop a hill
   With its summit firmly lost in the mesh of grey cloud and dirty fog, the
hill offers only a distressed signpost pointing south and west.

   Paths, or ragged lines of stones, lead off in said directions; another
path leads down to the beach.

>You move down.
On a grey beach
   A lone tree weeps at the edge of the beach, its roots firmly planted in
the grey sand. The wind toys with its fronds, a vicious unseen harpy, as the
sea washes upon the sand, the sting of icy spray hanging in the air.

   A hill leads up into the fog and your dinghy sits on the rocks to the
west.
   A light, dim in the fog, shines in from the south.

>
   Hungry, confused, desperate, you stagger out towards the light. Could it
be a boat? Are they looking for you? Questions, questions, questions - all of
them racing through your mind.
   And into the water, slipping on the rocks as you go, your eyes locked upon
the light, your mind fixed upon dreams of rescue. You don't feel the icy
chill soak through your clothes, feel the sting of the wind fighting your
advance.
   And under the water you go as you head out beyond the shallow depths, the
light shimmering somewhere above the surface - still, however, distant. It
feels nice, now. The wind doesn't reach you here; the sea can't snap out at
you with its watery talons.
   You close your eyes and the light, like yours, is gone.

   Somewhere, adrift on the farthest shore, a dinghy arrives on the rocks of
an island shrouded in a steel fog.
