Exits of The World -- Jim R. Harbis, QDK Lite 4.1.1, ASL 410
============================================================

A very small Doctor Who game: fourteen rooms, five objects that matter, and one
win.  You are the Doctor, standing at "The World" with Davros' Lair below you;
Davros has enslaved humanity and your belongings were scattered when you last
regenerated.  Recovering two of them -- the Sonic Screwdriver and the Type 40
TARDIS -- is the whole of the game, and everything else follows from those.

What the geography actually is
------------------------------
"The World" is a hub with ten directional exits.  Nine of them lead to rooms
named Exit 1 to Exit 10, and all ten of those rooms share the alias "Exits of
The World", the prefix "the" and the description "These are indeed the ends of
the earth..." -- so the compass rose looks like one enormous room that you can
wander for ever without getting anywhere.  Only two of the ten have an exit that
leaves the ring:

  * Exit 10 (DOWN from The World) has `out <J'doon Impound>` -- the pound where
    your TARDIS is chained up.  This is the way in.
  * Exit 9 (UP from The World) has
        out locked <Skarro; This leads to Skarro. home planet of the Daleks.
                    You need a ship to get there.>
    which is the game's one locked exit and its one real gate.

That lock is worth a note.  Quest's RoomExit.Go tests the lock before it looks
at anything else (RoomExit.cs:240-259) and the "locked" keyword is stripped for
"out" by the same parser that handles north/south (RoomExits.cs:143-147), so
Quest refuses the exit and prints the message.  geas used to check locks only in
its directional loop, so OUT from Exit 9 teleported you to Skarro on turn one --
with no TARDIS, no screwdriver and no way to leave again, since Skarro's two
`place` exits both need the TARDIS.  That is fixed, with fixtures/outlocked.asl
guarding it; the script below walks up to the lock deliberately (UP then OUT) so
that the refusal appears in the transcript.

The other gate is the hub's own `place` exit:

    place <Davros' Lair> if got <Type 40 TARDIS> and got <Dalekanium Panels>
        then goto <Davros' Lair> else msg <Even though you want to confront
        Davros right now, you need to upgrade your ship...>

GO TO DAVROS' LAIR is therefore refused until you hold both the ship and the
Dalekanium armour for it.  The room also declares, on the line above,
`out <Davros' Lair>` -- a plain, unguarded exit to the same place.  So OUT from
The World walks into the lair with no ship and no armour at all, which makes the
guarded place exit ornamental: the author gated the polite route and left the
back door open.  The script uses the intended route (GO TO) and only after the
guard is satisfied, so the refusal is shown once and then honoured.

The chain
---------
The startscript conceals Dalekanium Panels, microbacteria, Dead Davros, Radar
Device and Re-configured Radar Device, and hides Martha Jones and the Humans.
Every one of those is switched on by a single specific action, in this order:

  1. SPEAK TO ENSLAVED HUMANS.  Their speak script has two halves: with the
     TARDIS they beg for a doctor, without it they only moan -- and the
     *without* half is the one that `reveal`s the Sonic Screwdriver ("A pretty
     black girl dropped something on the ground").  Speak to them after you have
     the TARDIS and you never get the screwdriver, and with no screwdriver
     nothing else in the game opens.  This is the one place where the game can be
     made unwinnable, and it is the first thing you do.
  2. TAKE SPACE.  "space" is an object in The World whose take script reveals
     *and* gives the microbacteria -- you reach into the sky and come back with a
     handful of them.  Nothing hints at it; X SPACE only says outer space is
     vast and mysterious.
  3. DOWN, OUT to J'doon Impound, TAKE TYPE 40 TARDIS.  Its take script checks
     `if got <Sonic Screwdriver>`: with it you free the ship, without it "Your
     TARDIS is bounded by chains. There is a lock, but you have no key."
  4. GO TO SKARRO (now that the ship makes the `place` exits work), USE SONIC
     SCREWDRIVER ON DALEK SHELLS to reveal the Dalekanium Panels, TAKE them.
  5. GO TO THE WORLD, GO TO DAVROS' LAIR -- both guards now satisfied.
  6. SPEAK TO DAVROS and pick choice 1.  Choice 2 ("No, I don't...") is
     `playerlose`: he shoots you with a portable Dalek ray gun.  Choice 1 gets
     you nothing but a taunt about monologuing, which is fine -- the answer is
     not in the conversation.
  7. USE MICROBACTERIA ON DAVROS.  He is eaten alive by the enzymes; that hides
     Davros and reveals Dead Davros and the Radar Device.
  8. TAKE RADAR DEVICE, USE SONIC SCREWDRIVER ON RADAR DEVICE (hides it, reveals
     the Re-configured Radar Device), TAKE RE-CONFIGURED RADAR DEVICE.
  9. OUT to The World, USE RE-CONFIGURED RADAR DEVICE ON ENSLAVED HUMANS.  The
     slaves are hidden and both the Humans and Martha Jones are shown.
 10. SPEAK TO HUMANS, choice 3 -- the one that ends "...Ms. Jones, would you
     care to travel with me?".  Its script `show`s Martha Jones, who is already
     shown by step 9, so its real job is the line "Speak to Ms. Jones to
     continue."  Choices 1 and 2 are dead ends that leave the game running.
 11. SPEAK TO MARTHA JONES, choice 1 -- her selection has exactly one choice and
     it is `playerwin`.

Notes on the objects
--------------------
  * The Sonic Screwdriver is `drop nowhere <You need to be less careless.>`, so
    it cannot be lost, and `invisible` until the slaves drop it.
  * "space" is `drop nowhere <>` with an empty message -- dropping it prints a
    blank line.  It is never actually taken (its take script gives you
    microbacteria instead), so this never comes up.
  * The Dalekanium Panels have a bare `take` with no message, so taking them
    prints the default "You pick them up."  They are never used on anything: the
    "upgrade your ship" they are supposed to be for is only the `place` guard's
    `got` test.
  * Davros' Lair has no `look` of its own, so entering it prints the game's
    default description.

The script wins the game on the last line; the win text is
"...plotting the coordinates for your next adventure."
