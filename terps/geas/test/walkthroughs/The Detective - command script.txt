The Detective -- Catalin Nita, v1.0, 2005 (The Detective.cas, ASL 350)

A thriller told entirely through room descriptions, with no objects, no
inventory, no verbs and no library: 27 rooms, each of which is one scene, and
every action in the game is a room-level `command <...>` whose script is either a
msg or a `goto` to the next scene.  You never examine anything and you never
carry anything -- the "Inventory:" list on bridge street is text inside the
room's look tag.  Detective Peterson investigates a warehouse, loses his wife,
is blackmailed into a murder and a bank robbery, and the story switches you
between playing Peterson and playing the police officer chasing him.

Type what the room prints.  A scene that ends "type enter to continue" means the
literal command ENTER; some scenes want E, or S, or H, or P instead, and which
one it is is arbitrary.  Where a room offers a choice, one branch is usually
`playerlose` with a message calling it "one of the unfortunate endings" -- there
are twelve of those, and they are the only endings the engine recognises as
endings at all.


There is no playerwin

The file contains no `playerwin` anywhere, and `define text <win>` is empty.  Part
2 finishes in the Las Vegas warehouse with

    congrutalations. You have just completed 'The last detective'
    Enter this password at the main menu to unlock 'special features.
    zx12df

and then sends you back to the office, still running.  That line is the marker.


The password gates

The office is both the first room and the main menu, and it is where the game's
three passwords are typed.  Its own look text advertises two of them without
saying what they are:

    type the password here if you want to play  ' part 2'
    type the password here if you want to access 'special features'

  * WERTYUSDF unlocks part 2.  It is printed at the end of part 1.
  * ZX12DF unlocks the special features.  It is printed at the end of part 2.
  * MATE is the reward for finishing the "situations" minigame, and typing it at
    the special-features menu plays a coda in which the boss reveals that the
    body in the park was the wife's twin sister and the real wife was alive
    underground in the American warehouse -- which contradicts the study scene in
    part 1, where the son finds his mother murdered.  The game shipped both.

Because all three are commands in the office, all three work the moment you
start: nothing checks whether you have earned them.  The script below earns them
in order anyway.

Part 1 opens on something else entirely -- a five-question true/false quiz about
Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince, offering "the magnificent prize money",
with four wrong answers that are each a `playerlose`.  Answering the fifth
correctly prints "type enter to continue", and ENTER is what finally produces the
1) new game / 2) story menu.  The right answers are TRUE, 607, BLOOMSBURY,
HALF-BLOOD PRINCE.

Every keypad in the game is the same puzzle: you are shown four or five digits
"jumbled up" and have to type the intended order, which you cannot deduce -- you
have to know it.  The ones on the critical path are 376 (back entrance, from
637) and 3296 (warehouse gate, from 2639).  The C4 puzzle is stranger: you are
shown 7291 and have to answer with letters, DBCA, under a scheme the trainee
explains with two worked examples that do not agree with each other.


Branches the script takes, and the ones it doesn't

Two scenes fork into different rooms rather than into a loss, and the script
takes the longer side of both:

  * The lounge offers GET A PIZZA or WATCH TV.  Watching TV goes straight to HQ;
    the pizza detours through the Pizza Hut, where all three toppings lead to
    the same phone call.
  * HQ offers a fake ID or the helicopter.  The fake ID goes straight to the
    facility; the helicopter adds a scene where you are shot down and parachute
    in.  Both land outside the facility.

The one place a wrong-looking answer is required is the trainee's office: the
question is "do you get it, yes or no", and NO prints the second worked example.
The script answers NO and then YES, which is allowed because both are just
commands.

Two `command <>` entries with no name at all sit in HQ and at 25 Bridge St, and
`command <up>` and `command <left>` in the facility car park have no script, so
those two turns print nothing.  The script types them to show they exist.

The special features are three minigames that are pure keypad drills.  The
30-level keypad run in the script is the full set of answers; the "situations"
run is levels 1 to 5 (USE C4, HOUSE, UNIVERSE, then either direction in the
forest, then 32036), whose reward is the MATE password.
