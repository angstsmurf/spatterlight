Holocaust WW2 History 2011 Project -- anonymous, ASL 410 (QDK 4.1.5)
====================================================================
distributed as "final of social studies.cas"

A ninth-grade social studies project, and it is exactly that: twenty-nine rooms
reconstructing Dachau -- the Administration Building, the Jourhaus gate with
"Arbeit macht frei" on it, three sets of prisoner barracks, the bunker and prison
cells, the greenhouse, the gas chamber, a guard tower -- with every person in the
camp frozen in place and a photograph or a translated document behind almost every
object.  You are a student who fell asleep in class.  The whole puzzle is to work
out where you are and say it out loud.

    define object <Green Figure>
        look <Its a strange green figure with long ears and who is very short.>
        speak msg <"You who are hmmm?", the green figure speaks.  "Figured out
                   where you are have you yet, hmm?"  "Well when you have, tell
                   me and help you leave this place I can.">
        action <speak I am in Dachau> {
            msg <You win! HMMMMMMMM................................................>
            playerwin
        }

Yoda is in a wash basin in Barracks 4.  SPEAK I AM IN DACHAU GREEN FIGURE is the
win, and there are three clues that spell the name out and stop one letter short
each time:

    Barracks 5, TOUCH OPEN WOUND   "I am in a concentration camp in Germany that
                                   is very famous and is called D...."
    Gas Chamber, the German Coins  "CURSE YOU CAMP DAC-..."
    the Lobby note                 "Take the new guests to the shower as soon as
                                   they get here."  Hmm strange.

The engine fix this game needed
-------------------------------
The header declares nineteen game-scope verbs, and several of them have a trailing
space in the name because that is what the author typed into QDK's verb box:

    verb <use > msg <You can't use  that.>
    verb <use key in> msg <You can't use key in that.>
    ...
    verb <use> msg <You can't use that.>

Quest reads a verb name with GetParameter, which does not trim (V4Game.cs:1874),
and ExecVerb then tests `BeginsWith(cmd, thisVerb & " ")` (ibid. 2978-3003).  So
"verb <use >" is matched against "use  " with two spaces and can never fire, and
the next verb in declaration order gets the input.  That is load-bearing here:
the only locked door in the game is opened by the Closed Door's `action <use key
in>`, which is reachable solely because the dead "verb <use >" is skipped.  geas
trimmed the name, so "use " claimed every USE in the file, USE KEY IN CLOSED DOOR
answered "You don't see any key in closed door.", and Big Office 2 -- Himmler's
office, with the aerial photograph of the camp on the desk -- was unreachable.
test/fixtures/verbspace.asl pins the rule in both directions, since the same
trailing space is *not* dead when the name sits in a semicolon list.

What those verbs do to the rest of the game
-------------------------------------------
ExecVerb runs before every built-in command (V4Game.Part2.cs:4205-4240), so a
game-scope `verb` shadows the engine's own handling of that word.  This header
declares `verb <take>` and `verb <pick up; take, take>`, which between them
shadow TAKE and PICK UP for the entire game -- and neither does anything but look
up a property on the object.  The result is that almost nothing can actually be
picked up:

    take key                 -> "I got a key!!!"   (properties <take = ...>)
    pick up piece of paper   -> "I now have it."
    take huge map            -> "I now have it.  There is a pen under this map!!!"
    take german coins        -> "You now have them!"

and the inventory stays empty through all of it.  The one exception is the
photograph in Office 2, which carries a real script:

    action <pick up> { give <Picture 2>  msg <I now have it!> }

so Picture 2 is the only object you ever hold.  The winning text -- "Then you put
your hand in your pocket and out tumbles the camera letter and other things you
picked up along your travels..." -- describes an inventory the game cannot give
you.  (Note also that the comma in "pick up; take, take" means the second name
registers as "take, take"; Quest splits that list on semicolons only.  So TAKE is
handled by the separate `verb <take>` further down, and TAKE, TAKE X really does
work, using the "pick up" property.)

Three more verbs are dead the same way -- `verb <talk >`, `verb <talk to >` and
`verb <open >` -- which is why TALK TO GREEN FIGURE falls through to the engine's
own "talk to " handling and reaches his `speak` script instead of a verb default.
And `verb <open left drawer>` needs an object after it to match at all, so OPEN
LEFT DRAWER on its own is handled by plain `verb <open>` against the "Left drawer"
object, whose `action <open>` is what reveals the Letter.  The Big Desk's own
`properties <open left drawer = There is a letter here.>` is unreachable: OPEN
LEFT DRAWER BIG DESK does match, but the desk also carries an `action <open left
drawer>` of the same name, actions are checked first, and that one is a silent
show/reveal pair.

The Administration Building
---------------------------
    Lobby           the Big Desk.  OPEN LEFT DRAWER reveals the Letter; opening
                    the Letter (a `container`) reveals the Note about the showers.
    West Hallway     you trip over the carpet on the way in; PULL CARPET reveals
                    the Key.
    Big Office 1     the Box, "a radio of some sort".  USE BOX plays imperial.wav
                    (the Imperial March -- absent from the .cas, so silent here)
                    and starts a 10-second timer that keeps reporting the music.
                    Its `if is <#quest.currentroom#;Big Office 2>` guard on the
                    30-second stop timer can never be true, since you are
                    standing in Big Office 1 when you press it, so the music
                    never stops.
    Office 2         the Papers, and the photograph of Himmler visiting Dachau.
    Office 5         the note dated June 6 1942 about the new gas chambers.
    Office 1/3/4     "just an empty white room" x2, and the stairs down.
    North Hallway    the locked door.  USE KEY IN CLOSED DOOR opens it; the script
                    also does `create room <Big Office 2>` and unlocks the exit
                    three times over, twice with the same line.
    Big Office 2     the frozen man at the desk and the aerial view of the camp.
    Bunker Room      the map of occupied Germany; TAKE HUGE MAP reveals the Pen,
                    OPEN PEN spills the note saying it was Himmler's, presented
                    on his 1936 visit.
    Open prison cell a frozen prisoner and a copy of Mein Kampf ("EWW! Why would
                    I want that trashy book!").

Outside
-------
Six barracks, three crossroads, the gate, the greenhouse, the gas chamber, the
guard tower.  Points of interest:

    First set        Person 1's tattoo (140603), and the necklace in the guard's
                     pocket with a family photograph in it.
    Barracks 1       the toilet, and Gringarth Schmidt's letter at the bottom of
                     it -- "I am a German political prisoner held in my own
                     country by my own people".  It is `invisible` and nothing
                     ever `show`s it, so you have to know to LOOK AT DIRTY PIECE
                     OF PAPER; the toilet's own description is the only hint.
    Second set       the Camera.  USE CAMERA shows three of the game's photographs
                     in a row.
    Barracks 5       the injured man and the open wound.
    Gas Chamber      PULL LOOSE STONE, then OPEN SMALL BOX for the coins.
    Guard Tower      USE MACHINE GUN is the game's only loss: you hit people below,
                     "thus changing the future and in fact making it so that you
                     were never born."  The script below looks at it and leaves it
                     alone.
    Entrance         the gate is locked and the barbed wire stops you climbing it.
                     There is no way out of the camp; Yoda is the exit.

Loose ends
----------
`Barracks7` is a duplicate of Barracks 1 (same room text, same toilet, same
letter, all the objects re-declared with a "2" suffix and aliased back) whose only
exit leads west to First set of Buildings -- but First set's east exit goes to
Barracks 1, so nothing reaches it.  `command <>` in the header is an empty
declaration QDK emitted.  The four game-scope `action` blocks at the top of
`define game` -- `action <yell "I am in Dachau!"> playerwin`, `action <yell I am
in Dachau>` (which calls playerwin twice), `action <I am in Dachau>` and `action
<say I am in Dachau>` -- are the author's earlier attempts at the win condition;
actions belong to objects, so none of them is reachable.  `verb <speak I am i
Dachau>` is a typo of the winning verb, kept alongside it.

A hundred and four commands.  The marker is from `define text <win>`.

Two things print nothing and do so faithfully, so they are left out of the run:
TAKE PIECE OF PAPER, because a bare `take` line inside an object is filed by Quest
as a *property* named "take" with an empty value (V4Game.Part2.cs:3417-3420) and
then Printed; and LOOK AT PICTURE in Office 2, which is `look picture <...>` with
no text at all.  The latter is also why the aerial photograph has to be examined
in Big Office 2 *before* Office 2's photograph is picked up -- Picture 2 is
`alias <Picture>`, and once it is in the inventory the two are ambiguous.
