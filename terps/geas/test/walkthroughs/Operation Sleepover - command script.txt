Operation: Sleepover (Justin Bailey / Junkosoft, v2.0, ©2001-2005; ASL 350,
`nodebug', start <bed2>) -- an ending reached through a procedure, not
`playerwin'.

Content note, since the abbreviation in the banner is not obvious: this is an
"AR/TG" game, i.e. age-regression and gender-transformation transformation
fiction.  A faery turns the fifteen-year-old narrator into a toddler and then
into a nine-year-old girl, and the goal is to be let into his sister's slumber
party.  It is not pornographic -- there is no sexual content in it at all -- but
it is squarely in that genre and the prose dwells on clothes, cuteness and
embarrassment throughout.  It is here because the machinery underneath it is
unusually interesting, not for the story.

There is no `playerwin', no `playerlose' and no score display.  Every ending in
the game -- and there are several, including two deaths by sticking metal
objects into the electrical outlets -- funnels into `define procedure <thend>',
which walks the inventory building two strings with `$objectproperty()$' and
`$displayname()$', prints "You finished this game as #name[sex]#, a %age% year
old #gender[sex]#.", then "The End", then calls `stop'.  So the runner sees
running=false at the end and the marker has to come out of `thend'.  The marker
used here is "job well done", which is the last line of `thend':

    if property <atari; dead> and got <cookies> then msg <|wCongratulations on
    a job well done. ...  |crSay Justin Bailey|cb in your bedroom next time you
    play.>

That line only prints if the Atari has been played to death *and* the cookies
have been baked, so the marker pins the good ending rather than merely reaching
an ending -- which matters here, because walking into an outlet also reaches
`thend' and would satisfy any marker taken from its earlier lines.

The name prompt is seed-dependent, and this is the one thing to know before
editing this transcript.  `qna.playersetup' calls `qna.randnames', which builds
two twenty-entry string arrays and picks with `$rand(1;20)$' twice: `name[0]'
from the girls' list and `name[1]' from the boys'.  Under --seed 1 those come
out Hannah (female[8]) and Hayden (male[10]).  The party door then does
`enter <planswer>' and compares your answer to `#name[0]#' -- the *girl* name --
so the line "Hannah" in this script is only correct at seed 1.  Answer with the
boy's name and Amy says "this party is for girls only"; answer with anything
else and she says she doesn't know anyone by that name; either way the game does
not end and the following script line is eaten as a command.  (`say justin
bailey' in the bedroom overrides both names to Justin/Bailey, which is the
Metroid password joke the author is named after; it also opens a second game,
on which see below.)

Four script lines are swallowed by `enter' prompts rather than run as commands:
two "yes" answers to the crawlspace's "It's dark down there.  Are you sure?",
the name, and the trivia letter.  That is the whole difference between the 96
lines below and the 92 turns the runner counts.

The three shapes of the game.  Scene 1: a fifteen-year-old boy.  Amisael has
magically locked the bedroom door in the opening scene, and `say open sesame'
unlocks it -- the author's idea of a fair first puzzle, given that the room's
own hint is "I'm not giving you a hint for this room.  It's too easy."  From
there the route is a scavenger hunt for two mixtures: the bottle of lighter
fluid on Mom's dresser plus the lighter from the kitchen junk drawer make a
working lighter, and the orange juice from the dining room plus the vodka in the
fridge make a screwdriver, which the game itself admits "doesn't really make
much sense when you think about it".  Amy is standing in the dining room and
blocks both of its exits until `speak to amy' moves her out (and teleports you
downstairs).  The lighter is what survives the pitch-dark crawlspace: `north'
from the laundry room runs `do <dark>', which is an `enter'-prompted
confirmation followed by `goto <dark>', and `dark' counts turns and feeds you to
the grues on the fifth one.  `use lighter' there is the only way through, and it
lands you in the west crawlspace, where the big cardboard box is `properties
<taped>' and `use scissors on box' does
`for each object in <crawl1> if property <#quest.thing#; boxed> then show' --
one line that empties the whole box at once, revealing the Atari, the cow
jammies, an old skirt and some action figures.  Then `look at vent' in the east
crawlspace, which is gated on `%age%;15' and `%sex%;1', ends scene 1: Amisael
catches you spying and regresses you to a toddler.

Scene 3, the toddler, exists for exactly one reason.  Being small is what makes
the heating vent in your own bedroom reachable: `vent2' is `hidden', and the
only `show <vent2>' in the file is inside bed2's own description, guarded by
`if is <%age%;2>'.  So you cannot pry the vent open as a teenager -- the object
is not there to be prized at.  As a toddler `use screwdriver on vent' does
`create exit north <bed2; bed3>' and `create exit south <bed3; bed2>', and Amy's
bedroom, whose door is locked from the inside and cannot be unlocked from the
hallway, is finally reachable.  Note that the regression ran `returninv', which
sends every non-clothes item in your inventory back to its `homeroom' property,
so the lighter and the scissors are back in the junk drawer -- but the box stays
cut open and the lighter stays `full', which is why the second trip into the
dark only needs `open drawer' and `take lighter'.  Do not touch the knitting
needle or the screwdriver in the living room while small: both have
`use on <outlets>' handlers that fry you and call `thend'.

Scene 4 is the endgame, and taking the crystal ball is the hinge.  Its `take'
script sets age 9 and sex 0, strips everything you were wearing into the
`clothes' holding room, dresses you in a cami and red panties (shir 2, pant 2),
sets `pjlevel' to 1, banishes Amisael, and -- the part that matters for routing
-- does `create exit south <bed3;>' and `create exit north <bed2;>' with *empty*
destinations, sealing the vent passage behind you.  geas drops those exits
entirely (neither room lists them afterwards and both directions answer "You
can't go that way"), which is the intended effect; there is then exactly one way
out of Amy's room, and it is `unlock door', whose script requires `%size%;gt;1'
and so was unavailable to the toddler.  Miss that and the game is unwinnable
with the player standing in a room with no exits.

The party door.  `command <knock>' in the downstairs hallway requires sex 0 and
then runs `do <party>', which after the name check demands `%shir% >= 2',
`%pant% >= 3' and `%pjlevel% >= 2' before Amy will let you in.  This is the QNA
clothing library, and it is the best thing in the game: garments are `type
<clothes>' subtypes carrying `pant' and `shir' layer values (`sleeper' is 4/4,
`dress' 8/8, and so on) plus a `size' property, and `qna.wearable' refuses a
garment whose layer value is `lt=' the total you are already wearing (you would
be putting an underlayer on over an outer one) or whose size falls outside
`%size%'..`%size%+1'.  `qna.agesize' derives `size' from `age', so every
transformation silently resizes the entire wardrobe through `qna.resize'.  The
cow jammies are `size=4' and age 9 gives size 4, so they fit only in this scene;
they are also `properties <ripped>' with an `action <before>' that refuses the
wear outright, and the only cure is `use sewing kit on jammies', which is itself
gated on `%sex%;0' ("Sewing isn't exactly a manly activity").  The sewing kit is
`hidden' under the black sweater in the living room and is revealed by
`take sweater' -- which only works once Mom has been moved out of the room,
which the toddler transformation did.  Wearing them adds their `pjlevel=2', so
pjlevel goes 1 -> 3 and shir/pant go 2 -> 6.  Beware the decoys: the sweater's
own `action <after>' does `set <pjlevel; %pjlevel% - 5>' and the old skirt's
does the same, so a player who dresses up in the things lying around fails Amy's
inspection while wearing more clothes than the route does.  Carrying them is
harmless; wearing them is not.

The trivia question is a non-puzzle wearing a puzzle's clothes.  `party' rolls
`set numeric <trivia; $rand(1;3)$>' twice in a row (the first roll is dead code)
and prints one of three sports questions -- Babe Ruth's statue, the Alamodome,
Terry Bradshaw -- and all three set `answer' to `a', so the letter "a" is
correct whatever the seed does.  Getting it right is what turns you back into a
boy; getting it wrong ends the game as Amy's new little sister.  Both endings
run `thend'.

The cookies are the other half of the marker, and they are only bakeable in
scene 4: `use chocolate chips on flour' checks `if is <%sex%;1>' and refuses
with "You don't know anything about cooking", so the girl gets "Using your
female intuition, you toss together some cookie dough" and the boy gets nothing.
`use dough on oven' bakes them.  Note the kitchen's exit script,
`for each object in <kitchen> move <#quest.thing#; $objectproperty(#quest.thing#;
homeroom)$>' -- everything you leave lying on the kitchen floor is teleported
home the moment you walk east, so the flour and sugar cannot be hoarded.  The
Atari is the last errand: `use atari on television' in the living room runs
`leonpreview', which prints a trailer for an unwritten Junkosoft game and sets
`property <atari; dead>'.

What this route deliberately skips.  `say justin bailey' in the bedroom followed
by `use atari on television' does not print the trailer at all; it starts a
whole second game inside the file (rooms `1'-`7' and `a'-`h', lines 1489-2205,
with its own inventory, its own age counter and its own scoring), and finishing
that one with a perfect 137 is the only thing in the file that ever sets
`property <pocketwatch; magic>'.  Carrying a magic pocketwatch into the party
switches both party endings to much stranger ones -- a body swap with Amy, or
being trapped in Amisael's body -- but it is a second walkthrough's worth of
work for a variant ending, so this transcript takes the plain branch and does
not pick the watch up at all.  Also unvisited: the red swimming trunks, which
`open dresser' produces in scene 4 as another Metroid joke (they are what Samus
wears under the password this author is named after) and which advance `scene'
to 5 without unlocking anything.  This script takes them because they cost one
turn and they prove the scene counter moves; they are still in inventory,
unworn, in the closing summary.

Engine notes.  Nothing here needed a change.  Worth recording anyway: the empty
`create exit <dir> <room;>' form described above; the malformed take script at
line 638, `take if here <mom> (%scene% = 1) then msg <...>', where the author
seems to have meant an `and' between two conditions -- this route never takes
the knitting needle, so what geas makes of it is untested and worth a look if
anyone ever routes through the living room as a toddler; and `properties
<homeroom=...>' being used as a poor man's object-return system, which combines
with `for each object in <inventory>' and `$objectproperty()$' to do everything
the clothing library needs without a single array of object names.

Verified: 92 turns, 96 script lines (four of them eaten by the two darkness
prompts, the name prompt and the trivia prompt), marker "job well done",
seed 1, no --tick; reaches "The End" and the game stops.
