ESPER: The Secret of Drom Bennacht
ESPER, v.1.2, ASL 350, "Created with QDK 3.53 - UNREGISTERED VERSION", !include <Typelib.qlb>

You are an investigator for ESPER -- Extraordinary Supernatural Phenomena
Explored and Revealed -- called to a Scottish castle because the dying patriarch
Angus Winters has been hearing the Bean Sidhe.  By the time the butler lets you
in, the man who hired you has been shot dead in his study, and the case has
turned into a murder mystery with a Lovecraftian basement.  24 rooms, no score,
no inventory puzzles at all: the entire game is a chain of four gates, and it
ends with a four-question viva voce that you either pass or die on.  114 turns
below, taking in every room and every conversation.


THE FOUR GATES

  1. RING DOORBELL on the porch.  The Porch has no north exit until then; the
     command runs `create exit north <Porch; Lobby>`.  Ringing twice gets you a
     scolding from the butler and nothing else.

  2. TAKE WALDEN in the guest room.  This is a room-scope `command`, not an
     object -- the bookshelf's `walden` is TLTscenery and cannot be taken by the
     ordinary verb -- and it does `move <walden; storage>` plus
     `create exit up <Guest Room; Top of North Tower>`.  Jenny tells you about
     it if you ask her about Drom Bennacht.

  3. ZACAR in the music room.  The word comes from the "Words of Power" section
     of the grimoire in the Secret Chamber, and the "Personal Notes" section
     tells you where to use it: Jonathan Winters sealed the chapel stairs and
     parked the phonograph on top of them.  The room command does
     `create exit down <Music Room; Dark Corridor>`.

  4. ZACAR again, once you are sealed in below.  This time it is the game-scope
     `command <zacar>`, which does nothing at all unless the `deathcoming` flag
     is set; with it set it runs `zacargate`, which turns off the death timer,
     makes you effectively invisible, and brings James and Moira in with the
     drugged Jenny to start the ritual.

Everything else in the castle is evidence, not machinery.


THE TWO DEATH TIMERS

Entering the Dark Corridor seals the way back up ("The way is sealed!" is the
`up` exit's whole implementation) and starts `Death`, interval 75, which walks
the robed pair in and shoots you.  Saying ZACAR turns `Death` off and starts
`deathtwo`, interval 90, at which point the ritual completes and the world ends.
Both are generous -- this script reaches the endgame 8 turns after going down --
but they are the only two ways to lose apart from a wrong answer, plus one
trap: ATTACK anything in the Large Room while the ritual is running breaks the
invisibility and loses immediately.


THE VIVA

SPEAK TO ROBED MAN (or the woman -- both `action <speak>` entries are
`do <endgame>`) starts a four-selection interrogation.  Every wrong choice runs
`diedummy`, which shoots you on the spot, so the four answers are the whole
endgame:

  Why must the Elder Gods be called?  -> 3  Because the world is theirs and
                                             they want it back.
  Why did we kill Winters?            -> 2  You considered him a fool and did
                                             not want him to fail to properly
                                             perform the ceremony.
  What was the Banshee?               -> 3  It was the vibration of the old
                                             belfry ropes.
  Who killed Laird Winters?           -> 1  James did it.

Each answer is fairly earned somewhere in the castle: the grimoire's
Introduction and Elder Gods sections for the first, the Ritual section for the
second, TURN CRANK on the roof above Angus' room for the third, and the seal on
James' wardrobe read against the grimoire's "Seals of Power" for the fourth.
None of that is enforced -- the selections do not test a single flag, so a
player who guesses right wins without leaving the guest room -- but the script
below earns them all, which is also the only way to see most of the game.

The one place evidence does change the game is Angus: the crank sets
`flag on <turncrank>`, and his `action <speak>` switches from "Damned...  Bean
Sidhe..." to "Heard... her... again!!!" afterwards.  Worth the walk back
upstairs, which is why the script makes it.

Note also that the Seals of Power section only prints its second paragraph --
the one that names James -- if you have LOOKed AT the seal on his wardrobe
first (`flag <seallook>`).  So do James' room before the tower.


TYPELIB

The game includes Typelib.qlb and tags its objects TLTscenery, TLTactor,
TLTreadable and (for the necklace) TLTshirt, but uses almost none of the
library's machinery: the scenery tag is there to make things unremovable and the
actor tag to make people speakable, both of which the game also spells out by
hand.  The necklace is the one TLTshirt, so WEAR NECKLACE works.


THE EASTER EGG

The necklace under Moira's bed is, by the author's own admission in its examine
text, "not actually good for anything.  It was going to be, but I never wound up
implementing it."  There is a second way to get it: the undocumented game
command BUTTMONKEY, a reward for reading the readme file, which does
`give <Necklace>` with a message about the short story that shipped with the
game.  The script below takes the bed route.


A NOTE ON THE ROOM HEADERS

Several rooms print a truncated location line -- "You are in the", "You are on"
-- with no room name after it.  That is the game, not the interpreter: Quest
appends the room name to an `indescription` only when the line ends with a
colon, and this author ended none of them with one (V4Game.Part2.cs:1783-1802).
The three basement rooms sidestep it by printing `#quest.currentroom#`
themselves inside a `description` block, which is why only they read properly.
