The Pilgrims Progress (TA) -- Jason Dyer, v0.075, QDK Pro 4.0.5 (ASL 400)

Stage 1 of part 1 of a straight retelling of Bunyan, and by the look of the
qdk-notes ("Convert to everyday language & NKJV", "LOOK whenever a new exit is
created") the author stopped there.  You are Christian: you read the Bible in a
field, fail to convince your family, meet Evangelist, lose Pliable in the Slough
of Despond, are talked off the road by Mr Worldly Wiseman, and are put back on it
in time to reach the wicket gate.  Almost every step forward is a `create exit`
fired by an action rather than a door you can find, so the map only opens up in
the order the book does.


There is no win and no score

`define text <win>` and `define text <lose>` are both empty and nothing ever
calls playerwin.  The furthest state is the room `wicket gate`, whose look text
is the ending:

    Over the gate there was written, "Knock, and it shall be opened to you."
    WELL DONE!
    YOU HAVE COMPLETED STAGE 1 OF PART 1 OF THIS ADVENTURE
    LOOk OUT FOR STAGE 2 - COMING SOON ...

so that is the marker.  The `Percent Complete` variable in the status bar is the
game's own progress measure and it only ever reaches 7.5 -- it is set to 5 in the
slough and 7.5 on the high hill, and the two death messages quote it back at you
("You have mastered 7.5 percent of this adventure").  Stage 1 really is one
fortieth of Bunyan.

Two deaths are reachable and both are real `stop`s: sinking in the Slough of
Despond, and walking up into the dark at the top of the high hill without a lit
candle.


The route

READ BOOK in the opening field is the whole first puzzle -- it runs the `Read
Bible` procedure and does `create exit <fields; house>`, so the house does not
exist until you have read.  SPEAK TO WIFE in the house gotos the bedroom; LIE ON
BED (the bed's `action <lie on>`, not SLEEP -- `command <sleep>` answers "Sleep
on what?") passes the night, sets `night over` and `unlocked`, and lets OPEN DOOR
create the exit to the landing.  SPEAK TO WIFE again on the landing -- she is a
separate object, `wife2` with `alias <wife>` -- drops you into the nine-room
field maze.

The maze is the one thing in the game you cannot walk straight through.
Evangelist appears in `fields2` only when

    property <fields3; visited> and ... and property <fields10; visited>
        and got <cry for help> and %first meet evangelist% = 1

i.e. every one of the other eight fields has been entered, you are holding the
`cry for help` from `fields10`, and you have come back to `fields2`.  Every field
is aliased `fields` and looks identical ("You are walking in the fields ..."), so
the exits are the only way to tell them apart:

    fields2  n->3  s->7  ne->9        fields7   n->2  s->8
    fields3  n->4  s->2  e->9         fields8   n->7  s->2  e->10  w->6
    fields4  s->3  sw->5              fields9   e->10 w->3  nw->4  sw->2
    fields5  e->3  w->6  ne->4 se->2  fields10  w->9
    fields6  e->5

The script's N N SW W E SE S S E W SW covers all eight and picks up the two
things worth having on the way: the box of matches in `fields6` and the `cry for
help` in `fields10`.  Take the matches even though nothing asks you to -- see
below.

SPEAK TO EVANGELIST hands over the parchment roll.  Read it with X PARCHMENT
ROLL, not READ PARCHMENT ROLL: the roll's `look` is what sets `flag on <parchment
roll read>`, and the game block's `verb <read #text#> msg <You can't read that.>`
swallows READ before it can reach the object.  (READ BOOK works only because the
book carries an explicit `action <read>`.)  Then SPEAK TO EVANGELIST a second
time for the "keep that light in your eye" speech, which creates the exit from
`fields4` to the wide plain and starts the `flee too slowly` timer -- if that
fires you are dumped back in the bedroom with the candle blown out, so go: N, N,
GO TO WIDE PLAIN, GO TO MIDDLE OF THE PLAIN, GIVE BOOK TO PLIABLE.  Handing
Pliable the book is what turns the timer off, converts him, sends Obstinate home
and opens the way to the slough.

The Slough of Despond is a waiting game.  `command <struggle>` has two halves:
while `%struggle in bog%` (initially 3) is non-zero it just decrements, and only
once that has run out does it check `%sinking% < 8` and let you out.  `sinking`
starts at 10 and is decremented by the `sinking in mire` timer alone, which
starts at interval 15 and shortens itself to 10 when it first fires and to 5 when
Pliable gives up and climbs out on his own side -- so you need three firings
before STRUGGLE will work, and the fourth firing (`case <0>`) is the death.  The
script says STRUGGLE 43 times and the 43rd is the one that gets you out; the
count is exact on purpose, because a spare STRUGGLE anywhere else in the game
with `%sinking% < 8` re-runs the escape branch and teleports you back to the
sound ground.

After that it is a straight line: GO TO CROSSING OF WAYS, SPEAK TO WORLDLY
WISEMAN, TAKE GOOD COUNSEL (the taking is what creates the exit to the high
hill), GO TO HIGH HILL, U, U, TAKE REGRET, then D, D back down -- `high hill`'s
script waits for `got <regret>` before it produces Evangelist, and Evangelist is
what creates the exit to the wicket gate.  SPEAK TO EVANGELIST, GO TO WICKET
GATE.


The candle, the matches and the fourth level of the hill

`high hill4`, one level above where the regret is, carries the qdk-note

    '!qdk-note:  No death if got candle and candle lit. Put matches in field.

and its script kills you outright unless `flag <candle is lit>` and `got
<candle>`.  Nothing in the game hints at this: the candle is a piece of bedroom
scenery you can walk past, taking it explicitly blows it out (`action <take>`
does `flag off <candle is lit>`), and the matches are lying in one field of a
nine-room maze that has no reason to be searched except that Evangelist will not
appear until you have.  The author's note is the only reason to connect them.
The wicket gate is reachable without ever going up there, so the script climbs to
`high hill4` with the candle lit purely to prove the survival branch works -- it
creates an east exit back to `high hill2` and a down exit, and prints "You temble
and quake exceedingly with fear ...".


What this game needed from the engine

geas did not implement the `visited` room property at all.  Quest's PlayGame
stamps every room it enters with it (V4Game.Part2.cs:6688-6708), and this game
tests it in two places -- so without the stamp Evangelist never appears in
`fields2` and stage 1 cannot be finished.  Which side of the room's own entry
script the stamp lands on is version-dependent, and that turns out to matter
here:

    if ((ASLVersion >= 391) & (ASLVersion < 410)) AddToObjectProperties("visited", ...)
    await ShowRoomInfo(room, ctx)
    ... run the room's script ...
    if (ASLVersion >= 410) AddToObjectProperties("visited", ...)

At ASL 400, as here, a room is already `visited` by the time its own script runs.
`middle of the plain` is written as

    script if property <middle of the plain; visited> then  else msg <The
        neighbors also came out to see you flee ...>

which is the usual "first visit only" idiom -- and at 400 it can never fire, so
that paragraph of Bunyan is simply never printed.  Under 410 it would.  Quest
evidently moved the stamp for exactly this reason.  fixtures/visited.asl and
fixtures/visited410.asl pin both halves down.


Odds and ends

  * The intro appears twice.  The startscript is `clear / displaytext <intro> /
    pause <2000> / clear`, and Quest then displays the intro section again by
    itself unless a startscript says `nointro` (V4Game.Part2.cs:7987).  In the
    real player the two `clear`s wipe the first copy off the screen; in a
    transcript both survive.
  * `define text <bible text 4>` is dead.  The `Read Bible` procedure only
    switches on cases 1, 2 and 3, and the wicket gate sets `bible text` to 4, so
    the "Enter by the narrow gate" verse the ending is written up to can never be
    read.  READ BOOK there falls through to `case else` -- "You read the book."
  * Every room from `sound ground` on says "You are at the high hill" whatever
    level of it you are on: `high hill2`, `high hill3` and `high hill4` all carry
    `alias <high hill>`, exactly as the nine fields all say "fields".
  * `command <help>` is room-sensitive and worth typing in the two places it has
    something to say: the slough ("You will need to struggle hard ...") and the
    middle of the plain ("Pliable might be interested in the book.").
  * The `time passes` timer is switched on again by `afterturn` every single
    turn, so its "Time passes ..." fires every 200 turns for the rest of the
    game.  It does nothing else.
  * The burden never comes off -- `drop nowhere <You cannot seem to drop the
    great burden from your back.>`.  In Bunyan it falls off at the cross, which
    would have been stage 2.
