All the news that's fit to print -- anonymous, QDK 4.1.4 (ASL 410)

A journalism-simulation sketch about the November 2011 pepper-spraying of student
protesters at UC Davis, lightly renamed "UC Cashell".  You are a new reporter at
the Daily Objective and every room is a menu: the Lobby (displayed as
"Introduction") sends you to The Field, and The Field is a hub of seven
categories -- Administration, Police, Protestors, Students, Business Sector,
Activist organizations, Professors -- plus the editor's desk.  Interviews are
SPEAK TO commands whose scripts are chains of `if ask <...>`, so playing it is a
series of Yes/No prompts, and every answer worth having ends in `give <some
quote>`: the quotes are objects parked in a room called `hidden` and moved into
your inventory as you collect them.


There is no win

`define room <the end>` is empty -- no look text, no script, no playerwin -- so
the furthest state the game has is standing in it, which prints the default "You
are in the end" and nothing else.  The script below stops there, and that line is
the marker.

The editor's desk explains a mechanic that was never written:

    [Now that you have your raw material for your article, here's where you put
    them into paragraphs.]  ...  [Put X in Paragraph Y, for paragraphs one to six]

There are no paragraph objects anywhere in the file and no `put` handling beyond
whatever stdverbs.lib refuses by default, so the inverted-pyramid editing the
whole game is built to set up cannot be done.  The turn limit went the same way.
`define game` declares

    define variable <Inventory space>
        type numeric
        value <8>
        display <Turns left>

which nothing ever decrements, and the author says so in the opening text: "[Note:
Turns were not implemented properly.  When you have 8 items in your inventory,
'go to editor' from the field.]"  In practice the editor is reachable from The
Field from the first turn, whatever you are carrying, because it is just another
`place`.


What the script does

Interviews everyone in all seven categories -- 24 SPEAK TO commands, 32 quotes --
and then walks to the editor and into the end.  Answering every prompt Yes gets
every quote, with two exceptions worth knowing about:

  * The Professor of Communications studies is a joke at the reporter's expense:

        speak if not ask <Soldier, you're setting foot behind enemy lines. Turn
        back?> then give <comm prof>

    You have to answer *No* to get the interview, so that one prompt is answered
    2 where every other is 1.

  * SPEAK TO FRINGE prints nothing at all.  Its whole definition is `speak give
    <marcus protest>` -- the item arrives, there is no text, and the turn looks
    empty.  That is the game, not the interpreter.

Two authoring slips show up in the collected inventory.  Shawna Stone's
pepper-spray answer in the Police category ends `give <chancellor pepper spray>`
instead of `give <shawna pepper spray>`, so her quote is filed under the
Chancellor and `shawna pepper spray` -- which is defined in `hidden` -- is
unreachable.  And several branches carry a stray `give <>` with no parameter
(three of them in the President's and the Chief's scripts), which does nothing.

The one interview that hands over nothing on purpose is the student far away from
the event, whose two answers are the point ("I have no idea what the protest is
about", "Well, he must have done something").

Objects are addressed by their internal names, which are usually shorter than the
labels the menus print: "Cellphones", "on campus", "far away", "Fringe",
"Debt free", "Business professionals", "Communications studies".  Abbreviations
are on (`define options / abbreviations on`), so GO TO BUSINESS SECTOR and GO TO
ACTIVIST ORGANIZATIONS work as written.
