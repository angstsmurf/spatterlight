On the far blue -- Odeon, v1.0, ASL 410 (QDK 4.1.5)
===================================================
distributed as "On the far blue.cas"

A shipwreck survival game, and the only thing in the Quest 4 corpus so far that is
timed from the first turn to the last.  You wake in a locked sailor's cabin with
five minutes before the ship goes down, and every stage after that has its own
clock: two minutes to seal yourself into a beer barrel, two minutes on a raft with
a shark that keeps coming back, forty-five seconds in a forest with the man who
sank your ship.  Eighteen timers, `a` through `r`, do all of it.

Because of that this is the first script in the suite that needs the runner's
--tick, which calls tick_timers() once per turn -- one second per turn.  Every
number below is therefore a turn count, and the raft section in particular is
arithmetic: the commands have to land on exact turns or a timer kills you.

The clocks
----------
    a-e   1,60,120,180,240   the countdown announcements on the ship
    f     300                the ship sinks and you drown
    g     120                you failed to secure the barrel lid and drown
    h     300                +1 Hunger, then re-arms itself
    i     10                 a shark surfaces; arms j
    j     25                 the shark pulls you off the raft
    k     10                 re-arms i (this is what makes the shark recur)
    l     120                "You reached land!" -- the raft crossing is over
    m     50                 the raft snags on a rock; arms n
    n     20                 the sharks roll the raft
    o,p,q 10,15,15           Mad Simon taunting you from cover; q reveals him
    r     5                  Mad Simon kills you

The startscript arms a-f and h.  `jump off board` turns a-f off and arms g;
securing the lid turns g off.  h is armed for the whole game and never turned off,
but Hunger only kills at `gt=;11` -- 3300 turns -- so the seagull eggs on Southern
rock island are a mechanic with nothing to do.  A full run is 233 turns, so Hunger
never even reaches 1.

The ship: two words beginning with C
------------------------------------
LOOK AT PILLOW says to look under it, `verb <look under>` is declared in the header
with no message at all, and the Pillow carries `action <look under>` -- so LOOK
UNDER PILLOW is the cabin key, and USE CABIN KEY ON CABIN DOOR is the way out.
(The bare verb declaration means LOOK UNDER anything else prints nothing, which is
faithful: with no script after the `>`, ExecVerb runs ExecuteScript("") --
V4Game.cs:3060-3066.)

Old Sam on the upperdeck found a key and put it in the captain's cabin; Steve on
the lowerdeck lost the Galley keys up there.  Between them that is the whole
sequence, and the captain's cabin door is the one puzzle in the game:

    First : what glow's in the dark but is often dark when it glow's outside?
    And the second : What animal is men's best friend? You will need to use his
    first latin name.
    Both words begin with the letter ''C''

candle and Canis.  What the door does not say is that they are not two answers but
one command.  The header declares `verb <candle>`, and the lowerdeck holds an
invisible object called `Canis` whose only job is to carry `action <candle>`, so
the door opens to CANDLE CANIS -- verb plus noun, in that order, with no prompt of
any kind asking for either.  Sam's second line, "No! the light from the candle is
out!", is the hint for the first word; the candle itself is an ordinary scenery
object on the upperdeck.

Canis also carries `action <lamp>` with the same goto, left over from when the verb
was called `lamp`.  The rename was only half done -- the verb's default message is
still "You can't lamp that." -- and since no `verb <lamp>` survives, LAMP CANIS is
"I don't understand your command."  Two spellings of the answer, one of them dead.

Then USE GALLEY KEY ON GALLEY DOOR, TAKE BEER BARREL (which `show`s the otherwise
hidden, invisible `board`), and JUMP OFF BOARD.

The barrel: two keys through the lid
------------------------------------
TAKE METAL RING, USE METAL RING ON LID -- which swaps both objects for a third,
"Lid with metal ring" -- then USE CABIN KEY ON LID WITH METAL RING and USE GALLEY
KEY ON LID WITH METAL RING.  Each `inc <key's attached; 1>`, and the variable's
onchange fires at 2: you sleep, the barrel beaches, and `goto <Northen rock
island>`.  Both keys are consumed (hidden), which is fine -- nothing needs them
again -- and the door they came from is gone with the ship.

The rock island: build an axe
-----------------------------
The only untimed part of the game.  Five rooms around Middle Rock island.

    Western    Sam's body, and the invisible Dagger at his waist.  TAKE DAGGER,
               TAKE PIECE OF SMALL ROPE.
    Southern   the seagulls.  TAKE SEAGULL EGG, then USE YOURSELF ON SEAGULL EGG
               eats it -- `Yourself` is a real object, given to you by the
               startscript, and "You can eat thing's by using yourself on it."
    Middle     the giant rock with a badger's cave under it.  USE DAGGER ON CAVE
               collapses the ground, rolls the rock east, and stocks the eastern
               area with wood and stone.
    Eastern    TAKE BRANCH, USE DAGGER ON BRANCH -> Stick; TAKE ROCK; USE STICK ON
               ROCK -> Semi axe; USE PIECE OF SMALL ROPE ON SEMI AXE -> Axe.
               Then USE AXE ON SMALL FOREST -> Poles.
    Northen    USE DAGGER ON ROPE STUCK IN THE SAND reveals the Rope; TAKE ROPE.

Back east, USE ROPE ON POLES makes the Wooden raft and shows an invisible object
called `sail` whose `action <Set>` is the launch, so the command is SET SAIL
(`verb <Set>`).  The raft is `show`n but never `give`n, so it stays in the room --
which is correct, you are pushing it into the water from there.

Three objects in this game cannot be addressed at all, because their names contain
a Windows-1252 curly apostrophe that no player can type: `Piece's of the broken
rock` and `Broken tree's` in the eastern area, and `Seagull nest's` in the south.
All three are listed in the room description, all three answer "You don't see any
...", and one of them -- the nests -- has the only joke about killing the birds
attached to it.  They are left out of the script.  (Everything else in the file
uses a plain ASCII apostrophe, including `Pile of large beer barrel's` and
`Captain's table`, both of which work.)

The raft: 120 turns, and the shark cycle
----------------------------------------
Entering "On the raft" arms i, l and m.  Counting turns from SET SAIL:

    T+10   the shark surfaces, j armed (25 turns to live)
    T+11   USE DAGGER ON SHARK    -> Wounded shark, j re-armed, i off
    T+12   USE AXE ON WOUNDED SHARK -> shark dead, i and j off, k armed
    T+23   k fires and re-arms i
    T+33   the shark is back

so the period is 21 turns from one killing blow to the next surfacing, and the
whole crossing costs five sharks.  The dagger alone is not enough (the wounded
shark ignores it) and the axe alone is not enough (the healthy one dodges it): it
has to be dagger then axe, in that order, both within j's 25 turns.

Layered on top of that, m fires at T+50 and snags the raft on a rock with n armed
for 20 turns; USE AXE ON SEA ROCK at T+51 clears it and turns m off for good, so
it happens only once.  The script therefore reads: ten waits, kill, twenty-one
waits, kill, fifteen waits, free the raft, five waits, kill, twenty-one waits,
kill, twenty-one waits, kill, sixteen waits -- and l fires on turn 120 exactly,
which is why the last shark of the crossing is killed on turns 119 and 120 and
never gets a sixth appearance.  `lib command <wait> msg <Time passes.>` is the
filler; the run uses 105 of them.

Timer l's action turns off i, j, k, m and n (twice over, in the author's usual
style), which matters: without it the shark timer would follow you ashore.

The forest: forty-nine seconds
------------------------------
The beach is one room with one exit.  Going north into The forest runs a script
that arms o and `destroy exit <The forest; Eastern beach of forest island >` --
you cannot go back.  o(10), p(15) and q(15) chain one into the next, and every
step costs interval + 1 turns because Quest's SetTimerState only flips TimerActive
and raises BypassThisTurn, never resetting TimerTicks (V4Game.Part2.cs:561-574):
o at +11 and p at +27 are taunts, q at +43 shows Mad Simon and arms r(5), and r
kills you at +49.  So there are exactly five turns to act, and
again it is dagger then axe: USE DAGGER ON MAD SIMON turns r off and leaves
Wounded Simon, USE AXE ON WOUNDED SIMON does `create exit <The forest; Village>`,
and GO TO VILLAGE is the win.  Simon was the madman laughing at you on the
upperdeck five minutes and two hundred turns ago; the game never explains why he
is here, or why he wanted the ship sunk.

Do NOT take the rock
--------------------
On Eastern rock island the axe is built by USE STICK ON ROCK, and the script that
does it is `use <Stick>` on the object `Rock` -- so Rock has to be the thing the
*second* blank resolves to.  ExecUse resolves that blank against the current room
first and only falls back to the inventory on a miss (V4Game.Part2.cs:5376-5390),
and the same room also holds `Piece's of the broken rock', whose name ends in the
word "rock".  So the moment you TAKE ROCK -- whose script `give`s it, moving it to
the inventory -- USE STICK ON ROCK stops finding Rock in the room, the ASL >= 391
abbreviation pass matches "rock" against "Piece's of the broken rock" instead
(Disambiguate, V4Game.cs:4730-4767; this game declares `abbreviations on'), and the
axe can never be built.  Leave the rock lying where the giant rock dropped it: the
item half of USE has to be held, the target half does not.

Notes on the verbs
------------------
The header declares `verb <take> msg <You can't take that.>`, and a game-scope verb
runs ahead of every built-in command (V4Game.Part2.cs:4205-4240), so TAKE is
handled by that verb for the whole game -- ExecTake is never reached.  In practice
this changes nothing, because a `take { ... }` block inside an object is filed as
an *action* named "take" (V4Game.Part2.cs:3438) and ExecVerb finds actions first,
so TAKE DAGGER and TAKE BRANCH still run their scripts.  It does mean that TAKE
anything-else gives the verb's message rather than the engine's, and that the
several `take` scripts which are just `reveal`/`show`/`give` with no `msg` print
nothing at all -- TAKE ROPE and TAKE METAL RING are both silent, and both are
faithful, since a script-type take runs the script and nothing else
(V4Game.Part2.cs:5212-5215).

Half of the author's `take` scripts do their reveal/show/give pair twice in a row,
and the Rock and the branch do it twice with the message in the middle.  It is
harmless -- giving an object you already hold is a no-op -- and it is the clearest
sign in the file of someone clicking "add line" in QDK until it worked.

`verb <eat>`, `verb <use seagull egg>` and `verb <enter>` exist only as defaults;
nothing carries a matching property.  `define selection <Enter cabin>` at the
bottom of the file -- "Enter captain cabin?", yes/no, with the yes branch doing the
`goto <Captain cabin>` the riddle now does -- is never shown by anything, an
earlier and much kinder version of the door puzzle.

232 turns.  The marker is the Village room script's win text.
