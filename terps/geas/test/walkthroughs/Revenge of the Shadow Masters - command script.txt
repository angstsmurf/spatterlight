Revenge of the Shadow Masters (Slackers Inc., "Demo 0.5.2", 2010; QDK Pro 4.1.3,
ASL 410) -- furthest-reachable-state pin, not a win.

There is no `playerwin' anywhere in this game and no scoring: it is the opening
two levels of a planned sequel to something called The Quest to Find the Dark
Hills, released as a demo and never finished.  The demo announces its own end in
the Refuge of Riddles, whose room script prints "You have reached the end of this
version. Please give us feedback..." -- but three more rooms exist south of it
and are reachable, so the marker for this walkthrough is the description of the
last one, Abyss of the Wise3: "Coming up on a stream, you see what you believe to
be a dwarf."  Beyond that the file stops being a game: `Abyss of the Wise4', `5'
and `6' are defined, with their own scenery and their own characters (Ike, a
blacksmith who looks like Mortimer), but they have no exits at all and nothing
anywhere links to them, so no sequence of commands can enter them.  The Refuge's
other exit, northeast to `Mine of the Crystal', names a room that was never
written.  So the transcript this script produces is the whole of the playable
game.

The shape of it.  You start in a cave on the wrong side of the Dark Hills with a
towel, an axe, a shield, an amulet and a rock.  Moving the rock uncovers your
backpack, and *taking* the backpack is what unlocks the cave's northeast exit
(the `gain' script does `unlock <The Cave; northeast>'), so the game cannot be
left until you have it.  The backpack is a container and has to be opened before
its pocket knife and first aid kit can be taken -- both of them, plus the shield,
are the three gifts the tribal leader asks for at the very end of the demo, so
they have to be carried the whole way.  `look at bush' on the Path to Village is
the one piece of scenery you must poke: it hands over the crowbar and a
twenty-dollar bill, and you need both.  Money starts at $30 and the run spends
$5 on a mela oak leaf, $5 on a piece of mirror, $5 on a mug o' grog and $15 on a
hotel room, so the extra $20 from the bush is not optional.

The middle of the game is the Drink of Immortality.  In the hotel room, `look at
bed' puts a heavy book where you can read it -- it is "The Manual of
Immortality", and reading it gives the recipe: a mela oak leaf and a pinch of
salt into a mug of grog.  The leaf comes from Pomona's Plants and Petals (`buy
mela oak'), the grog from The Conjurer's Bar (`buy mug o' grog'), and the salt is
lying loose in Casa De Comida.  Use the salt on the mug and then the leaf on the
mug -- the second `use' is what produces the drink -- and the drink's `gain'
script is what unlocks the Town of Nevanwatts's southeast exit to the Refuge of
Riddles, so merely *holding* it is enough.  Do not drink it: that starts a chain
of three timers that ends with "Oh no, I'm back at the begining!!", hides your
shield and axe and sends you back to the cave.  Do not skip anything else: the
two locked
exits below the Refuge are opened by conversation, `speak to lucy' then answer 3,
and `speak to mortimer' then answer 3, and each of the three "I still think there
is something I need to do before I go that way" messages is a separate gate.
The last one is the tribal leader: answer 3 to hear the request, hand over the
pocket knife, the first aid kit and the shield, then speak to him again and he
gives you the sacred fire and unlocks the way south to the dwarf.

The hotel room key comes from a timer, and this is the one thing about this game
that needs a runner flag.  `speak to thomas' at the Holistic Resort desk starts
`define timer <get a room>' with `interval <5>', and the key only arrives when
that timer fires five turns later.  This script takes the key and uses it on the
hotel's front door and on room 115, so it must be run with --tick or the clerk
never comes back and both doors stay shut.  A crowbar-only route is possible --
the crowbar has `use on' handlers for all three doors, each guarded by
`if got <Room Key>' so it refuses once you have the key -- but the timer is the
more interesting thing to exercise, and this script uses the crowbar for the
men's restroom (before the key arrives) and the key for the two hotel doors, so
one transcript covers both paths.  The walk down to the restroom and back is what
gives the five turns time to elapse.

Menus.  Six conversations here `choose' a text menu, and in geas as in Quest a
pending menu eats the next line of input as its answer, so every one of them is
followed by an explicit number: Damien takes two answers in a row (1 then 4),
Thomas 3, Ed 3, Lucy 3, Mortimer 3, and the tribal leader 3.  `read book' in the
hotel room is a *disambiguation* rather than a conversation -- the towel book
from the backpack is still in inventory and both objects answer to "book" -- so
it needs an answer too, 2 for the Manual.  Getting any of these wrong does not
produce an error message; it silently spends the following command as a menu
answer, and the route then quietly falls apart several turns later.

Engine note.  This game is the reason geas now distinguishes Quest's two
spellings of `hidden'.  The Mug o' Grog is declared `properties <hidden; Drink;
buy; steal>' with no bare `hidden' line and nothing in the game ever calls `show'
on it, and Quest's object loader undoes a load-time hidden that arrived through a
properties line -- `if (!hidden) o.Exists = true' at the end of the definition
loop, V4Game.Part2.cs:3550-3553 -- so in Quest the grog is on sale from turn one.
geas used to hide it, which made the drink unmakeable and the whole second half
of the demo unreachable.  Seven objects here use the properties spelling and are
now visible from the start, which is faithful but does look odd in places (the
map behind the picture frame, the Post-it, the leaf).  The Back Pack is the
instructive one: its author wrote *both* a bare `hidden' tag and
`properties <hidden>', and since the bare tag is what raises the loader's flag it
stays hidden -- which is why the cave listing is unchanged and `move rock' still
has something to reveal.  fixtures/hiddenprops.asl pins all four combinations.

Other quirks worth knowing.  Several rooms `clear' the screen in their
description, so a transcript of this game is mostly single screens.  The game
defines a `command <end game>' that just starts the credits timer, and a
`0 money' cheat command; neither is used here.  The typos in the transcript
("wrecked havoc", "fifteen dollas", "remodling", "usful") are the author's.  The
`Amulet' disappears from the cave listing after the first look because the
opening script takes it.  Casa De Comida claims to be closed for remodelling from
the town description but is enterable anyway, which is how the salt is reachable.

Verified: 78 turns, 86 script lines, marker "you see what you believe to be a
dwarf", seed 1, run with --tick.  No win: there is none in the file.
