Londe Perplex -- Kirk Gough, v1.0, ASL 410 (QDK 4.1.4)
======================================================

Seven rooms of second-person mysticism in which a chorus of unseen "guardians"
narrates your progress through the powers of the mind.  Mechanically it is a
chain of five custom verbs and one selection menu, and every room description
ends with a red line telling you which verbs it will answer:

    Ballroom        You may PLAY, you may FEEL, you may ATTACK.
    Mind corridor   You may KILL. You may LOVE.
    West War        You may ATTACK.
    Maze            You may OPEN, CLOSE, DESTROY, ATTACK, EMBRACE.
    Forest          You may ATTACK.
    East Evermore   You may ATTACK, you may DANCE, you may JUMP.
    Edge            !mih eerf yam uoY

That last one is not a typo.  The Edge's description is `|jc` (centred) and then
switches to `|jl` for a line printed backwards -- "You may free him!" -- which is
the only place the game tells you the verb that wins it.

Two of those hints are lethal.  The Forest says "There are three Freighters here
and we need you to attack them", and all three freighters answer
`action <Attack> playerlose`.  East Evermore says "You may ATTACK" above a
paragraph begging you not to, and the Bollbellians have `look playerlose`,
`speak playerlose` and an attack script that also loses.  JUMP there is not a
death but a quit: its script ends in `stop`, so you walk out of Londe and the
game simply stops.

The route
---------
    Ballroom -> Mind corridor -> (West War) -> Maze -> Forest -> Mind corridor
    -> East Evermore -> Edge

    ATTACK SCEPTRES                 the sceptres are `invisible`, so nothing in
                                    the Ballroom listing hints at them; punching
                                    them does `give <Pain>`
    TAKE PIANO KEY
    N, LOVE INSANITY                `action <love>` does `give <Rage>`
    TAKE INSANITY
    W, USE RAGE ON WARRIOR, E       optional -- see below
    N, SPEAK TO RIDDLER             the riddle: "a frantic creature like you
                                    embraces his disease"
    TAKE BROKEN TILE
    USE INSANITY ON DOOR            `create exit north <Maze; Forest>` and
                                    `unlock <Maze; north>`
    N, SPEAK TO GUARDIANS           the Discus (see below)
    USE DISCUS ON FREIGHTER 1/2/3
    S, S, E
    USE DISCUS ON BOLLBELLIANS      `hide <Bollbellians>` and
                                    `unlock <East Evermore; east>`
    E, FREE OMENGUARDIAN            opens `define selection <OMENGUARDIAN menu>`
    5                               "Use Piano Key on the OMENGUARDIAN":
                                    `hide <OMENGUARDIAN>` then `playerwin`

The Discus, and why this game needed engine work
------------------------------------------------
Speaking to the guardians is the one puzzle with real machinery behind it:

    define object <Personified Sacred Guardians>
        alt <guardians>
        speak {
            add <Discus; Magic Box>
            msg <...They show you Discus...The magic box has been opened.>
            background <white>
            open <Magic Box>
            property <Discus; take=You may now take the Discus...>
            give <Discus>
        }

    define object <Discus>
        take msg <You cannot take it just yet.>
        parent <Magic Box>

`add <child; parent>` and `open <container>` are Quest *statements* -- ASL >= 3.91
script commands, distinct from the ADD and OPEN verbs, silent, and with no
container check of any kind (V4Game.Part2.cs:5900-5915).  geas had neither, so
the two lines were logged as unrecognised script and the Discus stayed sealed in
the container it was declared inside for the whole game: SPEAK TO GUARDIANS
printed its paragraph and then "You don't see any discus" was the answer to
every Discus command, which left the Bollbellians' exit locked and the game
unwinnable.  Both statements (plus `remove` and `close`) are implemented now;
test/fixtures/addremove.asl and addremove391.asl pin them, including the 4.10-only
rule that `add` also marks the parent "seen" without which the contents stay
invisible.

Note that the Magic Box is `container` with an `open <It cannot be opened. You
can barely see it in the mist.>` message override, so OPEN MAGIC BOX by hand can
never work -- the script statement is the only thing that opens it.

The door in the Maze is the same trick in reverse.  It is a container whose
`open` line is "The door is locked. Speak to the Riddler.", and USE INSANITY ON
DOOR does not touch it: it rewrites that message to "You have opened the door
with insanity. Well done." and separately unlocks the *room exit*.  So OPEN DOOR
congratulates you and does nothing, before and after, and the north exit is what
actually changes.  (The script's `create exit north <Maze; Forest>` is redundant
beside the `north locked <Forest>` the room already declares.)

Optional to a fault
-------------------
Almost none of the above is load-bearing.  Verified minimal wins:

  * 15 turns.  The Freighters guard nothing -- the Forest's only exit is the
    south one you came in by -- so all three USE DISCUS commands can be dropped.
    Pain and Rage are ordinary visible room objects with a bare `take` sitting in
    plain sight before you earn them, so TAKE PAIN and TAKE RAGE replace ATTACK
    SCEPTRES and LOVE INSANITY outright.

  * 12 turns.  The menu's choice scripts carry no `got` guards whatsoever, so
    "Use Piano Key on the OMENGUARDIAN" wins whether or not you ever picked the
    piano key up.  N, TAKE INSANITY, N, USE INSANITY ON DOOR, N, SPEAK TO
    GUARDIANS, S, S, E, USE DISCUS ON BOLLBELLIANS, E, FREE OMENGUARDIAN, 5.

The West War is a whole room of that kind: the Warrior is `use on <Warrior >` --
with the trailing space the object name carries -- and destroying him sets no
flag, unlocks no exit and is never mentioned again.  The Broken Tile is menu
choice 1, which fails.  Both are in the script below because they are plainly the
intended route.

The menu
--------
Six choices against the OMENGUARDIAN, and FREE OMENGUARDIAN can be repeated:

    1  Broken Tile   fails, and `hide <Discus>`
    2  Insanity      fails, and hides Pain, Discus and the Piano key
    3  Discus        playerlose
    4  Rage          playerlose
    5  Piano Key     hide <OMENGUARDIAN>, playerwin
    6  Pain          fails, and hides the Piano key, Insanity, Rage and Discus

Choices 1, 2 and 6 read as soft-locks -- they strip the items the other choices
name -- but since nothing is guarded, choice 5 still wins after any of them.
The only unrecoverable answers are 3 and 4.

Loose ends: the Statues in the Maze threaten that speaking to them will make you
"die in desperation here" and then do nothing at all; the Dance in the Ballroom
is `invisible` and reachable only by LOOK AT DANCE; and the Discus's
Bollbellians script contains `create exit east <; >`, a create-exit with both
parameters empty, which is ignored.
