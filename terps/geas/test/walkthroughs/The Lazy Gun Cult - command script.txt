The Lazy Gun Cult (part one) -- Felic Roman, 2005 (lazy gun cult part one.asl, ASL 350)

A proper puzzle game, and the first one in this batch: 20 rooms of a country
mansion, a ritual to assemble, and an ending in which you become the statue you
have spent the game feeding.  You are a paranormal investigator who got an
anonymous tip and let himself in; the front door shuts fast behind you on turn
one.  Part two "will be finished soon" and never was.


There is no playerwin

The ritual moves you to `define room <The Message>`, whose look text is the whole
ending:

    This is the message.

    You have completed part one of 'The Lazy Gun Cult'.  Part two begins where
    you left off, as the statue in the ritual room.  It will be finished soon.

That is the marker.  There is no `playerlose` either -- the two ways to die both
just print "You have died.  Type RESTART to begin again from the start, or LOAD
to play from a saved point." and hand the turn back, so a game where you have
drunk the poison early carries on with the bottle gone and the ritual now
impossible.  `define text <win>` and `define text <lose>` are both empty.


The ritual

The statue in the ritual room wants four artifacts, each of them marked with a
red K, and each of them behind a puzzle:

  * the foreign coin -- TAKE COIN in front of the blue painting, plucked off the
    necklace of the woman in it.  The room swaps the painting for `blue painting
    2`, identical except that the necklace now has "a black indentation".
  * your own hand -- the bronze hand in the Display Room sits under a glass
    case.  Smash the case with the hammer (hidden on the corpse at the top of
    the stairs, found by examining it) and then TAKE BRONZE HAND: the pillar
    drops it away and a hatchet on a wire takes your hand off at the wrist.  From
    that turn on the game's `afterturn` prints "The stump of your arm is bleeding
    profusely..." in yellow, for the rest of the game, including after the
    ending.
  * the ceremonial arrow -- in the workdesk drawer in the hidden workroom, which
    needs the small key.
  * the Inca head-dress, completed with the pewter feather -- USE FEATHER ON
    HEAD-DRESS at the end of the eastern corridor.

Then PRESS BUTTON opens the statue's mouth and drops a living heart on the
ground.  USE OIL ON HEART (the cask is on the plinth in the eastern corridor),
USE MATCHES ON HEART, and finally DRINK BOTTLE -- the poison from the workroom,
whose label reads "lethal if all artifacts are not activated" and whose
accompanying note explains that the soul transfer needs fire.  Drinking it
anywhere else, or here before the heart is burning, is the death that isn't a
death.

The hidden half of the map is entered from the staircase: X ALCOVE on the
mid-landing to find the lever, PULL LEVER to open a hatch, ENTER HATCH, then up a
ladder to a venting tube and south to the workroom.  The small key is not there
-- it comes from the lion's-head bannister at the foot of the stairs, TWIST HEAD,
which drops it out of the lion's mouth.  Going DOWN from the workroom is a chute
into the Control Room, a booth the size of a phone box with two levers and a
peephole: PULL SHORT LEVER rotates the code wheel and the peephole then shows the
painted woman's eyes replaced by two silver numbers, 5 and 7.  That is the
combination for the typewriter bolted to the wall of the Large Antechamber:
TYPE '57', with the quotes, because the command is `command <type '57'>`.  PULL
LONG LEVER is the only way out of the Control Room and it drops you back down by
the paintings.

The route below walks all of that in one pass, and examines things along the way
-- the guest book in the entrance hall (whose page lists four cult members, one
signature unreadable and annotated "(whisper words)"), the note, the bottle's
label, the coin, the arrow, and the hand, which has a K coagulated on its palm.


Dead content

`define room <Ritual Room (statue'd)>` is written and unreachable.  It is the
scene the ending describes -- looking out from inside the statue at moonlight,
your own body sprawled in front of you clutching the bottle -- but the ritual
`goto`s The Message directly and nothing ever goes there.  `define text
<describe>` is likewise unused: the game overrides room display with its own
`description` script in `define game`, which is also why every room prints its
parts in a fixed order.  The win sequence contains a stray `show <small key>`,
re-showing a key that has been in your pocket for fifty turns.
