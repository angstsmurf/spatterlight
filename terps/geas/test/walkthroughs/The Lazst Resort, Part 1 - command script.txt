The Lazst Resort, Part 1 -- B.J. Best (Quest 3.11, asl-version 311)

A murder-mystery weekend at a decaying lakeside hotel that never quite made it
out of the 1970s.  You check in, you poke at everything the resort still has
working, and eventually you find Ray Lazst -- the proprietor and the man who
organised the murder mystery -- genuinely dead in the walk-in freezer.  Part 1
ends there; the author never wrote Part 2.

Scoring is out of 125 and the win text prints a rank drawn from a ladder, of
which only the top rung -- "Perfectionist" -- is reachable at exactly 125.  That
makes it an unusually strict win marker: this script cannot drift a single point
without failing.  The 125 comes from twenty separate awards, most of them for
merely *using* a piece of hotel equipment (the telephone +3, the toilet +2, the
shower +1, the clock radio +2, the ballroom microphone +1, the lobby's Chicago
Manual of Style +1, the lamppost +1, the jukebox +2, the piano +2, the cocktail
+1, the Diet Pepsi +3, opening the can +2) plus the real puzzles: the
housekeeping padlock +5, finding the corpse +20, the string-and-toilet-roll dumb
waiter +10, driving the car +1, the compass +10, the island keyboard +15, and
retrieving the office key +5, with +38 for the ending itself.  Nothing in the
game is optional if you want the rank, which is why the script visits every
bathroom fixture in the place.

Two of the puzzle answers do not exist in the story file as text at all.  They
are in the .jpg files the game shows with `picture`, and the ASL only contains
the bare numeric and string comparisons the images are supposed to teach you:

  * The housekeeping-closet padlock (`use lock`, three `enter` prompts) wants
    8, 20, 6 -- the dartboard in the bar, `dartboard.jpg`.
  * The island shed's keyboard (`use keyboard`, one `enter` prompt) wants
    "cion" -- the kids' crossword on page B-4 of the newspaper,
    `crossword.JPG`.

Both are checked here against the source's own literal comparisons rather than
against the pictures, since a headless runner never shows them.

Timing.  The game has four `define timer` blocks and the interesting one is
`drinktimer`, interval 30: when you tip Jack the bartender and order a drink,
the cocktail is not made for thirty turns.  There is no way to hurry it and the
cocktail is worth a point, so the script spends the wait in the bar -- the timer
action checks `if here <jar>`, i.e. whether you are still standing at the bar,
and sends you down a different branch if you wandered off.  Overshooting is
harmless while an excursion would have to land back on exactly the right turn,
so the wait is deliberately spent reading the newspaper cover to cover (which is
also where the crossword lives) and looking at the fixtures.  This walkthrough
therefore needs --tick, so that `tick_timers` runs once per turn and the timer
intervals behave as turn counts.

Two commands the author wrote are dead, and they are dead in real Quest too --
this is not an interpreter divergence.  `define synonyms` maps `drive = use` and
`type = use`, and Quest applies synonym substitution to the whole input line
before any command matching (V4Game.Part2.cs:4149-4163 rewrites
`" " + input + " "`, so even a bare one-word `drive` is caught).  So
`command <drive>` and `command <type on keyboard>` can never fire; the surviving
routes into the same scripts are USE CAR (line 29) and USE KEYBOARD (line 58),
which is what this script uses.  In the same vein `open`, `read`, `play`,
`drink`, `wear`, `climb`, `close`, `tie`, `fill` and a dozen more all collapse to
`use`, and `in` collapses to `on` -- hence USE OARS ON OARLOCKS and
USE MICROPHONE WITH MAGNET ON SHINY THING.

One naming trap: the bed and the bathroom both sit in `place <your; room>`, but
the room's own alias is "hotel room", so the exit reads "your hotel room" and
GO TO YOUR ROOM fails.  The script says GO TO HOTEL ROOM.

Route notes.  The first-floor corridor is five rooms long -- from the downstairs
landing you go north five times to reach `1st hall 1` and the padlocked
housekeeping closet -- and getting that count wrong silently strands you one
room short of the closet, room 105 and the ladder.  The lake is a hand-built
grid of eighteen `bay` rooms reached by rowboat (five dollars to Hank at the
pier, and you must fit the oars to the oarlocks before anything moves); the
shiny thing you fish up with the magnetised microphone is at bay 14, two moves
southwest-ish of the mooring, and the island is northwest from the far corner of
the grid.

Money is exactly sufficient and is spent in this order: one dollar for the
newspaper, one for the jukebox, one for the piano, one to tip Jack, two for the
drink, five for the boat -- nine of the starting twenty left over.

Verified: 234 turns, 125/125, rank "Perfectionist".
