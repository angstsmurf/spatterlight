Shipwrecked (by Wonderjudge, version 1.3, QDK Pro 4.1.1, ASL 410)
- raw command script for geas_walkthrough_runner.
Game system: Quest 4.  Win marker: "You have completed Shipwrecked".
Needs GEAS_SEED=1: three of the fights below are won with fixed menu answers.

A big, generous treasure hunt across three islands.  You wash up on the west
shore of island A with nothing; you leave on a repaired sail boat.  In between:
a pirate sloop to loot, a raft to build, a native village to bribe, a monkey to
befriend, a jungle tomb to light and rob, a volcanic island to bridge, and a
nine-piece sliding-tile door guarding a Manbearpig.  The script below is one
continuous run of 419 turns; the section markers are the only comments.

  1. Island A, the wreck (lines 1-83).  Collect timber, hammer, skull, canteen,
     coconut, spear.  THROW COCONUT knocks the machete out of the palm - and
     the coconut stays behind in the Meadow, which is why line 172 walks back
     for it much later (it is the only thing that will break the bee hive).
     The machete cuts the vines and the wall of vegetation; the pick axe opens
     the boulder; the stone rod opens the temple door and, used on the cave
     drawings, is the hint for the colored-hole puzzle.  The raft is assembled
     out of the tortoise shell, timber, rope, hooks, oars, crate and chest of
     gold - and then you must be holding almost nothing, hence the six DROPs:
     the raft refuses to launch while you are overloaded.
  2. The pirate sloop (84-141).  MOP + KEY RING gets you out of the brig.  READ
     BOOK sets the flag that makes you competent with a cutlass - without it
     every KILL answers "you don't know what you are doing with that cutlass"
     and the game is unwinnable.  RAISE SHIP'S FLAG drops the cutlass onto the
     deck.  The drunken pirate, the ship's wheel, the pry bar, the barrel, the
     parrot (feed it a cracker, then SPEAK to it, then say SKULLDUGGERY to the
     closet) and the pistol all come from here.
  3. Island A again (142-271).  The angry villager has to be killed before the
     village will let you east; the sack of rum buys off the villagers inside.
     The tall grass hides the box of matches.  The row boat is the ferry back
     and forth to the sloop.  The monkey follows you once you SPEAK TO MONKEY
     and stays riding your back for the rest of the game - keep it, the Ruins
     refuse to let you in while the loose Monkey object still exists, and the
     iron grate in the west tomb can only be opened by it.
  4. The colored holes (206-215).  EXAMINE STONE BUILDING reveals them; USE
     STONE ROD ON COLORED HOLES then asks seven times which hole to press.  The
     order 5 6 4 1 2 3 7 is the one the cave drawings describe.
  5. The cannon (247-258).  LOOK IN CANNON is what finds ceramic tile2.  LOAD
     CANNON twice (gunpowder, then the cannon ball), TAKE LARGE RUBY off the
     wall, FIRE CANNON to blow the village gate.
  6. Island B, the volcano (272-295).  Sail across, bridge the lava stream with
     the pile of timber, quench the hot tile with the bottle of water for
     ceramic tile5, and pick up the empty jar on the west hill.  Note line 268:
     the box of matches is deliberately left ashore, because swimming through
     the long tunnel ruins anything you carry that is not sealed in honey.
  7. The ant hill and the bee hive (296-339).  Fill the jar at the ant hill,
     carry the jar of ants to the anteater in the wooded grove, bring the
     anteater back, and EXAMINE ANT HILL for ceramic tile6 and the sapphire.
     USE COCONUT ON BEE HIVE then EXAMINE BEE HIVE for the emerald, the small
     basket and the honey.  The lantern is on the cliff top, which needs the
     spiked boots from the monkey nest.
  8. The tomb (340-358 and the tomb block that follows).  This is the fiddliest
     sequence in the game and it must be done in exactly this order: LIGHT
     TORCHES at the tomb entrance (needs the matches), go back up, tie the
     small basket to the rope on the stone dais, PUT LANTERN IN BASKET, LOWER
     BASKET, and only then descend - the archway blows the lantern out if you
     carry it, and refuses to let you past unless the lit lantern has already
     been lowered ahead of you.  The same rule applies leaving the main tomb,
     which is why the lantern goes back into the basket at the end.
  9. The three wings.  East: the dead thing must be fought hand to hand (the
     pistol was already spent on the pirate captain), 14 hit points.  West:
     EXAMINE IRON GRATE reveals the small opening, and USE MONKEY ON SMALL
     OPENING is the only way through.  South: all four gems go into the stone
     door, one at a time.  Each wing holds one ceramic tile.
 10. Island B again, and island C.  Collect the five cached tiles at the east
     shore, fill the jar with honey in the valley, PUT BOX OF MATCHES IN JAR OF
     HONEY (this is what lets them survive the swim), take the barrel of
     gunpowder out of the sail boat's hold, sail over, swim the long tunnel,
     DROP BARREL OF GUNPOWDER on the south hill and LIGHT GUNPOWDER to clear
     the rubble.
 11. The sliding-tile door.  EXAMINE STONE DOOR reveals the indentation; the
     eight tiles 1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9 go in in any order, tile5 last.  LOOK AT
     INDENTATION then asks "Do you wish to move the tiles?" and, once you
     answer yes, repeats a five-way menu (1 up, 2 down, 3 left, 4 right,
     5 exit) until the picture is right.  The start layout is 6 8 1 / 3 2 4 /
     9 7 5 with the blank at bottom-left and the goal is 1 4 7 / 2 5 8 / 3 6 9;
     the twenty moves below solve it.  USE CERAMIC TILE5 ON INDENTATION then
     grinds the door open.
 12. Manbearpig, 50 hit points, the longest fight in the game.  Killing it
     reveals the main sail and the black pearl and unlocks the door again.
     USE MAIN SAIL ON RIGGING and SAIL ends the game - and note that the sail
     has to go on LAST, because while the bare Rigging is still there SAIL is
     the ferry command that shuttles you between islands.

Deterministic combat.  Every fight is the same two-step loop: `combat` rolls
$rand(1;5)$ into tt1, asks how you attack, then rolls again and asks how you
defend.  For the attack, the choice that scores 2 damage is tt1-dependent
(1->2, 2->3, 3->2, 4->5, 5->4); for the defence, picking the number equal to
tt1 blocks completely.  Under GEAS_SEED=1 the whole $rand stream is macOS
libc srand(1)/rand(), so the runs below (11 answers for the drunken pirate,
11 for the angry villager, 17 for the dead thing, 55 for Manbearpig) take zero
damage and never miss.  They are position-dependent: every `gain do <invent1>`
- that is, every treasure or tile you pick up - burns one more $rand, so
inserting or removing a TAKE anywhere above will desynchronise every fight
after it.

Inventory.  `invent1` also enforces a limit of 14 carried items, not counting
the lantern, the spiked boots, the monkey, or anything inside a container you
are carrying; go over it and the game drops a random object.  That is why the
script caches the five ceramic tiles and the empty bottle on the east shore
(297-302) instead of carrying them around.

Engine notes - divergences this game exposed, all now fixed:

  * `list empty` / `list closed` were being dropped by the loader, so
    `property <crate2; list empty>` was always false and the last piece of the
    raft could never be added (fixtures/listempty.asl).
  * `repeat until <cond> <script>` with no `do` keyword was parsed as needing a
    literal `do` and the whole loop was silently discarded, which left USE
    STONE ROD ON COLORED HOLES doing nothing at all (fixtures/repeatcond.asl).
  * A scripted `out { ... }` exit had its de-inlined script text pasted into the
    room description ("You can go out to do <!intproc242>.") on the sail boat
    (fixtures/outscript.asl).
  * A container's `add` action/property was never consulted by `put X in Y`,
    and `add` was in neither of the loader's reserved-word lists, so PUT LANTERN
    IN BASKET and PUT BOX OF MATCHES IN JAR OF HONEY both fell through to
    geas's generic "You put X in Y." and set no flags (fixtures/containeradd.asl).
  * `got <X>` only looked at the object's immediate parent, so the matches
    stopped counting as carried once they were inside the jar of honey and
    LIGHT GUNPOWDER answered "You don't have anything to light it with."  Quest
    copies the container's ContainerRoom onto whatever is added to it, so it
    stays "got"; geas now walks the container chain, as its `here` already did.

Two walkthrough footguns worth remembering for other QDK games:

  * This game defines its own `verb <open>`, which bypasses geas's built-in
    open command and therefore never marks the container "seen" - and an unseen
    container keeps its contents out of scope.  Hence the LOOK AT STONE
    SARCOPHAGUS before every OPEN STONE SARCOPHAGUS, and the LOOK AT STONE BOX
    on line 217.  This is faithful: Quest gates container contents on Seen the
    same way.
  * A room exit created at runtime with `place <...>` is entered with GO TO
    <name>, not with the bare name; hence GO TO STONE BUILDING and GO TO SAIL
    BOAT rather than STONE BUILDING / SAIL BOAT.

Scoring.  This run finishes on ending1 with 30/400, the "you feel the outcome
could have been better" ending; all three endings print the same win text.  The
higher endings want the treasures deposited in the glass case in the sail
boat's hold (and, for ending3, five different things tea-bagged), which is a
much longer game than winning it.
