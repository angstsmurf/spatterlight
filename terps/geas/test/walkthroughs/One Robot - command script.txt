One Robot
Michael Tritz, v1.0, ASL 350, "Created with QDK 3.53 - UNREGISTERED EVALUATION VERSION"

You are E173-BETA, code name PATRICK, a servant robot rolling off a production
line in the walled city of Meliosis in the year 3058.  166 rooms and three
continents later you fire the assassin robot Horikama out of a cannon.  Almost
every gate in the game is a `use <weapon> on <thing>` or a `speak to` that
reveals the next weapon, so the walkthrough is a chain of errands with a great
deal of walking between them; 148 turns, no score.


THE WEAPON CHAIN

  Box of Weapons (Weapon Shop back room)  -> Boss Hunter gives the Beam Sword
  Beam Sword on Commuter 1                -> reveals the Energy Bazooka
  Energy Bazooka on the Unused Gate       -> `create exit south <Ruin; South Nibel>`,
                                             i.e. the way out of Meliosis
  Beam Sword on the Judge                 -> recruits Horikama and Apoc
  Horikama + Apoc in inventory            -> the Instructor uploads Flying Data
                                             and reveals the CP 9000 Keys
  Chain Gun (Exile Island scrap heap)     -> the three thugs, and L913
  Shining Swords (behind a cracked wall)  -> the boulder, and the endgame

The Thin Sword and the Gun in the optional training course are never needed
again; the course is walked anyway below, because it is three rooms long and the
Super Switch Presser 2000 that ends it is the game's one joke prop.  Its own
gating is the only `exists` test in the game written the right way round: the
Switch electrocutes you unless both training bots are already down, and Training
Bot 2 refuses to be shot if the first one is still standing.

Three of the rewards -- Beam Sword, Energy Bazooka, CP 9000 Keys -- are declared
`invisible` and turned on with `reveal`.  In Quest that flag suppresses the room
listing only; DisambObjHere resolves a typed noun on the availability flag that
show/hide sets and never consults visibility, so an invisible object is already
referrable.  So TAKE CP 9000 KEYS works in the hangar before you ever visit
flight school, in Quest as much as here.  This script plays it straight and
talks to the Instructor first.


THE FLIGHT GRID

Once the Cheese-Puff 9000 starts, flying is an 8x8 grid of rooms literally named
`<0,0>` .. `<6,7>` wired to each other with all eight compass directions -- 61
rooms of pure connective tissue.  Three cells have real names instead, and those
are the only three with a `down`:

  Air over Space Center           = (1,3)   down -> Hangar (Nibel)
  Air over Exile Island Airport   = (1,5)   down -> Exile Island Hangar
  Air Over Crudaso Airport        = (5,5)   down -> needs the landing pass

so the whole of the air travel in this walkthrough is E E, then S S S S.  Row 6
has no south exits and column 7 no east ones: that is the edge of the map.  Two
rooms are mis-titled -- `define room <2,>` and `define room <3,>` -- and the
author papered over it with `alias <2,1>` and `alias <3,1>` rather than fixing
the name, so their neighbours point at `<2,>` and `<3,>` and it all still hangs
together.

Landing at Crudaso is the one place the game asks you to type something that is
not a command: `down` prints "Enter the code found on the back of your Crudaso
Landing Pass" and the room defines `command <87461983> goto <Hangar 3>`.  The
code is in the pass's own look text.  The pass is `hidden` in the Scrap Heap and
cannot be picked up there -- joining the resistance in the Huge Chamber is what
`show`s it and `move`s it into your ship.

Take the Chain Gun out of the Huge Pile before you fly on.  There is no way back
to Exile Island afterwards: Ignition 3 in the Crudaso hangar answers "Not going
back just yet!  You have a mission!", and the gun is needed for the three thugs
in Crudaso City and for L913 on the 80th floor.  Refusing the resistance is a
`playerlose`.


THE TWO INVERTED BOSS CHECKS

Both observatory bosses are guarded by four switches, and both tests are written
backwards:

    use <Shining Swords> if exists <switch 1> and exists <switch 2> and
        exists <switch 3> and exists <switch 4> then { ...weakened... }
        else msg <The base is too strong to be attacked.  Maybe if you hit
                  those switches...>

`exists` is false once an object has been hidden -- the Mayor's House uses it
correctly two hours earlier, printing "Get out of my house!" while all three
thugs still exist and dropping the boat key once they are gone.  So Horikama
Base and the Horikama Shield Generator can only be attacked while their switches
are *intact*, and hitting the switches the game tells you to hit locks the run:
the boss becomes permanently unattackable and neither observatory floor has a way
back down.  The winning move is to walk straight past all eight switches.
(Observatory 2F's switch7 and switch8 hide `switch 3` and `switch 4` -- the
switches on the floor below -- which is the copy-paste that produced the whole
pattern.)


OTHER AUTHORING HOLES WORTH KNOWING

The Weapon Shop's back room was meant to be locked.  The shop defines an
`invisible` Key with `use on <Locked Door>` that would `create exit west <;
Back Room>` -- but nothing ever reveals the Key, there is no Locked Door object
anywhere in the file, and the shop already declares `west <Back Room>` outright.
So you simply walk in and take the box.

`Air over SD` is a room you can never stand in: its `script` immediately goes to
the Shopping District, which is how climbing west off the transit roof drops you
into the human-only district.  Its look text mentions a ladder, which belongs to
`Air over SD 2` (same alias, the climb back up).

Horikama 2 and Apoc 2 -- the recruitable copies -- are defined in Town Square,
not in the Town Hall where the judge dies, so you have to walk back south to
take them.  Their `take <You recruited Horikama.>` form is the whole recruitment
mechanic; the Instructor's test is `if got <Horikama 2> and got <Apoc 2>`.  The
original Horikama's `speak` is `if exists <Judge> then msg <...>` with no else
branch, so he goes silent for the rest of the game once the court scene is over.
Horikama 5 is never hidden after the duel either, so Observatory (3F) ends up
holding both the assassin and his own unconscious body.

Boarding the SS Cheese hides Horikama 2 and Apoc 2 out of your inventory; from
there on they are scenery in each scripted room.


DISAMBIGUATION

The three thugs all display as "Thug" and the eight switches all display as
"Switch", so USE CHAIN GUN ON THUG raises "Which thug do you mean?".  The script
answers 1 twice; the third blast finds only one thug left and asks nothing.  In
Observatory (3F) USE SHINING SWORDS ON HORIKAMA needs no answer, because
Horikama 5's alias matches exactly while "Horikama (unconscious)" would only
match on a partial word run, and an exact match wins outright.


ROOM LOOK TEXT AND SEMICOLONS

Crudaso City's Intersection 1 declares

    look <Cheese Street runs northeast to west; Milk Avenue runs north to south.>

and prints only "Cheese Street runs northeast to west" -- in Quest too.  From
ASL 350 on, a room's look tag is also copied into the room object's property
list with AddToObjectProperties ("look=" + r.Look, V4Game.Part2.cs:1170-1177),
that routine splits its argument on ';' (V4Game.cs:3995-4031), and ShowRoomInfo
prefers the property over the room's own Look field
(V4Game.Part2.cs:3863-3875) -- so the second half of the sentence becomes a
property named " Milk Avenue runs north to south." and is never printed.  Below
350 no property is written and the whole sentence survives; geas rewrites the tag
into a property whatever the version, so a pre-350 game with a semicolon in a
room look would lose its tail where Quest would not.  Nothing in the corpus does
that, so this is noted rather than changed.


THE ROUTE

Factory and training course, then the Hunter's Agency errand, the Beam Sword,
the Energy Bazooka, and out through the City Gates into Nibel.  Cheese Town for
the court scene and the two recruits, the Space Center for flying lessons and
the plane.  Fly east to Exile Island for the Chain Gun and the resistance, then
south to Crudaso: kill the gang for the mayor, buy passage with the boat key,
and let the captain wreck the ship on the way.  Down the hole on the Mysterious
Island, the Shining Swords behind the cracked wall, split the boulder, and ride
the resistance's tunnel into the basement of Government Tower.  Then up: L913 on
80, both observatory bosses without touching a single switch, the duel with
Horikama, and the cannon on the roof.
