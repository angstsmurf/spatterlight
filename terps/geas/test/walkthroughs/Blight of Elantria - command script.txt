Blight of Elantria -- command script
==========================================

"Blight of Elantria" v3, by Craig Dutton, (c) 2011, ASL 410, "Created with
QDK 4.1.5".  Shipped as a compiled Blight of Elantria.cas; read the source with
    perl terps/geas/uncas.pl "Blight of Elantria.cas" blight.asl
which yields 2941 lines.  At 80 rooms it is the largest map in this suite, and a
completely straight-faced high-fantasy treasure hunt: a demon named Xanthos
(spelt "Xanthus" in the intro text and "Xanthos" everywhere else) is raising an
undead army in the Southern Peaks, and the only thing that can stop him is the
Pandemonica, a demonic tome he keeps in his own dungeon.

This script wins the game outright in 401 turns.  Marker:
    "Elantria is in your debt forever"
Run it without --tick (see THE BELL below) and under the harness's GEAS_SEED=1
(see THE SCROLL below -- the answer to one puzzle is drawn from the RNG).


WHY THIS GAME IS WORTH KEEPING
------------------------------
It found a real engine divergence, and it is the only game in the suite whose
critical path depends on the exact turn a room's `afterturn` hook fires.

Quest reads the current room's index *once*, at the top of ExecCommand
(V4Game.Part2.cs:4116, `var roomID = GetRoomID(_currentRoom, ctx)`), and reuses
that one index for both the beforeturn hook (4177-4188) and the afterturn hook
(4567-4581).  Nothing recomputes it after the command has been dispatched, so
the afterturn that runs at the end of a turn always belongs to the room the
player was standing in when the turn *started*.  Walk into a room and its own
afterturn stays quiet until the following turn.  geas used to look the block up
from the post-command location instead, which fired the new room's afterturn on
arrival.  Fixed in geas-runner.cc (run_command now snapshots
turn_start_location); pinned by fixtures/afterturnroom.asl.

The Ice Queen's throne room is built on that one turn of grace:

    define room <throne room>
        south <crystal chamber>
        afterturn {
            if not flag <icequeendead> then { msg <...she kills you...> playerlose stop }
        }

and the Queen herself stands in that room, so `use warhammer on ice queen` has
to be typed *inside* it.  With Quest's timing you get exactly one turn: the turn
you walk north still belongs to the crystal chamber, and the turn you swing the
warhammer sets `icequeendead` before the throne room's afterturn tests it.  Fire
the new room's afterturn on arrival instead and the Queen kills you as you step
through the door, the ice key never drops, the Pandemonica's chest keeps one of
its two locks, and the game is unwinnable.  The transcript shows the intended
shape working: entering prints the room description, and it is the *next* turn
that would have killed you.

Beyond that it is a good exercise in bulk: 80 rooms, ~120 objects, nine keys of
six different metals, thirty-odd `use <a> on <b>` handlers, `create exit` in
three different syntaxes, two container-scope quirks, an RNG-derived password, a
follower NPC driven from a game-level `afterturn`, and eight ways to die.


THE SCROLL (why this walkthrough is seed-locked)
-----------------------------------------------
`startscript` ends with

    set numeric <scrollnumber; $rand(1; 10000)$>

and the tower of knowledge holds "1000's" of scrolls:

    take { msg <Which scroll from 1 to 10,000?>  enter <scrollnum>  ... }

Only the one numbered %scrollnumber% is the scroll of translation, and the
translation spell is mandatory -- `use scroll on pandemonica` sets
`tometranslated`, without which `use pandemonica on blue fire` refuses and the
Orb of Blood never appears.  The number is legitimately discoverable in-game:
`turn mirror` in the tower of light aims the Mirror of Enlightenment at the ball
of energy overhead, and `look at mirror` then reads it out.  This script does
both (the transcript prints "The mirror's reflection shows you the number:
6808.") and then answers 6808, because the `enter` prompt needs a literal.  That
6808 is the first and only value the RNG is asked for in the whole game, so it
is a pure function of the seed: under a different seed the mirror will report a
different number and the line after `take scrolls` must be changed to match.
`enter` consumes the following script line without spending a turn, which is why
the turn count is 401 for 402 script lines.


THE BELL (why this walkthrough runs without --tick)
---------------------------------------------------
Smashing the black sphere in the echo chamber is fatal unless you are deaf:

    use on <sphere> { if not flag <skullfound> and flag <deaf> then {...show <skull>...}
                      else { msg <...your ears start to bleed and you fall down dead.> playerlose stop } }

You go deaf by ringing the belfry bell, which also starts

    define timer <bell>  interval <60>  action { flag off <deaf> ... }

-- sixty seconds of *real* time, not sixty turns.  The belfry is in the
graveyard in the northern forest; the echo chamber is inside Xanthos' cave
system past the grassy plain, roughly thirty rooms and (in this route) two
hundred turns away, and the warhammer you need to smash the sphere with is in
the ice palace, further still.  There is no plausible way to make that trip in a
minute, so as written the puzzle is only solvable because the harness does not
advance real-time timers unless --tick is passed.  Left as-is deliberately: the
walkthrough exercises the ring/deaf/smash chain, and adding --tick would turn a
sloppy timer into a hard block.


TWO CONTAINERS THAT HIDE THEIR CONTENTS
---------------------------------------
Three of the game's payoffs sit inside containers that the player has to open
by hand even though nothing in the prose says they are shut, because the author
declared the container `opened` (or opened it from a script) but left the child
objects out of scope until an explicit open:

  * the secret recess in the temple, revealed by `use salt on bowl`, holds the
    bronze key and the ruby -- `open recess` first;
  * the sarcophagus in the crypt, which `use shovel on sarcophagus` already
    calls `open <sarcophagus>` on, holds a `hidden` corpse -- `look in
    sarcophagus` first, then `look at corpse` drops the gold key;
  * the hollow in the restored oak in forest3, revealed by `look at oak`, holds
    Prince Einhart's crown -- `open hollow` first.

Without those three extra commands `take bronze key`, `look at corpse` and
`take crown` all answer "You don't see any ...".


THE SHOVEL, WHICH MUST BE BROKEN AND MENDED IN THE RIGHT ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------
The shovel from the wizard's flowerbed has four `use on` handlers and a
life-cycle:

    use on <grave>        needs `not flag <shovelbroken>`  -> digs up the coffin
    use on <avalanche1>   needs `not flag <shovelbroken>`  -> digs up the Goblin
    use on <sarcophagus>  if `not flag <shovelfixed>` it BREAKS the shovel;
                          otherwise it levers the lid off
    use on <sap>          mends it (`shovelfixed` on, `shovelbroken` off)

So the sarcophagus can only be opened by a shovel that has already been broken
on it once and then mended, and the mending has to happen in forest2 -- the sap
from the yadda tree has no `take`, so it can never leave the room where the
knife cut it.  This script therefore digs the grave first (while the shovel is
whole), snaps the shovel on the sarcophagus, walks two rooms to forest2 to glue
it, walks back to lever the lid, and only much later digs out the Goblin.  The
same trip to the sap also mends the boathouse oar, which is the only way to
reach the island.


ONE APPLE, TWO TAKERS
---------------------
The barrel in the cellar holds exactly one apple, and two characters want it.
`give apple to horse` just eats it ("The horse take it and munches it up"), while
`give apple to ogre` buys the pickaxe -- and the pickaxe is the only thing that
clears the fallen rocks, which is the only way to the Ice Dragon's lair, which
is the only source of the gemstone of light, which is the only thing that kills
the Krahl Crones and drops the fire key, which is one of the Pandemonica chest's
two locks.  Feeding the horse quietly ends the game.  (`eat apple` does the same
damage.)

The horse is wanted for something else entirely, and takes two rides.  In the
stables `ride horse` moves it to forest1 and bumps `horseride` to 1; riding it
again in forest1 makes it rear into the trees and dislodge a Zuzu bird's egg,
which Malbus trades for the amulet.  The two branches are tested in the wrong
order in the source (`if is <%horseride%;1>` comes first, then a redundantly
nested `if is <%horseride%;0>`), which is harmless but explains why the first
ride does not also produce the egg.


THE KRAHL CRONES WILL EAT YOU FOR THE WRONG PRESENT
---------------------------------------------------
    give anything { msg <'Wrong, my dear...'  The witches grab you and eat you alive!> playerlose stop }

Only three gifts are safe -- the dead mouse from the belfry rafters, the severed
hand cut off the avalanche Goblin, and the skull from inside the black sphere --
and all three are needed: `makepotion` has to reach 3 before the cavern's
afterturn (`if here <Krahl Crones> then do <potioncreated>`) will brew the Potion
of Decay.  The witches also have to be alive to brew it, so the gemstone of
light that obliterates them and drops the fire key must be used *after* the
potion is in hand.  Malbus then turns the Potion of Decay into the Potion of
Life, which revives the dead oak in forest3.


SMALL THINGS
------------
  * `create exit <great hall; secret room>`, fired by turning the wheel behind
    the banquet-hall tapestry, is the two-parameter form: a *place* exit, not a
    directional one.  The great hall then reports "You can go to secret room."
    and the command is `go to secret room` -- no compass direction reaches it.
    The other two are ordinary: `create exit north <fallen rocks; lair>` and
    `create exit west <hall of mists; lava river 1>`.
  * The stone head's combination is written on the sign in the oak-panelled
    room: 'PUSH LEFT, PUSH BOTTOM, PUSH MIDDLE, PUSH RIGHT.'  Bottom is the
    mouth and middle is the nose, so the order is left eye, mouth, nose, right
    eye, stepping `headcomb` 0->4; any push out of sequence resets it to 0.  Say
    "left eye" and "right eye" in full -- both objects carry `alt <... eye>`.
  * The Trapper needs two conversations and a shield.  His first reply is fixed
    until you have actually seen a dragon, and the flag for that is set by the
    lair's room entry `script`, so you must walk into the lair once (which is
    survivable) and then go back to the log cabin.  His second reply sets
    `trapperfollows`, and the game-level `afterturn if flag <trapperfollows>
    then move <Trapper; #quest.currentroom#>` walks him along behind you.  Once
    he is holding the shield the lair's `south` exit kills you both instead of
    letting you leave, and only firing the golden arrow clears the flag again --
    so `give shield to trapper` and `use arrow on ice dragon` have to be
    consecutive.
  * `use pandemonica on fire` raises a disambiguation menu, because by then you
    are also carrying the fire key.  Say `use pandemonica on blue fire`.
  * The lamp is the only way home.  The ice bridge melts behind you as you cross
    ("it crashes and melts into the lava river"), and the far bank's only exit
    leads deeper in, to Xanthos' sanctum.  `rub lamp` beside the lava river once
    Xanthos has been destroyed teleports you back to Malbus, where `speak to
    malbus` is the game's single `playerwin`.  Take the lamp with `steal lamp`:
    plain `take lamp` is refused while Malbus is watching ("That lamp is
    useless, don't bother with it").
  * The gemstone's handler ends with `show <cauldron>`, and no object called
    "cauldron" is defined anywhere in the 2941 lines -- a dangling `show` that
    Quest and geas both ignore.
  * Eight ways to die: firing the arrow at the Ice Dragon without the Trapper
    holding the shield (two separate branches), leaving the lair while he holds
    it, smashing the sphere while you can still hear, entering or lingering in
    the throne room while the Queen lives, speaking to her, offering her
    anything, looking at the webs in the cavern of webs, and offering the Krahl
    Crones anything but their three dead things.
  * `use <a> on <b>` in real Quest requires b to be in the room or in your
    inventory (`Disambiguate(useOn, _currentRoom)` then `Disambiguate(useOn,
    "inventory")`, V4Game.Part2.cs:5380-5403, else PlayerError.BadThing); geas
    does not check.  Every `use ... on ...` below is typed in the room that holds
    the target, so the script is faithful to the real interpreter too.
