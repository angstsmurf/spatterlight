INTRODUCTION.
People laughed at you when you told them you wanted to be a professional sports star. You don't have the tenacity, they told you. The discipline. You're not in the gym ten hours a day and you've never gotten up at four AM.

Then, you told them you were going to be a professional gamer, and they didn't laugh. Well, not exactly. They kind of didn't know that was a thing, it being in the years before pro gaming was a big deal.

Finally, you met them in the middle. You combined your two loves, sports and games, and you announced to the world that you would be the world's greatest tenpin bowler. And yes, initially, when they heard that your family put their heads in their hands for a little while and wondered where they went wrong, but nobody's wondering now. And nobody’s laughing, either. Because you're doing it.

You're almost there. In fact, if you win tonight's Grand Ultimate League Championship, you’ll be jet-setting to Detroit, the bowling capital of the world. (Don't listen to any of those other cities who say it's changed.) The only thing standing in your way is Bradley.

You can't stand Bradley. He grew up with bowling privilege: his father and uncle own their own alley, so he’s been practising since he was born. His game average is the same as yours, and you share a local record for most consecutive strikes in one night. But you’ve got him tonight: you've heard he injured his index finger and hasn't been able to practise for a whole week. He’s finished!

A couple of hours before you're supposed to arrive at the Championship, you start driving, but then you remember they don't allow warm-up time. You'll need to stop in at another alley beforehand to get your arm limber. Your GPS directs you to the nearest place that’s still open... and to your dismay, it's Bradley's family's. Well, you don't have much choice. And maybe he won't be there.

You enter and look around. And standing right by the pinball machine is Bradley.

You aren't sure how it happens. One minute, he’s reaching forward for a friendly-looking handshake; next minute, powerful hands have shoved you from behind and you see black as your head hits the floor. You feel hands rummaging through your pockets, grabbing your wallet and phone. You hear voices you recognise – Bradley's team mates for non-solo competitions. Nobody's coming to your aid. That's what you get for stepping onto your rival’s home ground. By the time you open your eyes and make it to your feet, there’s absolutely nobody left in the building, and the door is locked behind you.

Was this a set-up? But how? How could they have possibly known you'd... unless they were the only bowling alley still open in the area, and they knew you'd need to warm up... and now your locked in, and that trip to Detroit is as good as Bradley's. Well, you're not going to let that happen. There must be a way out.

The Bowling Alley
With trepidation, you examine your surroundings...

The front door is in the south-east corner on the south wall. To your left are two arcade games, a pinball machine and a claw crane. Beyond that is a long counter, behind which is a rental area full of shoes. The north half of the room consists of the bowling lanes: four of them, each set up with its pins, chairs, ball return machine and computer. Against the west wall is a prize machine that presumably goes with the arcade games. By the east wall is a food counter, and hanging from this wall is a league board, a big placard with names on it. In the middle of the floor is a large rack of usable bowling balls.
