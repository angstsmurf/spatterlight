Kingdom (Grecianu Andrei-Paul, v0.9, ASL 300, QDK 3.03) -- command script

Kingdom is not an adventure.  It is a one-room, turn-based management sim: your
liege has handed you a fief with three villages in it, and you type orders at a
prompt until you are exiled, killed, or overthrown.  There is no map, no
inventory, no object anywhere in the file, and -- this is the important part --
no way to win.  `playerwin' does not appear in the source, `define text <win>'
is an empty block, and the eight endings the game does have are all `playerlose'.
So this entry is a furthest-reachable-state pin in the mould of Blade Sentinel,
Theses of Canada and Incident of the Undead: the marker is a line of the
game's own text at the end of the route, not a victory.


WHY IT CANNOT BE WON, AND WHY IT BARELY CAN BE SURVIVED

The whole game is one balance sheet, and the author tilted it.  `beforeturn'
recomputes some seventy-five numeric variables -- per-village growth, riot
pressure, prosperity, siege strength -- and then does this:

        set numeric <Gold; %Gold%-%Army%>

Upkeep is one gold per knight per season.  The author meant to start you with
enough of both to feel it: the blocks say `value <$rand(30;60)$>' for Gold and
`value <$rand(10;20)$>' for Army.  Neither is a number.  geas reads a variable
block's initial `value' with parse_int (geas-state.cc:708-728), which is VB's
Val() -- the leading numeric part of the string, 0 if there isn't one -- so both
variables start at 0 and `[status]' says so before the first command of any run,
at any seed.  (Whether Quest 4 evaluates a function there is untested; the
Val()-shaped read is what geas does today, and it is what this route is derived
against.)

That zero is the whole game.  With no knights there is no upkeep, so the
subtraction is a no-op and the treasury only moves on income -- and the treasury
starts empty, which walks straight into `afterturn':

        if ( %gold% <= 0 ) then ...

That is checked at the end of every season, exiling you with a one-in-three
reprieve of $rand(5;15)$ gold from the liege.  A season that brings in nothing
is a two-in-three loss, and season one has to find a coin from somewhere before
it ends or the run is over on turn 1.  Income is thin: the three villages'
tributes are %V<N>econ% gold each, clamped to %V<N>max% = %V<N>pop%/2, which in
practice is the one gold a season each you see below; "Ask more funds from
liege" pays $rand(10;30)$ but ends the game outright on a 1-in-4 and quite
often just answers "I have a tight budget this year"; "Send knight on an
adventure" is the one reliable purse; and "Organize tournament" and every
village funding proposal only spend.

The other half of the zero is what makes the ending reachable.  `V<N>battle
riot' compares %Army% against the village garrison:

        if ( %Army% > %V3army% ) then ... else { msg <We have been defeated ...>
                                                 set numeric <Army; 0>
                                                 playerlose }

With no army you can never be on the winning side of that comparison, so the
moment Village3 revolts, riding out to it is a guaranteed defeat.  That is the
marker, and it is why this route never hires a knight and never summons one
home from a village: a single knight would retake Village3 instead, reset
%V3rioting% to 0, and the ending would have to be waited for all over again.


WHAT THE ROUTE DOES

Thirteen seasons, seventy-four script lines, and every menu answered.

  * Season 1 (lines 1-6) begs the liege.  It pays nothing this time, but the
    two tributes that arrive in the same `afterturn' are what keep the treasury
    off zero and out of the exile branch.
  * Season 2 (7-12) sends a knight on an adventure -- "Your knight has come back
    covered in fame and riches" -- which is the run's real income.  There is no
    knight to send; the command does not check.
  * Seasons 3-5 (13-18, 19-25, 26-32) tour all three villages.  "Travel to
    VillageN" costs $rand(2;4)$ gold and is the only way to see a village's
    numbers: V<N>inspection reads out the leader's competence, food,
    prosperity, riot pressure and population.
  * Seasons 6-11 (33-37, 38-43, 44-49, 50-56, 57-62, 63-68) keep an eye on
    Village1 while Village3's riot pressure climbs.  `V3riot' rolls
    $rand(1;500)$ against %V3riot% once a season, so the revolt has to be waited
    out; touring is the cheapest way to spend a season that still prints
    something.
  * Season 12 (69-73) is offered a tournament for 45 gold and declines it, and
    Village3 revolts at the end of that same season: "Village3 has overthrown
    thine authority and the leader hasn't come in this season."
  * Line 74 rides out.

        We have been defeated by Village3's army.
        And thus ends your rule.

That first line is the marker.  "And thus ends your rule." is `define text
<lose>' and prints for all eight endings, so it would match a run that regressed
into a turn-one bankruptcy just as happily; the battle line is specific to this
route.

Every menu in between is answered the same way, and the answers are forced
rather than chosen.  Receive every village leader who turns up with the tribute
-- refusing him prints "He has left, sorely insulted" and costs happiness, and
happiness is what riot pressure is computed from.  Take the standard tribute,
"That will be fine": "Ask for double" pays %V<N>econ2% but subtracts $rand(1;3)$
happiness, the same bad trade as insulting the leader.  Refuse every funding
proposal -- each wants ten to forty gold for a field, a workshop, a knight or a
shrine, and the treasury does not have it.


THE THREE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-NINE LINES OF SUCCESSION

Most of this transcript -- 359 of its 795 lines -- is one sentence repeated:

        The leader of Village1 has died and his son has taken over.

That is faithful, and it is worth explaining because it is what task #1's geas
fix was about.  When a village is sacked its population drops by $rand(5;20)$,
and `V<N>pop's own onchange floors it at 1 -- so %V<N>max%, which is
%V<N>pop%/2 in integer arithmetic, becomes 0.  Now look at V<N>army's handler:

        onchange {
                if ( %V1army% > %V1max% ) then set numeric <V1army; %V1max%>
                if ( %V1army% < 1 ) then {
                        set numeric <V1army; 1>
                        msg <The leader of Village1 has died and his son has taken over.>
                        set numeric <V1comp; $rand(10;70)$>
                }
        }

With max at 0 the two clauses fight each other: the clamp writes 0, which is
below 1, so the second clause writes 1 and announces the succession -- and that
assignment re-enters onchange, where the clamp writes 0 again.  Quest has no
re-entrancy guard whatsoever; SetNumericVariableContents runs the handler
unconditionally on every write

        if (!string.IsNullOrEmpty(_numericVariable[numNumber].OnChangeScript) & !_gameIsRestoring)
            await ExecuteScript(script, ctx);

(V4Game.Part2.cs:526-530), so Quest 4 ping-pongs here too and keeps ping-ponging
until VB6 runs out of stack.  geas used to follow it off that cliff; now the
recursion is bounded and the game continues.  The repeated line is the visible
shape of that bound, which makes this walkthrough its regression test.


QUIRKS

  * The startscript says `Type "start" for game to begin.' and there is no
    `start' command anywhere in the file.  Nothing needs typing: the room
    description has already printed by then, and unrecognised input simply
    passes a turn.  The help text is honest about it -- "To pass a turn, Do one
    of the commands or write anything else you want."
  * It also says "respect wording and casing", which is superstition.  Quest
    matches `command <Fire knight>' case-insensitively, and so does geas.
  * Every village turn can raise three `if ask' / `define selection' menus in a
    row: the leader with the tribute, the tax rate, the funding proposal.  The
    runner feeds script lines to a pending menu exactly as it feeds commands, so
    an answer and a command are the same kind of line here and miscounting them
    silently turns the rest of the route into garbage.  Worse, an unanswered
    menu is not a stopping point: make_choice () defaults it to option 1 and the
    season carries on.  --echo's `[choice] N' lines are the only way to tell an
    answered menu from a defaulted one, and every menu in this script has one.
  * Menu answers draw from the RNG stream, so changing one answer changes every
    roll after it -- and so does changing a command, which is why the route had
    to be re-derived line by line when geas moved its `$rand(a;b)$' onto
    erkyrath_random() (xoshiro128**, the same generator the Spatterlight build
    uses) seeded from GEAS_SEED.  Under the old draws the same shape of route
    took six seasons; under these it takes thirteen, because Village3 sits on
    its riot roll for six of them.
  * Two copy-paste bugs, both preserved: `command <Summon knight from Village3>'
    guards on `not ( %V1rioting% = 3 )' where it means %V3rioting%, and
    V2taxes' "Ask for double" credits the happiness penalty to V1happ instead of
    V2happ.
  * The author's typos are load-bearing for the marker text, so leave them
    alone: "Village3 can give you 1 gold pieces.", "for to hire a new knight",
    "simoly", "sufficint", "knigdom", "unsuccesses".

Verified: 13 turns, 74 script lines, marker "We have been defeated by Village3's
army", seed 1.  No win: there is none to reach.
