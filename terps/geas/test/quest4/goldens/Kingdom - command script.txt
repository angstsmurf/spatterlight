Kingdom (Grecianu Andrei-Paul, v0.9, ASL 300, QDK 3.03) -- command script

Kingdom is not an adventure.  It is a one-room, turn-based management sim: your
liege has handed you a fief with three villages in it, and you type orders at a
prompt until you are exiled, killed, or overthrown.  There is no map, no
inventory, no object anywhere in the file, and -- this is the important part --
no way to win.  `playerwin' does not appear in the source, `define text <win>'
is an empty block, and the eight endings the game does have are all `playerlose'.
So this entry is a furthest-reachable-state pin in the mould of Blade Sentinel,
Theses of Canada and Incident of the Undead: the marker is a line of the game's
own text at the end of the route, not a victory.


THE BALANCE SHEET

Everything in Kingdom is arithmetic.  You start with

        define variable <Army>  value <$rand(10;20)$>
        define variable <Gold>  value <$rand(30;60)$>

which under seed 1 is twelve knights and sixty gold -- both rolled as the file
is read, which is what Quest does with a variable block's `value' and what geas
does since finding 17.  `beforeturn' then recomputes some seventy-five numeric
variables -- per-village growth, riot pressure, prosperity, siege strength --
and charges upkeep:

        set numeric <Gold; %Gold%-%Army%>

One gold per knight per season.  `afterturn' checks the till:

        if ( %gold% <= 0 ) then ...

and exiles you, with a one-in-three reprieve of $rand(5;15)$ gold from the
liege.  So a large army is a large bill, and the treasury is the clock.


WHAT THE ROUTE DOES

Twelve seasons, sixty script lines, and every menu answered.  Eleven of the
twelve commands are `Fire knight' -- the army goes 12, 11, 10 ... 1 -- and the
twelfth rides out to Village3.

The ending is in `V3battle riot':

        if ( %Army% > %V3army% ) then { ...we retake the village... }
        else {
                msg <We have been defeated by Village3's army.>
                set numeric <Army; 0>
                playerlose
        }

`Fire knight' guards on `not ( %Army% = 1 )', so one knight is the floor, and
V3army's own onchange floors the garrison at 1 as well.  With one knight against
one or more, `%Army% > %V3army%' cannot be true, and riding out to a village
that has revolted is a guaranteed defeat.  That is the whole plan: shrink the
army until the comparison is lost in advance.

The money is the other half.  Every leader who arrives with a tribute is
received (answer 1) and asked for double (answer 1 again), and every funding
proposal is refused (answer 2).  `Ask for double' pays %V<N>econ2%, which is
twice the standard tribute, and costs the village $rand(1;3)$ happiness; riot
pressure is

        V<N>riot = (10 * (%V<N>pop% - %V<N>happ%) / (%V<N>pop%+1)) ^ 2

rolled against $rand(1;500)$ once a season.  So the double tribute is both the
treasury and the fuse: the gold climbs from 60 to a peak of 163 in season 6 and
still stands at 143 at the end, while all three villages revolt -- Village3 in
season 5, Village2 in 6, Village1 in 7 -- and from then on a season is just
the three overthrow lines and a knight being let go.


WHY VILLAGE1 IS IN THIS TRANSCRIPT AT ALL

Village1's season is the one behind a wrapper:

        define procedure <V1report>
                if not ( %V1pop% <= 0 ) then do <V1riot>
        end define

Village2 and Village3 call `V2riot'/`V3riot' straight from `beforeturn'; only
Village1 is guarded, and the guard is always true, since V1pop's onchange floors
the population at 1.  geas nevertheless never ran it.  Quest's ConvertFriendlyIfs
rewrites a parenthesised condition following any of "if (", "until (", "while (",
"not (", "and (" or "or (" into `is <a;lt=;b>', and geas's preprocess knew only
four of the six; after an unconverted `not', the surviving raw "<" opened a
<parameter> as far as the tokeniser was concerned, the search for `then' ran off
the end of the line, and the entire if-statement was dropped.  Village1 never
rioted, never sent its leader with the tribute, and never appeared in the season
report -- in a game whose whole content is the season report.  That is finding
70, fixed in readfile.cc, and this walkthrough is its regression test: Village1's
tributes in seasons 1 to 5 and its "Village1  has overthrown thine authority"
from season 7 on are the visible proof.

The route this file used to hold was derived before that fix and before finding
17, when Gold and Army both started at 0 and no knight was ever hired or fired.
It also used to print "The leader of Village1 has died and his son has taken
over." three hundred and fifty-nine times, because a sacked village's population
floor left %V1max% at 0 and V1army's two clamps then wrote 0 and 1 to each other
until geas's recursion bound stopped them.  With the villages solvent that
oscillation no longer happens here; the bound is tested directly by
fixtures/scriptdepth.asl, which reduces the Kingdom shape to one variable.


QUIRKS

  * The startscript says `Type "start" for game to begin.' and there is no
    `start' command anywhere in the file.  Nothing needs typing: the room
    description has already printed by then, and unrecognised input simply
    passes a turn.  The help text is honest about it -- "To pass a turn, Do one
    of the commands or write anything else you want."
  * It also says "respect wording and casing", which is superstition.  Quest
    matches `command <Fire knight>' case-insensitively, and so does geas.
  * Every village turn can raise three `if ask' / `define selection' menus in a
    row: the leader with the tribute, the tax rate, the funding proposal.  The
    runner feeds script lines to a pending menu exactly as it feeds commands, so
    an answer and a command are the same kind of line here and miscounting them
    silently turns the rest of the route into garbage.  Worse, an unanswered
    menu is not a stopping point: make_choice () defaults it to option 1 and the
    season carries on.  --echo's `[choice] N' lines are the only way to tell an
    answered menu from a defaulted one, and every menu in this script has one.
  * Menu answers draw from the RNG stream, so changing one answer changes every
    roll after it, and so does changing a command.  geas draws its $rand(a;b)$
    from erkyrath_random() (xoshiro128**, the same generator the Spatterlight
    build uses) seeded from GEAS_SEED, so this route is reproducible but seed 1
    is not optional.
  * Two copy-paste bugs, both preserved: `command <Summon knight from Village3>'
    guards on `not ( %V1rioting% = 3 )' where it means %V3rioting%, and
    V2taxes' "Ask for double" credits the happiness penalty to V1happ instead of
    V2happ.
  * The author's typos are load-bearing for the marker text, so leave them
    alone: "Village3 can give you 8 gold pieces.", "for to hire a new knight",
    "simoly", "sufficint", "knigdom", "unsuccesses", "The local pries has
    starved too."

Verified: 12 turns, 60 script lines, marker "We have been defeated by Village3's
army", seed 1.  No win: there is none to reach.
