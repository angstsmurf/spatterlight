Pyramid Of Terror
v1, ASL 410, "Created with QDK 4.1.5"

Sutekh, Egyptian god of destruction and dust, has been sending you visions, so
you climb down a manhole in the road outside your Victorian folly and go looking
for him.  Thirteen rooms, one word, no score.  The whole game is a hunt for the
letters of MARZIPAN, because the hermit in the cell below the chamber of fire
knows what nobody else will tell you: Sutekh cannot abide wedding-cake, and a
wedding-cake ingredient is the only thing that will kill him.  Once you have
guessed the word you do not need to type it -- you need to be holding the yellowy
blob and THROW it in his chamber (not DROP it: see THE TWO ONE-WAY UNLOCKS).
80 turns below, taking in every room, every timer, and every letter -- including
the R, which sits on the far side of a window that slams shut at OPEN OVEN (see
THE GOTH).


THE LETTERS

  M   the rug in the sarcophagus chamber; also the magnolia walls of the
      seemingly empty room ("Magnolia begins with an M")
  A   inside the sarcophagus -- LOOK AT it before you open it
  R   READ the Jane Eyre book -- it answers even from inside the goth, so it
      works any time in her one-room window (see THE GOTH)
  Z   the stone king in the chessboard room ("a zip, I tells ya")
  I   the seven-legged spider in the cobweb
  P   the condensation on the oven window, after SWITCH ON OVEN
  A,N the magnifying glass in Sutekh's chamber: the letters PAN drift behind
      the lens

The M and the A in the sarcophagus chamber are `invisible` objects, so they are
never listed; you find them by reading the room description and typing the noun.


THE SPINE

Only thirteen of the 80 turns are load-bearing:

  n n n n e e            sewer -> passageway -> sarcophagus chamber -> kitchen
                         -> temple of enticement -> chessboard room ->
                         seemingly empty room
  take marzipan
  e / dive in            swimming-pool, then the dive into Sutekh's chamber
  throw marzipan         Sutekh dissolves, the trapdoor above clicks open
  up                     your drawing-room
  play record player     reveals the joint
  smoke joint            wins

Everything else -- the goth, the coffee-shop, the gingerbread man, the hermit --
is optional colour.  The script below does all of it, in the one order that
reaches all of it, because most of it is where the game's authoring quirks live.


THE TWO ONE-WAY UNLOCKS

The chamber of fire and the hermit's cell hang off a northwest exit from the
sarcophagus chamber that starts locked, and the only thing that unlocks it is the
gingerbread man's `drop` tag:

    drop {
        unlock <sarcophagus chamber; northwest>
        goto <sarcophagus chamber>
        ...
    }

So you must open the oven (SWITCH ON OVEN first, for the P), take the gingerbread
man, and DROP him -- anywhere, the script teleports you back to the sarcophagus
chamber itself.  EAT GINGERBREAD MAN instead is instant death (`playerlose`), and
the object warns you which way round it goes if you SPEAK TO it first.

The other locked exit is `up` out of Sutekh's chamber, and the blob offers two
scripts that open it: `action <drop>` and `action <throw>`.  Only THROW works in
Quest.  The blob has no raw `drop` tag, and Quest's own DROP handling runs
nothing else (finding 72 in the oracle FINDINGS): the real Quest 4.1.5 under
Wine (probed 2026-08-16, ~/quest-oracle/pyramid.cmd) answers DROP MARZIPAN in
Sutekh's chamber with the stock "You drop it.", leaves UP locked, and only
THROW MARZIPAN dissolves Sutekh and opens the trapdoor -- the `action <throw>`
is reached through the game-block `verb <throw>`, which is a live path where
DROP's is not.  geas still runs `action <drop>` (the divergence finding 72
records), so the script throws, which both engines play identically.

Don't drop or throw the blob anywhere else: in geas the `action <drop>` runs
`lose <yellowy blob>` on both branches and `action <throw>` starts with `lose`,
and there is no second blob.  (In Quest an early DROP is the harmless default
drop, so the two engines part company on a misstep here -- another reason the
script keeps the blob pocketed until Sutekh's chamber.)


THE GOTH, AND THE ONE-WINDOW DOORWAY TO THE COFFEE-SHOP

The goth is `parent <sarcophagus>` and `hidden`; the sarcophagus's open script
reveals her with a bare `show <goth>` and never opens the sarcophagus.  You get
one room's worth of her, and the script now takes all of it: give her the brass
ring, LOOK AT her to reveal the Jane Eyre book, TAKE the book (she refuses, and
the refusal is the hint), give it back for her invitation, READ it for the R --
the read answers even with the book back inside her -- and say YES GOTH.  The
two of you are transported to the coffee-shop of mystic interludes, the game's
one room with no exits: the way out is USE CAFETIERE (with MAKE COFFEE, a global
game command that pours anywhere) and then DRINK AMERICANO, whose script does
`goto <seemingly empty room>`.  The goth stays behind for good, dreaming of
limes, which is why the later SPEAK TO GOTH in the chessboard room answers "I
can't see that here."

The window is real, and it slams shut at OPEN OVEN, because the oven's open
script ends with a parentless `remove <gingerbread man>`:

    ExecAddRemoveScript, no ";" in the parameter (V4Game.cs:2695-2699)
        AddToObjectProperties("not parent", childId)
        UpdateVisibilityInContainers(ctx, _objs[parentId].ObjectName)
                                            ^ parentId is never assigned on
                                              this branch, so it is 0, so the
                                              name is "" -- a sweep of *every*
                                              container in the game

The sarcophagus is `seen` but was never `opened` (its open script ran instead of
DoOpenClose), so that sweep re-derives the goth's hidden flag as hidden and she
is gone.  A player who opens the oven before saying YES loses everything behind
her for the rest of the game: the book, the invitation, the coffee-shop -- its
three kinds of bean, its `weigh` command with an empty body, its Seattle Sound
-- and the letter R.  So the script does the whole goth line first and only then
walks back for the kitchen: the Americano drops you in the seemingly empty room
(where the marzipan is, but it can wait), and w, w, s returns you through the
chessboard room and the temple.

The window itself is what Quest does, and the QuestViva oracle plays the whole
coffee-shop scene byte-identically.  One earlier version of this walkthrough
went through the coffee-shop for the wrong reason -- geas used to name the
emptied container on that branch rather than sweeping everything, so the goth
survived the oven -- and the next went round her entirely, believing the sweep
made the shop unreachable; in fact the pre-oven YES GOTH is live in both
engines.


THE VENDING MACHINE

The vending machine in the seemingly empty room is a `transparent` `container`
holding Maltesers, monster munch and a raspberry slushpuppy, and WAVE CANE in
that room is what opens it.  But the wave script only shows two of the three:

    remove <raspberry slushpuppy> / <monster munch> / <Maltesers>
    show <raspberry slushpuppy>
    show <monster munch>
    show <monster munch>       ! the Maltesers line the author meant to write

The Maltesers are therefore only takeable if something else has already cleared
their hidden flag -- and LOOK AT VENDING MACHINE does it, because looking marks
the machine `seen` and a transparent, seen container makes its contents
available.  So the order in the script matters: look at the machine, then wave
the cane.  (The machine's own look text is the stock "Nothing out of the
ordinary."; the author gave it no description.)

DRINK SLUSHPUPPY starts the marzipan timer, which nags you every ten turns until
STOP DRINKING -- a game command, not the slushpuppy's `action <stop >`, whose
name has a stray trailing space and can never be typed.


THE TIMERS

Seven, of which two are on from the start: `sutekh timer` (20) nags while you
carry the brass ring, `horus timer` (30) while you carry the lichen.  The other
five are turned on by scripts -- the pipe in the temple, the spider's venom, the
slushpuppy's tartrazine, the record player's Kashka, and the mummy vision that
the mouldy Snickers gives you.  None of them can kill you; the Snickers vision is
cleared by SICK UP SNICKERS, the pipe by STOP PIPE.  At 73 turns the script trips
most of them at least once, which is why the transcript has stray nags in it.


THREE ENGINE FIXES CAME OUT OF THIS GAME

All three are pinned by fixtures now (danglthen, verbeval, contviscope).

1.  `verb <drop #@object#>` in the game block.  QDK invites this mistake: the
    author typed the whole command form into the verb box.  Quest reads a verb's
    name list with GetParameter, so `#@object#` -- there is no string variable
    "object" -- collapses to nothing and leaves the unmatchable name "drop ".
    The verb is dead and DROP falls through to Quest's own handling, which runs
    the object's `drop` tag.  geas read the name unevaluated, matched it as a
    two-noun pattern, and answered "Consider it dropped." to every DROP in the
    game -- including DROP GINGERBREAD MAN, i.e. the only way into the second
    half of the map.

2.  USE CANE.  Its script nests a dangling `then` inside a braced then-branch,
    which readfile's de-inlining pass splits into two independent statements
    exactly as Quest's ConvertMultiLineSections does.  Quest runs an empty
    then-script and then the next line unconditionally; geas started the
    then-branch one character past the "then" token and ran off the end of the
    string, aborting the process.

3.  SPEAK TO GOTH, in the one room where Quest still answers it.  She is
    revealed inside a sarcophagus that is never opened, so her visibility
    depends on nothing re-deriving the hidden flags of the sarcophagus's
    contents.  Quest's runtime calls to UpdateVisibilityInContainers name one
    container -- except the parentless `remove` above, which names none; geas
    swept them all every time, so the first unrelated LOOK AT a container
    anywhere in the game put her out of scope and even the ring and the book
    answered "You don't see any goth."


OTHER THINGS WORTH KNOWING

`command <speak to>` is defined in the game block with an empty body, and it is
harmless: a user command with no #parameter# in it matches only the exact text
"speak to", so SPEAK TO RAT never reaches it and falls through to the built-in
handling, which runs the rat's own `speak` tag.  Typing SPEAK TO on its own is
the one command in the game that prints nothing.  The same is true of
`command <weigh #@object#>`, except that one does have a parameter, so it
swallows every WEIGH and prints nothing for all of them.

DIVE IN is a game command (`splash; swim; dive in; make a splash`) that does
`goto <Sutekh's Chamber Of Cruelty>` from anywhere.  You can therefore reach
Sutekh from turn one; without the marzipan there is nothing to do there and the
trapdoor stays shut, so it is not a shortcut.
