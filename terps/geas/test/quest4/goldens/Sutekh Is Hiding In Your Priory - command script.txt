Author unrecorded -- the game block carries no `game author` line, only
`game info <Created with QDK 4.1.5>`.  ASL 410, ships as a plain .asl.
Command script for terps/geas/test/quest4/harness/run_walkthroughs.sh.

Verified: 158 turns, leftover 0, WON.  No --tick needed.


WHY THIS GAME IS IN THE SUITE

Almost every other game in the corpus is a room-and-object game that happens to have
a few `command <...>` lines.  This one is the other way round: the priory is a stage set
for six global parameterised commands, and the interesting engine surface is all in the
game block, not in the rooms.  It has

  * `afterturn` running a nine-deep nested if/else chain over a turn counter, ending in
    `playerlose` at exactly 200 turns -- so the whole walkthrough is played against a
    hard budget that the engine itself enforces;
  * `dec <number of turns; 6>` and `dec <number of turns; 2>` -- objects that hand turns
    *back*, so the counter has to be writable downward as well as up;
  * `command <dial #number#>`, `<say #text#>`, `<summon #spirit#>` and `<shout #loudly#>`,
    each dispatching on `select case <#param#>` with multi-value cases
    (`case <satan; the devil; devil; lucifer; set; sados; saddos>`) and a `case else`;
  * `if ask <...> then ... else ...` -- the `fly` command, where the Yes branch is a
    `playerlose` and the No branch is a `goto`, so the answer the runner feeds in is
    load-bearing rather than cosmetic;
  * five `choose <selection>` menus (books, newspaper, comic, gravestones, hymn book);
  * `lock` / `unlock` in the two-argument `<room; direction>` form, next to the
    author's stray `unlock <; >` with *both* arguments empty;
  * a rash of empty-argument commands -- `show <>`, `goto <>`, `flag on <>`,
    `timeroff <>` -- which QDK emitted whenever the author left a field blank in the
    editor.  Seven of them are on the winning path.  None may abort the turn.

There is exactly one `playerwin`, on the rose, and getting to it means threading past
about eighteen `playerlose` traps, several of which look like ordinary scenery.


THE SHAPE OF THE GAME

The intro tells you the truth: find and give Sutekh the one thing that banishes him,
then do "a couple of other duties".  Concretely, `playerwin` lives on the rose's
`give to <monk>` handler and fires only `if flag <mummy flag>`, and `mummy flag` is set
in exactly one place -- Sutekh's `give <signed photo>` handler, and only if you are also
carrying the amulet.  So the spine is:

  amulet + signed photo  ->  banish Sutekh in the priesthole  ->  rose to the monk.

Each of the three has a wrinkle.

  The amulet is buried in the mossy wood.  `take shovel`, `use shovel` (the shovel's
  `use` has no target, so plain "use shovel" is the whole command), `open trinket box`,
  `take amulet of something-or-other`.  The amulet has no `alt` names, so you must type
  all four words.  Do NOT then hand the shovel to the woodsman on the road, however
  much he asks: `give <shovel>` on him is a death.

  The signed photo is on the counter in the cathedral tea-room, and it is `hidden` until
  you `look at counter` -- the counter's `look` script is what does `show <signed photo>`
  (followed by a bare `show <>`).  Nothing announces it; you just have to take it
  afterwards.  The beam in the porch and the scratches on it spell out the hint.

  The priesthole is reached by `bounce on bed` in the bedroom -- `get into bed` is a
  death, and so is `enter wardrobe`.  The bedroom door is `east locked` from the
  observatory and is unlocked by `sing to david bowie` in the ruined summerhouse, whose
  handler ends with the empty `unlock <; >`.  Once in the priesthole, `down` is locked
  until Sutekh is gone, so there is no way out but through him.

And then the twist that catches everybody: the `sutekh` object in the priesthole is
`hidden`, and the *only* thing anywhere in the game that shows it is
`command <summon #spirit#>` with `case <sutekh>`.  You have to summon the demon by name
before you can hand him the photograph.  A player who never types "summon sutekh" stands
in an apparently empty slit-room with a locked exit and no way to finish.


GETTING TO THE TEA-ROOM, AND BACK

The tea-room has no exits at all.  There are two ways in.  The canonical one is the
scone: `switch on oven` in the servant's area, `cook scone mix`, then `eat scone`, whose
action is a bare `goto <cathedral tea-room>`.  The other is `fly`, a global command
gated on `if ask <Are you really sure?...>`: answer Yes and you flap off the branch and
die, answer No and the game rewards your good sense by teleporting you to the tea-room
anyway.  Note `fly` has no room check despite its prose, so it works from anywhere --
this script uses it from the graveyard, which is also where it gets its `ask` coverage.

The way out is `eat slice of walnut cake`, which `goto`s the road outside the grounds and
then `hide`s the cake.  There is only one cake and it is not replaced, so the tea-room is
a one-way trip: if you go there twice you are stranded.  That is why this script bakes
the scone but eats it *inside* the tea-room -- the re-`goto` is harmless and it still
exercises the scone's handler.

`switch on oven` is worth doing early for its own sake.  `flag <oven on>` is read by
Mr Loftus, by the phoenix, and -- crucially -- by the Hake of Retribution, below.


THE HAKE, WHICH IS A DEATH ROOM WITH A FLAG-GATED EXIT

`cross bridge` at the canal drops you into "the deep", a room with no exits and one
character.  `speak to hake of retribution` branches on `flag <oven on>`: with the oven
off the Hake eats you (`playerlose`); with it on he offers to release you if you sing.
`sing to hake of retribution` then `goto`s you back to the canal.  So an ordinary-looking
scenery verb in the kitchen, twenty rooms earlier, is the difference between a death and
a detour.  Worth keeping in the script for exactly that reason.


THE MUMMY HAS TO BE WALKED ROUND THE HOUSE BY HAND

Freeing the mummy is the second of the intro's "couple of other duties", and it is also
the tidiest way out of the priory after Sutekh is gone.  `burn down priory` needs three
things at once: `here <mummy of all malevolence>`, `flag <mummy flag>`, and the book of
matches from the woodsman's hut.  But the mummy starts nowhere: `play organ` in the music
room is what `show`s her, and after that she only moves when prodded, along a chain
written entirely as `here <object>` tests:

  prod her while `here <church organ>`   (music room)      -> move to panelled library
  prod her while `here <bust of a poet>` (panelled library)-> move to porch
  prod her while `here <doormat>`        (porch)           -> move to bedroom

The tests are on *scenery objects*, not on room names, and they are checked in that
order with no `else`, so the sequence is forced: music room, then library, then porch,
and only then is she in the bedroom where you will be standing when you come down out of
the priesthole.  Prod her anywhere else and nothing at all happens.  This script does the
three prods on the way past each room, which costs no extra movement.

`burn down priory` also `lock`s the road's south exit and `show`s the priory object, so
after it fires you are outside for good -- which is fine, because everything left to do
(rose, monk) is out there.  The alternative exit is `rub brass lantern`, which teleports
you to the road and locks the same exit; the lantern is what Stonehouse means by "the
eastern lawn holds the key" when the gate refuses you.

The lantern starts on the eastern lawn but is not picked up there, because by then it has
moved: `kiss statuette` does `lose <brass lantern>` before its `goto`, and `lose` puts the
object in the room you are standing in, so the lantern follows you into the orchard and is
lying there when you come back through the arch.  That is the whole point of the `lose` --
otherwise the kiss would have no reason to touch the lantern at all.


THE CHILD, WHICH IS THE ONE OPTIONAL BIT THIS SCRIPT KEEPS

`poke doorbell` in the porch is not a trap, it is the entrance: it drops you into the
secret tunnel, and west of that is the boutique of injury with a sick child.  `take mould`
in the tunnel, `give mould to child` (a `give to <child>` handler on the mould, the
mirror image of the `give <signed photo>` handler on Sutekh -- the game uses both
directions of the give idiom), and he tells you who he is.  `take child` then teleports
the pair of you to the graveyard and `move`s him there, and `bury child` lays him to rest
-- but only `if flag <candle on>`, which is set by `speak to candle` in the church.  It
buys nothing towards the win; it is in the script because it is the only place in the
corpus where a character is carried by being taken and re-parented by the same handler.


AUTHORING QUIRKS WORTH KNOWING

`define synonyms` is processed on whole-word boundaries over the raw input line before
any command matching happens (Quest's ConvertCommand, V4Game.Part2.cs:4150-4164), and
so a synonym can reach past the parser and rewrite a command the author meant to keep:

  `make = cook` means the command `make it stop` -- the author's own way of silencing the
  Sutekh timer -- is rewritten to "cook it stop" before `command <make it stop>` is ever
  considered, so that command can never match.  The response is "You don't see any it
  stop."  geas reproduces this exactly, which is the point of having the game here.

The rest of the table works as intended, including the two entries that look as though
they ought to break.  `pull pipe = open organ` rewrites to a command naming an object that
is called "a church organ", with no `alias` and no `alt` -- and it still resolves, because
the game sets `abbreviations on`, and Quest's `Disambiguate` (V4Game.cs:4599) falls back to
a word-start-anchored substring pass over prefix+alias when nothing matched exactly, so
" a church organ" matches " organ".  All three of `pull pipe`, `open organ` and
`open church organ` print the organ's `open` script -- the one that reveals the
jackal-masked Stonehouse inside it.  This script uses `open church organ` so the line
still reads correctly if the option is ever honoured more strictly.  `finger = push` is
likewise deliberate: the dado's "Have you fingered the panel?" really is a hint that
`finger panel` works, and `strike match = burn down priory` the same.

Other things to know:

  * `#number#` in `dial #number#` is a free-text parameter, not a number: `case <home>`
    and `case <random number>` are matched as literal strings, so "dial home" and
    "dial random number" are both real telephone numbers in this town.
  * `pick apple` does `dec <number of turns; 6>` and prints "five free goes"; the
    afterturn increment of 1 makes the net saving five, exactly as advertised.
    `eat pastries` in the tea-room refunds one turn the same way.  Both are used here.
  * but `eat apple` while holding one is a death, and the apple is `invisible`, so it is
    easy to pick one up and forget it is there.
  * `kill transluscent figure` is a death *only* before turn 50 (`if ( %number of turns%
    > 50 )`), which is the game's little joke about impatience.
  * `use kaleidoscope on stained-glass window` is a death, in the middle of a list of
    seventeen perfectly harmless `use on` targets.
  * the priory's `look` and the bedroom's `description` both branch on `flag <mummy flag>`,
    so the endgame re-describes the place as burning.
  * the ghostly priest interludes at 44, 88, 99, 133, 144 and 166 turns are the visible
    proof that the `afterturn` chain is being walked to the right depth.  This run is
    158 turns, so it sees the first five and not the sixth.
