"O, my dear wife, I am in myself undone because of a burden that lies hard upon me. I am certainly informed that this our city will be burnt with fire from heaven, in which you my wife, and you my sweet babes, shall miserably come to ruin, except some way of escape can be found whereby we may be delivered."

At this your relations were shocked. Not that they believed your words, but that they thought some dreadful fever had taken hold of you.

Therefore, because it was drawing towards night, and they hoping that sleep might settle your head, with all haste got you to bed.

So you would often retire to your bedroom to pray for and pity them, and also to console your own misery.

You would also walk solitarily in the fields, sometimes reading, and sometimes praying, and this for some days is how you spent your time ...

This miry slough is such a place as cannot be mended. It is the descent where the scum and filth that attends conviction of sin does continually run, and therefore it is called the Slough of Despond. For as the sinner is awakened about his lost condition, there arise in his soul many fears and doubts, and discouraging apprehensions, which all of them get together, and settle in this place.

The good counsel of Mr Worldly Wiseman,

"In my opinion there is not a more dangerous and troublesome way in the world than is that into which Evangelist has directed you. Know that the slough is the beginning of the sorrows that do attend those that go on in that way.

Hear me, you are likely to meet with wearisomeness, painfulness, hunger, perils, nakedness, sword, lions, dragons, darkness, and, in a word, death, and what not.

And as for that book, like other weak men, who, meddle with things too high for them, you do suddenly fall into these distractions, which not only unman men, as I perceive is the case here, but they run them upon desperate ventures, to obtain they know not what."
