Into The Briny Blue
briny blue.asl, ASL 410, "Created with QDK 4.1.5", Scott Lange and Abigail Auner,
Henchcorp Productions, 2011.  The ABOUT text says "Programmed in six hours" and
"Our third text adventure made, our first one that's any good."

You are Secret Serviceman Shane Georgeson, sent to find the President, who was
last seen three hundred miles off the coast of Florida looking for Atlantis.  No
score, no timers, no randomness.  117 turns below, which is the whole game plus
every joke response worth reading.


THE GAME IS A STRAIGHT LINE WITH ONE PUZZLE

The boat has five rooms (office, hallway, captain's quarters, storeroom /
Shane's private storage, diving deck, crow's nest) and the ocean is a single
descending corridor.  Almost every compass direction underwater is either a
self-loop -- `north <ocean1>` inside room `ocean1` -- or a `locked` exit whose
only content is a joke ("After hours of travelling upwards, you arrive on the
coast of Panama.  Realizing that you don't speak the language, you swim back out
to where you were and dive back 200 feet.").  There is exactly one branch, at
the ocean floor, and the branch is a dead end: north is a room containing a pile
of rocks, west is the way onward.

The one thing you have to work out is on the diving deck:

    LOWER LADDER    -> `create exit down <loading area; ocean1>`

That is the game's only `create exit`.  LOWER is in the verb list precisely
because of this one object.  Everything else is TAKE, then DOWN eleven times.

The route: office -> S -> S (storeroom) -> S (Shane's storage: TAKE GUN, TAKE
NOTE) -> N N -> E (captain's quarters) -> W -> UP (crow's nest) -> DOWN -> W
(diving deck: WEAR WETSUIT, LOWER LADDER) -> DOWN x4 -> NE -> NE -> NW (TAKE
BRACELET) -> N -> N -> DOWN x5 -> (ocean floor) N, S, W (TAKE FOLDER), W, W, N,
N, DOWN, DOWN, DOWN -> NW -> E -> TAKE HAM RADIO.


THE JOKE IS THAT THE GAME TAKES YOUR EQUIPMENT AWAY

The air tank is introduced with "WARNING: If you remove the scuba tank at any
time, you will run out of air and die!", and its `drop` tag really is a
`playerlose`.  Then the descent rooms' `script` blocks strip you anyway, one
item per room:

    ocean6      272 feet   "Your air tank explodes"        lose + hide air tank
    ocean7      unknown    the Tandy 400 implodes          lose + hide computer
    ocean8      700+ feet  the wetsuit tears               lose + hide wetsuit
    oceanfloor2 ~1100 feet everything else is crushed      lose + hide note,
                                                           bracelet, folder, gun

Nothing bad happens.  By the time you reach King Neptune's living room you are,
in the game's own words, "oxygen-less, naked", and still fine.  The gear was
never a resource; the warnings were the gag.

The two objects that look like clues are also gags.  The president's tracking
bracelet (tunnel2) and the president's manilla folder (path1) each have a
`use <tracking>` response that stops to tell you the tracker is not beeping, and
both are destroyed at the last ocean floor.  Neither is needed for anything.


THE ENDING

TAKE HAM RADIO in Neptune's garage.  The radio's `take` tag is a page-and-a-half
phone call from Commander Kenneth McDavidson: the mission was called off four
weeks ago, the intel was wrong -- the President went missing three *feet* from
the White House swimming pool, not three hundred *miles* off Florida -- and
Captain Darrel went home when they found out, assuming Shane was aboard.  Then
`playerwin`.  `define text <win>` is the two words "You win!".

Going EAST from Neptune's living room into the garage is irreversible: the room's
only exit is `west locked <nothing; Drat!  Someone appears to have shut and
locked the door on you!  If only there was a key around here somewhere...>`, and
there is no key in the file.  The teased upstairs bedroom (`up locked` in the
living room, same "if only there was a key" line) has no room behind it either.
Since the garage is also the win, none of that matters -- but do the trident and
the recliner first, because you cannot come back.


SIX WAYS TO DIE

  * DROP AIR TANK, or USE GUN ON AIR TANK.
  * USE CONTROLS -- you steer the ship into the cliffs of Dover.
  * SIT ON CONTROLS, TURN ON CONTROLS, PLAY WITH CONTROLS -- three more,
    separately written, all on the same object in the captain's quarters.  That
    one control panel is responsible for four of the game's losses.
  * SLEEP ON HERMIT CRABS at 75 feet.

Five of these are written `playerlose` twice in a row, which is an authoring tic
and harmless.


HOW THE VERBS WORK

The file `!include <stdverbs.lib>` and then does everything through property
strings.  Each object carries e.g.

    properties <read = "S.G."; play with = You fiddle with it, grow bored, ...>

and the game defines a fallback for each verb at game level (`verb <read> msg
<That particular item is illegible.  Try others.>`).  Where a verb needs to do
more than print, it becomes a block instead: `action <lower>` on the ladder,
`action <wear>` on the wetsuit, `action <play with>` on the seagulls (which
chases you out of the crow's nest), `action <sleep on>` on the hermit crabs
(which kills you).

The two in-game verb lists disagree with each other and with the file: HELP says
"only the following verbs" and lists twelve including USE, while VERBS says
"COMPLETE VERB LIST" and gives eleven, dropping USE and adding GET.  USE is the
verb the entire game runs on.


ODDITIES

  * `define room <nothing>` (line 144) is a real room with a full exit table --
    north/south to ocean5, east to `end of the tunnel`, west/northwest/northeast
    to ocean4, up to the boat, down to the cliff.  It is the target named by
    about twenty `locked <nothing; ...>` exits, and because `locked` prints its
    message without moving you, the room is never entered.  If any one of those
    locks were dropped, that room's `east` exit would skip most of the game.
  * The captain's quarters defines `define object <bed>` completely empty --
    no tags at all -- immediately above `define object <darrel bed>` with
    `alias <bed>`, which is the one you actually interact with.
  * USE COMPUTER ON <anything> is a running commentary the authors wrote for
    about fifteen objects; the computer is handed to you by the office's
    `script give <computer>` and is `drop nowhere`, so you cannot lose it before
    the ocean crushes it.
  * The joke `alt` names are worth knowing because they are the only clue that
    the authors were amusing themselves: the Oriental rug answers to "alice",
    the tracking bracelet to "carol", Darrel's bed to "sleepytime", the pile of
    rocks to "rockstars", the hermit crabs to "seafood".
  * `define options` has `debug on` and `panes disabled`.
  * The wetsuit's `action <wear>` ends with a bare `give <>` -- an empty give.
    geas ignores it; the message "You're wearing it!" prints normally.
