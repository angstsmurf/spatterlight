The Quest to find The Dark Hills (Slackers Inc., v2.0, 2009; QDK Pro 4.1.2,
ASL 410) -- a genuine win, but not through `playerwin'.

This is the game that Revenge of the Shadow Masters is a demo of the sequel to,
and unlike that demo it is finished and it can be won.  There is no `playerwin'
anywhere in the file, though, and no score: the ending is built out of timers.
The Dark Hills room script does `timeron <Alexendre on>' and `set numeric
<enemy health; 100>'; `Alexendre on' (interval 16) announces him and starts
`Alexendre Attack' (interval 6); and that attack timer is the only thing in the
game that ever checks whether the last enemy is dead -- when it ticks and finds
enemy health at or below zero it prints "You've defeated him!" and chains
`Credits 1' through `Credits 6' (intervals 6, 6, 6, 8, 6, 6), the last of which
prints "THE END" and calls `stop'.  So the whole game must be run with --tick,
and the marker here is "defeated him" rather than "THE END": the runner matches
markers case-insensitively and the tunnel under the cathedral describes
"something at the end of the tunnel" long before you get anywhere near the
credits.  The transcript does run all the way through to THE END and the runner
reports running=false, so the `stop' at the end of the credits is what ends it.

Combat, and why this script waits so much.  Every fight works the same way: a
room script turns on an "On" timer, the On timer shows the enemy and sets enemy
health to 100, and an "Attack" timer then ticks every few turns doing damage
until the turn on which it finds enemy health <= 0, at which point it hides the
enemy and unlocks the exit.  Two consequences.  First, you cannot hit an enemy
before its On timer has fired, because the enemy is `hidden' until then -- the
five `wait's in Fates Passage are the bobcat's `interval <5>'.  Second, killing
an enemy does not open anything by itself; the *next* tick of its attack timer
does, so the `wait's that follow each kill are load-bearing too, and the sixteen
in The Dark Hills are Alexendre's `interval <16>' arriving before you are
allowed to fight him at all.  The weapons: the can of body spray does
`dec <enemy health; 50>' -- but only inside `if got <Full Lighter>', which is
why the lighter has to be filled before you leave the house -- and the blue
towel from Damien's does `dec <enemy health; 100>' on all seven named beasts,
one line each, with no message of its own, which is why `use towel on bobcat'
looks like it did nothing.  The mountain lion therefore takes two sprays and
the bobcat one towel, and Alexendre, whose only `use on' handler in the game is
the body spray's, takes two more sprays.

The house.  `open book' drops the Key out of the old school book, `open desk'
reveals the map, the body spray, the lighter, the pen and the letter opener,
`use key on door' unlocks north, and the Back Pack is lying in the room.  The
Living Room is the real gate: its `afterturn' unlocks east only when you hold
the Full Lighter *and* the map *and* the back pack *and* have at least $10, and
because the unlock happens in `afterturn' the first attempt to leave always
fails -- the exit opens on the turn after the refusal.  That is what the
`search sofa' turn is doing here; it also finds a twenty-dollar bill, which the
route does not need but which is the only other money in the first half.  The
$10 itself comes from the night stand in the computer room, along with the
bottle of lighter fluid; `use lighter fluid on lighter' is what makes the Full
Lighter.  The detour west into the bathroom for `open medicine cabinet' and the
`take radio' in the bedroom look like scenery-poking and are not: the radio, the
baby powder and the bottle of tylenol are the three things the tribal leader
trades for near the end of the game, forty turns later and with no way back.

Outside.  Taking the Axe is what unlocks the northeast exit (its `gain' script
does the unlock, so merely holding it is enough), and `take wood' followed by
`use full lighter on wood' makes the Torch -- `take wood' prints nothing at all,
its take script being a bare `give' with no message.  The other way to a torch
is `kick desk' back home for a piece of desk, which costs 10 health, so the wood
is the cheaper route.  Do not go into the pub: The Pub's only exit is
`south locked <Outside the Pub>' and the sole thing that unlocks it is buying a
beer for $3.50, so a player who wanders in for atmosphere pays a toll to get
out again.  In The Forest, `use axe on east tree' is the only correct move --
the axe has a `use on' handler for eight trees, and seven of the eight
destinations (Callisto Road, Tribal Village, Forest of Demise, End of the line,
Jack's Mark) are one-line rooms whose script is just `stop', i.e. instant death
with a paragraph of explanation.

The churches.  Damien's Books needs `look for towel' before `take towel' will
work.  In the cathedral basement `use torch' shows a trap door and `open trap
door' does `create exit down' into the tunnel; the tunnel's own rats are on a
20-turn fuse and this route is through it in two turns, so they never appear.
In the basement of the second church, `open coffin' gives Clue 1 -- and the
wall tiles must be left alone.  Looking at them once gets a warning ("Open the
coffin if you must, but leave use alone or turn into dust"); looking a second
time hides the Torch and starts `Time passes in the crypt', which chains
`missing objects 1', `2' and `3' -- between them they hide the map, the key, the
back pack, the body spray, the Full Lighter, the axe and most of the rest of
your inventory -- and then `Crypt Ending', which stops the game.  Upstairs the
clues have to be found in order, `look at alter', `look at pews', `look at
candles', `look at flowers', because each look is what puts the next clue where
it can be taken, and then `levitate' reveals a second map object whose presence
in your inventory is what the room's `afterturn' tests before unlocking north.

Menus.  Two rooms ask `if ask <Do you want to save?>' on first entry -- the
Living Room and Outside the Pub -- and in geas as in Quest a pending prompt eats
the next line of input, so each is followed by an explicit "no".  `take map'
after `levitate' is a disambiguation rather than a question: the map from the
desk is `Map to The Dark Hills' with `alias <map>' and the new one is plainly
called `Map', so both answer to "map" and the answer 2 picks the new one.  Get
any of the three wrong and nothing complains; the following command is silently
spent as the answer and the route falls apart a few turns later.  Hugo Sawyer's
`speak' script has four `wait <Press any key to continue...>' pauses in it,
which the runner steps through without needing script lines.

The fork.  Zephyrus Way offers three ways to the Dark Hills and they are real
alternatives, all ending in the same room.  North is the longest: The Road Less
Traveled, a log cabin that wants a first aid kit, a journal and a sewing kit, the
Witch's Inn, and then The River, where the boat has a hole in it and the *towel*
is the plug -- so the north branch and the towel-as-weapon are mutually
exclusive.  Northeast is Dead Mans path, a chain of four timed fights (snakes,
eagle, bear, and the Black Beast of Epsilon, each one's death starting the next
one's On timer) followed by the Supherous of the Enchanted Oasis, which hits for
20.  This script takes the northwest branch, Fates Passage, because it is the
one that turns those three household objects into the tribal leader's necklace,
shield and deer meat: "if you want necklace, you must first give me boom boom
box" is the radio, "mystic smoke" is the baby powder and "happy pills" are the
tylenol, and the Campsite's `afterturn' unlocks northeast once all three are in
hand.  Then the graveyard: `speak to hugo sawyer' for the errand, `look for
grave stone' (the author wrote that one command twelve times over, with every
combination of search/look, grave/grave stone/tomb stone and an optional
article), `take shovel', `use shovel on grave stone' for the ring, and
`give ring to hugo sawyer', whose script does the unlock east into the Dark
Hills.

Engine notes.  Nothing here needed a change, which is worth recording because
its sequel did: this game hides 45 objects and every one of them uses the bare
`hidden' tag, so the load-time rule that Revenge of the Shadow Masters ran
afoul of -- Quest undoing a hidden that arrived through a `properties' line,
V4Game.Part2.cs:3550-3553, pinned by fixtures/hiddenprops.asl -- never comes up.
The bare tags are doing real work, too: the baby powder and the tylenol are
hidden inside the medicine cabinet and only `open medicine cabinet' reveals
them.  Unused here but present in the file: `command <list rooms>' and
`command <list objects>' are cheat menus that can hand over items or `goto' the
final room outright, and `Take all' is implemented with
`for each object in <#quest.currentroom#>'.  The typos in the transcript
("expline", "amazment", "gound", "vivid remind", "too quite") are the author's,
as is the habit of describing exits in prose that does not match the compass
directions that actually work.

Verified: 139 turns, 142 script lines (three of them eaten by the two save
prompts and the map disambiguation), marker "defeated him", seed 1, run with
--tick; reaches THE END and the game stops.
