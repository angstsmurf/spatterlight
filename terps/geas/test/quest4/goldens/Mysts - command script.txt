Mysts (mysts.asl, by Mihalis "DarkAng3l" Georgostathis, QDK 3.1, asl-version 310)
=================================================================================

A short Lovecraftian piece set on the coast of the Peloponnese: your master
Athinodoros has brought you to an abandoned cape and ordered you up the cliff to
toll the bell in the black temple at the top.  It is winnable in ten moves, and
its own win text calls it what it is -- "End of the preview.", which is the
marker this script checks.

The whole game is one errand with one obstacle: you cannot reach the bell, so you
have to throw something at it.  The stone is in the cave, and the cave is off the
plateau at the top of the *left* path, while the temple is at the top of the
*right* one:

  Beach -W-> Lower path -N-> Left (lower) -W-> Left (middle) -W-> Left (higher)
        -N-> Plateau -W-> Cave                     [the vision hands you a stone]
  Lower path -S-> Right (lower) -W-> Right (middle) -W-> Right (higher)
        -N-> Top of the cliff (right) -W-> Outside building -N-> Inside building
  USE STONE ON BELL                                             [playerwin]

Note the geography is deliberately unhelpful and the script walks all of it: the
left path only ever reaches the plateau ("No path leads further up"), and from
the right path the *south* fork -- Top of the cliff (left side) -- is a dead end
across a canyon from the temple, with nothing there but the view and the way
back.  The two paths never meet at the top.

The stone is not lying about; it comes from a set piece.  Entering the Cave runs
a room `script` that fires while you do not have the stone: the Orama1 vision, in
which you are cornered by two red eyes in a distorted version of the cave and
"reach for a stone", and you come out of it holding one.  The same script swaps
your mood -- the game tracks it as carried objects, `give <upset>` / `lose <calm>`
followed by `move <calm; feelings>` to shove the old mood into an off-stage room
called "feelings" -- which is why the inventory reads "upset" and "stone" and
never anything as helpful as a list of exits.

The script then walks WEST from the cave into the room called Spook, which the
author wrote as a one-way trap: it prints the screaming-in-the-dark scene, sends
you back to the Plateau and leaves you `frightened`.  Once you are, the Cave's
description checks for it and bounces you straight out again -- "On no way should
you enter this vile cavern." -- and this is the only thing in the game that can
lock content away, so the script visits Spook only after the stone is safely in
hand.  (It is not actually fatal: the Cave's script runs on entry regardless of
the bounce, so a player who somehow lost the stone would be handed another one
along with a repeat of the vision.  Try dropping it instead and you get the
stone's own `drop everywhere <Who needs it?>`, and no way to pick it up again --
it has no `take` line at all, so "You can't take it.")

Everything else here is scenery the script looks at on the way: your master, who
cannot be spoken to ("You wouldn't dare"), the bushes on the right path, the
statue in the temple (too big to carry, and you "wouldn't even dare to touch
it"), and the bell itself.  Tolling it is the end: the statue comes alive behind
you, the fog uncovers an island that was not in the bay before, and you wake on
a boat heading for it with your master and four strangers.
