Barbarian (by Wonderjudge, QDK Pro 4.1.1, ASL 410)
- raw command script for geas_walkthrough_runner.
Game system: Quest 4.  Win marker: "You have finished Barbarian!".
Needs GEAS_SEED=1: eight scripted fights are won with fixed menu answers, and
one five-stage trap sequence has a fixed answer per stage.
Final score 250 out of 250, in 508 turns (636 script lines; the extra
lines are menu answers, which the runner does not count as turns).

You are Tandum, heir to the chieftainship of the village of Golsiv.  Thorgin,
the shifty elder holding the seat in your stead, sets you three tasks he expects
you to die attempting; Councilman Hardin, your late father's friend, hands out
the destinations.  The tasks are a jewel in a graveyard crypt, a dragon scale on
Fang mountain, and - once you have worked out that Thorgin is selling the
village to the Red Scorpions - Thorgin himself, in a duel to the death on the
proving grounds.  Everything is one continuous run; there are no section markers
in the script body, because a `!` line after Start: would be sent as a command.

WHY EVERY OPTIONAL FIGHT IS COMPULSORY.  Winning a fight gives +1 health and +1
maxhealth, and Gak the blacksmith will only teach a new attack once maxhealth
passes a threshold: Cleave at >11, Unseat at >13, Behead at >15, Vault at >16
(the last also being the game's last 10 points).  The final duel opens with a
mounted phase whose loop is `repeat until not flag <enemymounted>`, and the only
thing in the whole procedure that clears that flag - short of your own death -
is the Vault (`define procedure <thorgincombat>`).  So the game is unwinnable
unless maxhealth reaches 17 before the council scene, which means the orc, the
bear, the graveyard skeleton, the crypt skeletons, the mercenary, the six
goblins and the ogre all have to be beaten.  Hence lines 105-125 and 130-150:
the merchant's orc and the mounted bear look optional and are not.

COMBAT IS FULLY DETERMINISTIC.  Every round draws one `$rand(1; 4)$` for the
enemy's response, and geas draws through `erkyrath_random()` (xoshiro128**, the
same generator the Spatterlight build uses) seeded from GEAS_SEED, so under
GEAS_SEED=1 the whole stream is known in advance.  `combat` and `mcombat`
consume one draw per round; `multicombat` (two foes at once, used by the crypt
skeletons and the goblin lair) consumes two, the second one unconditionally.
Your menu answer is read *before* that round's draw, so a fight can be planned
offline round by round, and because no non-combat turn touches the generator,
inserting or removing map moves anywhere in the script cannot invalidate a plan.
Changing the *number of rounds* a fight takes does, though - every round eats a
draw - so the eight blocks below were planned in order, each one behind the
already-fixed blocks in front of it.  Two tactical facts drive most of the
sequences: a thrust (2) does no damage but leaves the enemy off balance, and a
Cleave (4) against an off-balance enemy does 6, so 2-then-4 is six damage every
two rounds at no risk; and a thrust is harmless against every enemy response
except 3, 9 and 10, so against a foe whose mode table avoids those three it is a
free pass whenever the round would otherwise cost health.

  1. Golsiv, day one (lines 1-104).  The vase on the parlour mantle has to be
     SHAKEn and then BROKEn for the chisel; the chisel opens your own bedroom
     window from the alley, which is the only way to the pile of clothes.  Gak
     gives you the battle axe, Selma the seer sells the green potion, Turner
     sells the vegetables, Tessi the cook turns a tomato and a lettuce into the
     cabbage, and the cabbage is the one thing the wild horse in the corner
     stall will eat.  Hardin's "Hi there Hardin!" at 103-104 only checks the axe,
     but that is what unlocks the roads west and south out of the crossroads.
  2. The orc and the bear (105-129).  The traveling merchant on the western
     road is being robbed: `attack orc` then 3,1,1,1,1.  The black bear on the
     southern road is fought from horseback: 1,1,1,2,1,2.  Take the head and the
     skin.
  3. Armour (130-156).  Gak makes the studs, Inga the tanner turns studs + skin
     into the studded leather and the bear head into a helmet.  With axe, horse
     and armour all three, Hardin creates the exit to Chantly graveyard - a
     "place" exit, so the command is `go to chantly graveyard`, not the bare
     name.
  4. Chantly graveyard (157-200).  EXAMINE GRAVESTONES in each of the nine
     plots turns up the props; the tree branch, the cloth and the flint and
     steel make the torch.  Pressing the plate in each of the four corner
     graveyards opens the crypt in the middle, and pressing them again in the
     same order at 291-302 is what reopens the gate on the way out.  The
     graveyard skeleton: 2,4,1.  TAKE PILE OF BONES - those bones are used
     three separate times later and are never consumed.
  5. The crypt (201-249).  Four tombs, each with a coffin and a rock.  EXAMINE
     COFFIN in a tomb before you can do anything with it; the mummy in Tomb2
     wants the torch (its timer kills you in a handful of turns otherwise) and
     leaves the silver coin and the dusty scroll.  Each rock has to be carried
     to the coffin of a *different* tomb - the four PUT ROCK IN COFFIN lines
     are the long walks at 217-249.
  6. The ring puzzle (254-261).  EXAMINE STONE DOOR, answer yes, then the six
     rings in the order 1,3,3,5,5,7.
  7. The Eye of the East (262-272).  The statue holds the wooden shield; the
     large tomb holds the sack.  PUT PILE OF BONES IN SACK, then USE SACK ON
     PEDESTAL: the pedestal will only trade the eye for the sack while
     `is <#Pile of bones:parent#; Sack>` holds, and springs a lethal trap
     otherwise.  You must be wielding the shield when the red cloaked figure
     appears in the doorway, hence the axe/shield swap either side of it.
  8. Out of the crypt (273-290).  PULL LEVER, then the two crypt skeletons in
     `multicombat`: 4,4,4,1,1,3.  The Note they leave is the first of the
     three pieces of evidence.  UNWIELD TORCH is safe here and only here: the
     game block's afterturn drops any unwielded torch and it goes out, and the
     crypt is behind you.
  9. Night one, and Thorgin's second task (303-358).  EXAMINE RUGS in the
     chieftan's chamber for the Parchment (evidence two); Marta puts you up for
     the night; in the morning Thorgin takes the eye, Gak teaches the Cleave,
     and Selma and Hardin between them point you at the wizard's tower.
 10. The wizard and the mercenary (359-386).  KNOCK ON DOORS, GIVE DUSTY SCROLL
     TO WIZARD for the blue potion (the only thing that will get you past the
     dragon's breath).  The imposing figure on the way to Fang mountain is a
     Red Scorpion assassin, met on horseback: 2,1,5,4,1,4.  He unhorses you in
     round 2, which swaps the four-option mounted menu for the six-option one -
     so the 5 in round 3 is Unseat, knocking *him* off his horse and off
     balance, and the Cleave that follows lands for 6.  EXAMINE DEAD MAN, then
     TAKE EYE OF THE EAST - that second eye is evidence three, and the game
     will not let you leave until you have searched him.
 11. Spiked boots (387-409).  Gak's spikes plus Inga make the spiked boots,
     without which the ice on the great stair is fatal.
 12. Fang mountain and Fred (410-441).  USE PILE OF BONES ON IRON DOOR, DRINK
     BLUE POTION *before* entering the cavern (its script kills you outright
     without the potion's 40-turn timer), then SPEAK TO DRAGON and say yes:
     Fred is friendly, and will hand over a scale in exchange for the two
     treasures the goblins and the wizards stole from him.  The first is behind
     the wooden door: the goblin lair, six goblins in three pairs, sixteen
     rounds of `multicombat`, 1,1,4,1,4,4,3,1,1,4,1,1,1,3,4,3, at no cost in
     health.
 13. The altar trap (442-448).  REMOVE LARGE RUBY FROM LARGE ALTAR, yes, and
     then five dodges in a row - 1 (shield up, against the darts), 4 (duck,
     ceiling blade), 5 (jump, blade at your feet), 2 (dodge, the axe from the
     altar top), 3 (run, the ceiling comes down).  Every other answer at every
     stage is instant death.
 14. The wizards' diamond (449-477).  Fred trades the ruby for the magic ring.
     Through the portal: USE PILE OF BONES ON IRON DOOR again, take the diamond
     off the pedestal to raise the portcullis, and RUB MAGIC RING *before*
     putting it back - the alarm brings a red cloaked figure whose magical
     flames are survivable only while the ring's own 40-turn timer is running.
     EXAMINE DEAD WIZARD reveals the lever; USE CHISEL ON LEVER jams the
     portcullis open so the diamond can leave the room.  USE BATTLE AXE ON
     CEILING at the end of the tunnel brings it down behind you and stops the
     pursuit timer - going east with the diamond instead is death.
 15. Home with the scale (478-505).  Fred *tosses* the scale on the floor, so
     TAKE DRAGON SCALE is not optional: the crossroads nightfall scene checks
     `got <Dragon scale>` and packs you straight back to Fang mountain without
     it.  Hardin's second option is the accusation; Marta's daughter Megan is
     missing, and Gak's "other way in" hint reveals the loose stone behind the
     great hall fireplace.
 16. The hedge maze (506-548).  Twenty rooms and four dead ends, each dead end
     holding one part of the well winch: metal bar, chain, bucket, handle.  USE
     CHAIN ON METAL BAR, USE BUCKET ON BAR AND CHAIN, USE HANDLE ON BAR CHAIN
     AND BUCKET, USE BAR CHAIN AND BUCKET ON WELL.  Each stage re-parents the
     handle to the new well object and TURN HANDLE checks
     `is <#handle:parent#; Well4>`, so the four USEs have to be done in exactly
     this order.
 17. Carrying Megan out (549-578).  Megan refuses to be picked up while
     anything at all is wielded, so both the axe and the shield go away first;
     `wield` refuses while you are carrying her, so they stay away.  Going down
     at Hedge maze6 while carrying her makes her hand over the iron key, which
     unlocks the gate at Hedge maze1 - she is dropped for the ogre fight and
     collected again afterwards.
 18. The ogre (579-600).  `attack ogre` then 2,2,4,4,4,2,2,2,2,2,2,3,4,2,4:
     fifteen rounds, no damage taken at all.  Its mode table is 1,5,7,11, and
     none of those four is one of the three responses that can punish a thrust,
     so every round whose roll cannot be turned into damage is answered with a
     2 and simply costs nothing - the club arc (11) in particular is deflected
     outright.  Nine of the fifteen rounds are that stall.  Then back for Megan
     and south twice to the village center, where walking in with her while
     Marta is there is the reunion: +40 points and health restored to
     maxhealth.
 19. The council (601-613).  Gak teaches the Behead and the Vault (maxhealth is
     17 by now), Thorgin concedes the second task, and in the dining hall you
     SPEAK TO MEMBERS OF THE COUNCIL first - the give handlers all refuse until
     you have addressed them - then hand over the note, the parchment and the
     eye.  Marta and Megan are the fourth piece of evidence, and Thorgin throws
     down the gauntlet.
 20. The duel (614-636).  Mount up, wield the axe *and* the shield, and say yes
     to Hardin at the crossroads.  Mounted phase: answer 4, the Vault, which
     unhorses both of you in one round and is the only exit from that loop.  On
     foot, Thorgin wears a healing ring - the round his health is found below
     10 he jumps back and resets it to 25 - so no amount of damage can kill
     him.  What the heal does do is set the `hack1` flag, and in that one
     following round every attack lands unopposed: a thrust knocks him off
     balance, and a Behead against an off-balance opponent sets his health to 0
     outright.  Hence 4,4,1,4,4,3,4,4,2,6: chip him from 25 to 9, spend the
     heal round, thrust, behead.  The shield is worth the second wield slot -
     eight branches of the duel check `got <Wooden shield (wielded)>` and turn
     a wound into a clean block - and even so the duel is the only fight after
     the crypt that costs health, 17 down to 13.

Two engine divergences turned up here, both now fixed with fixtures.  The
game's own `unwield` command computes `$left(#command1#; %length1%)$` over
"Battle axe (wielded)" to get back to "Battle axe"; geas ended the argument
list at the *first* right paren, so the argument list collapsed to one item,
the function returned nothing, and the weapon simply vanished instead of being
handed back.  Quest's DoFunction takes the last one (InStrRev, V4Game.cs:6745).
And Quest records containment as the child's "parent" property, which games
read back as `#child:parent#` - both the crypt pedestal and the hedge-maze well
winch are built on that check, and geas kept containment only in its own
parent field, so neither puzzle could ever match.
