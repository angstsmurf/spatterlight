Fantasy BriceBall, Episode 1: the 2002 Revival -- Brice R. Binder, ASL 400
==========================================================================

A fetch quest across an eleven-room map of Alameda, California.  You are a
foreign exchange student in Mr. Simpson's ESL class; your tutor Brice mentions
the lost sport of BriceBall; you then recruit four more players and gather six
props, and walk onto the field.  There is nothing to solve and nothing to fail
at except one thing (see below), so the script is a route, not a solution.

The win is a room script, so it fires on entry to BriceBall Memorial Field:

    script if got <Brice> and got <old baseball bat> and got <three old tennis
      balls> and got <pen> and got <backstop> and got <tow rope> and got <Corey>
      and got <Ivana> and got <Hector> and got <Cynthia> and got <official
      BriceBall scorecard> then playerwin
      else msg <You are not ready to play yet. ...>

`got` is inventory, and the four recruits are ordinary objects you TAKE -- which
is where the one way to lose comes in: every character carries

    drop playerlose

so DROP anyone, or INVENTORY-shuffle carelessly, and the game ends with "The only
rule of this game is to keep your most precious commodity: PLAYERS!"  The field
can be re-entered as often as you like, though: a premature visit only prints the
"not ready" message.

The list
--------
SPEAK TO BRICE is a four-screen conversation ending in `reveal <List>`, and the
List's description is the checklist: a bat, tennis balls, a backstop, a blank
scorecard, a pen, a rope.  It is pure documentation -- nothing checks whether you
ever read it, and the recruits are not on it at all.  The four `wait <Hit any key
to continue|cb>` pauses inside the speak script do not consume a script line, so
each SPEAK is a single command here.

Two of the six props are inside containers, both `opened` and both needing a LOOK
before the object exists to take:

    define object <garbage can>          (Tennis Courts)
        look { reveal <three old tennis balls>  remove <three old tennis balls> }

    define object <Brice's car>          (Parking Lot)
        look { remove <backstop>  msg <Brice opens his trunk...> }

The garbage can's look script has no `msg` at all, so LOOK AT GARBAGE CAN prints
nothing whatsoever and then the balls are takeable -- easy to read as a dead end.
Both objects also declare `parent`, so before the reveal/remove they are listed
as container contents rather than as room objects.

Red herrings
------------
Three characters cannot be recruited and have no TAKE script: Bill in the Velour
Grill ("Go back to where you came from!"), Mr. Simpson in the classroom, and
Betty on Otis Drive, whose "I'll do anything for fifty bucks!" is followed by the
author's own aside, "** You don't have $50 **".  There is no money in the game.

The route below is a single loop that ends in the Parking Lot, so the two
container props are picked up last and the final move east is the win.

Pickups in order: Brice and Ivana and Hector (talk, then take), the pen at home,
Cynthia on Lincoln Avenue, the tow rope in the Hardware Store, the bat in
Franklin Park, the scorecard in Brice's room, Corey at the Velour Grill, the
tennis balls at the courts, the backstop from the car.
