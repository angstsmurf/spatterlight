Prison Break
Prison Break.asl, ASL 410, "Created with QDK 4.1.5", Roy Wong, copyright "No
rights reserved."

You are Boyboy, a teddy bear, taped shut inside a cardboard box in a storage room
on Christmas Eve because your owner Freda does not want you any more.  Dungdung
the cat claws you out and recruits you.  Three rooms, no score, no timers, no
randomness, and -- unusually -- no way to lose.  65 turns below, which is the
whole game plus every SPEAK TO joke.

The marker is the last line of the escape ending.  Neither ending uses `playerwin`
or `playerlose`; both finish with a bare `stop`.


THERE ARE NO COMPASS DIRECTIONS

Travel is three game-level commands, each gated on a flag:

    command <go to kitchen>       requires flag <opendoor>
    command <go to sitting room>  requires flag <speakkdd3>
    command <go to dark room>     always refuses: "There is no point going back
                                  there."

Everything else is state held in flags and read back out through the rooms'
`description` blocks.  The Storage Room's description is a five-deep nested
`if not flag` ladder, and each rung prints the nudge for whatever you are
supposed to do next ("You really should thank Dungdung for her help first.",
"The room is too dark.  You cannot see much.", "You see the key being trapped
between two giant cardboard boxes.").  In this game LOOK is the hint system, and
it is also the trigger: the first LOOK after thanking Dungdung is what reveals
the toy arrow, the glasses and the matches, and the first LOOK in the kitchen
after talking to her is what reveals the pen and the paper.


PUZZLE ONE: THE STORAGE ROOM

  SPEAK TO MYSTERY VOICE                -> she becomes Dungdung
  SPEAK TO DUNGDUNG (four-deep menu)    -> she cuts the tape; you are out
  SPEAK TO DUNGDUNG                     -> thank her
  LOOK                                  -> toy arrow, glasses, matches
  USE MATCHES                           -> reveals a scented candle, out of reach
                                           on top of a large box
  USE TOY ARROW ON CANDLE               -> knocks it to the floor
  USE MATCHES ON CANDLE                 -> light; reveals the key, wedged between
                                           two boxes the two of you cannot move
  USE TOY ARROW ON GLASSES              -> suction cup pressed onto the lens =
                                           one long reaching hook
  USE LONG HOOK ON KEY                  -> you have the key
  USE KEY                               -> Dungdung stands on your shoulders and
                                           opens the door
  GO TO KITCHEN

USE TOY ARROW ON KEY before building the hook gets the explicit hint ("still too
short... You may need to use the arrow on something to extend its length"), which
is in the script because it is the only clue for the combination.


PUZZLE TWO: THE KITCHEN

  SPEAK TO DUNGDUNG (three-deep menu)   -> she admits nothing much
  USE KEY                               -> the storeroom key was the cabinet key
                                           all along; you get a can of salmon
  LOOK                                  -> pen, paper
  USE PEN ON CANNED SALMON              -> you jump on it as a lever and snap it;
                                           leaves the ink cartridge, and Dungdung
                                           points out a locked glass cupboard with
                                           a butter knife on the top shelf
  USE PAPER ON CUPBOARD                 -> slid under the door as a landing pad
  USE INK CARTRIDGE ON KNIFE            -> pushed off the shelf onto the paper,
                                           pull the paper back out
  USE KNIFE ON CANNED SALMON            -> open
  SPEAK TO DUNGDUNG                     -> she hands you "the entrance key"
  GO TO SITTING ROOM

The paper is the one ordering constraint in the game, and even that is signposted:
USE INK CARTRIDGE ON KNIFE with no paper in place tells you exactly what is
missing.  The script does it in the wrong order first so the refusal shows up in
the transcript.

READ PAPER is the only content behind any of the eleven blanket verbs, and it is a
nice touch: it is Freda's moving checklist ("cardboard box X 20 / duck tape X 2 /
book lorry / get key / get sofa / more paints / check holidays"), it is the same
sheet you shove under the cupboard door, and the ending has her picking it up off
the kitchen floor and adding "kitchen wraping paper" to it.


THE ENDGAME IS A CHOOSE-YOUR-OWN-ADVENTURE

Entering the Sitting Room is not parser-driven at all.  The room's description
prints, waits for a key, and then runs `choose <escape1>`; ten selections
(escape1 through escape12) form a branching tree, and every leaf either descends
further or `goto`s one of the two ending rooms.  The path below is:

  escape1   sneak to the corner where the Pucca doll is    -> escape2
  escape2   tell Pucca to take care of Freda for you       -> escape5
  escape5   hide behind the bookshelf                      -> escape7
  escape7   sneak behind the coat rack                     -> escape8
  escape8   the key wouldn't work, climb out the window    -> escape11
  escape11  climb out the window and leave                 -> Somewhere You Belong

Only that one leaf reaches the escape ending.  "Make a run to the door!" at
escape6/7 puts you in escape9, where Freda sees you; escape9 and escape12 are
mostly direct jumps to the other ending.  The best joke in the tree is escape10:
if you choose to unlock the front door with the key Dungdung gave you, the key
does not work -- because it is the kitchen cabinet key again, which the game has
been hinting at since she palmed it to you while eating your salmon.

Both endings are real endings, not a win and a loss.

  Ending: Somewhere You Belong -- you climb out, Freda sees you go and does
    nothing, and you lie down in an alley wishing never to wake up.  A poor man
    finds you and gives you to his daughter Ameli for Christmas.

  Ending: The Truth (alias "Ending: The Abandoned Doll") -- Freda picks you up.
    The box was for moving house, the "truck people" are movers, she is taking you
    with her, and the doll being left behind is Pucca.  It is longer and better
    written than the escape, and it is what you get for every wrong answer in the
    tree, which reads like the point.


AUTHORING NOTES

  * State changes are done by swapping whole objects rather than editing one.
    There are four objects called "key" (`key`, then `key2`/`key3`/`key4` each
    carrying `alias <key>`), three called Dungdung, two papers and two knives.
    Each swap is a `hide`/`show`/`give` triple.
  * The candle's `use` tag, its `action <light>` tag and the matches' `use on
    <candle>` tag are three verbatim copies of the same twelve-line script, and
    the matches' `use` and `action <light>` are two more.
  * The help text exists three times -- `command <help#something#>`,
    `command <help>`, and inline in the Storage Room's first description -- with
    the examples reworded ("kiss baby", "eat candy", "kick dog" in the room copy
    versus "play piano", "eat candy", "read newspaper" in the command copy).
  * Eleven verbs (wear, put on, shoot, punch, kick, kiss, eat, light, kill, read,
    open, break, insert) are declared at game level with a blanket refusal, and
    the objects that answer them do so through `properties <punch = ...>` lists.
    Dungdung has two different sets, one per room: in the storage room she "seems
    really mad" if you kick her, and in the kitchen -- after she has got what she
    wanted out of you -- she dodges and jokes about it.  That is why PUNCH
    DUNGDUNG is in the script.
  * Every single object answers SPEAK TO with a line in its own voice.  The toy
    arrow is embarrassed about its suction cup, the ink cartridge hates you for
    breaking its body, the butter knife wants out of the cupboard as much as you
    want out of the house, and all four keys insist there is no escape.
  * `command <the #something#>` and `error <badcommand; ...>` both exist to catch
    players who type "use A on the B".  There is also a
    `command <fuck#word#;rape#word#> msg <You shouldn't say something like that.>`
  * The in-game instructions describe `panes on`, an inventory box in the upper
    right, and browsing your command history with the up arrow -- the Quest 4
    Windows runner's UI.  Where the game says "press the up arrow until you see
    'speak to d'", this script just types the command.
