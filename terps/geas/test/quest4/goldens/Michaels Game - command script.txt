Michael's Game -- command script
================================

Game:      Michael's Game  (games/Michaels Game.asl)
Author:    "Connie Strump"
Copyright: "Groupon Inc 0169"
Engine:    ASL 410, QDK 4.1.4 (the `game info` line claims QDK Pro 4.1.2)
Marker:    "DOOR TO THE END OF THIS DUMB GAME unguarded"
Length:    172 turns (173 script lines -- one of them answers the art quiz)

*** THIS GAME CANNOT BE FINISHED.  See "THE DEAD FINAL DOOR" below. ***
The marker above is the furthest reachable state: every puzzle in the game is
solved, the guard is asleep and the final door is unlocked.  Walking through it
does nothing, in geas and in real Quest 4 alike.

WHAT THIS GAME IS
-----------------
An office in-joke, written at Groupon and lightly filed off as "Gloopon".  The
framing: you are Michael, a child who has been raised in a single room by the
company, and the narrator is the company -- relentlessly condescending, forever
reminding you how lucky you are and how dangerous everywhere else is.  The
joke underneath is that Michael himself has hidden six notes through the game
he was given as a teaching tool, and they are a smuggled plea for help ("HELLO
GLOOPONS. ITS ME MICHAEL. IM SO SORRY... SO BEAT THE GAME SO YOU CAN STOP ME").
Eleven rooms, a five-step chemistry puzzle, and a great deal of swearing.

The map is the game's one genuinely good joke, and it is also a puzzle answer.
Five rooms form a north-south spine, and three two-room arms run east off the
top, middle and bottom of it:

    Jungle Room ---- The Louvre ---- Underwater Room
        |
    Public Employee Room
        |
    The Library ---- Gloopon office ---- Michael's Room
        |
    Independence Hall
        |
    The Science Lab ---- The Oval Office ---- The Moon

That is a capital **E**, and the answer to the guard's riddle ("assuming that
every room in this game is the same size, when you draw them out on a map what
letter do they make the shape of?") is E.  Exactly eleven rooms, which is
5 + 3x2 -- the author counted.

THE CRITICAL PATH
-----------------
Michael's Room (start):
  TURN SILVER HANDLE (on the toilet) drops a CD out of the ceiling.  Take it.
  USE CD ON MICHAEL'S COMPUTER shoots a Key out of the disc drive.  Take it.
  USE KEY unlocks the door west.  TYPE PASSWORD123 is optional -- it is
  Michael's message, and it is where the game's conceit is spelled out.

Gloopon office: TAKE GLITTER.  You need it much later, and it is the only
  object in the room that is not lethal.

Public Employee Room (Library north): SPEAK TO POLICE OFFICER, SPEAK TO
  FIREMAN, SPEAK TO CONSTRUCTION WORKER.  Each hands over one item -- a note,
  the LIGHTER, and the HAMMER -- and the game apologises for using them as a
  shortcut ("These people are great and not at all a shortcut for the game
  programmer in giving you clues").

The Library: SMASH GLASS CASE with the hammer, then TAKE MACHETE.
  LOOK AT OTHER BOOKSHELF is what reveals A NOTE FROM MICHAEL.

Back in Michael's Room, now that you have the lighter:
  BURN MONKEY POSTER reveals the Louvre key (called "Gold Key" in the text).
  BURN SHARK POSTER reveals the Glass of milk.  Take both.  The Glass of milk
  is one of the guard's three sleep items, so this pair of arson jokes is on
  the critical path.

Jungle Room (Public Employee Room north): TAKE SHOVEL, then DIG SHOVEL -- and
  the shovel's own description apologises for the phrasing: "because of this
  dumb program I have to tell you that to dig with this thing you have to type
  'dig shovel'.  I am sorry about this."  HACK VINES with the machete unlocks
  the door east.

The Louvre: LOOK AT ARTS starts a five-option art quiz; the right answer is
  option 4, `dbface`.  It reveals A FOURTH NOTE FROM MICHAEL, and taking that
  note while holding both the Louvre key and the Glass of milk triggers the
  ghost who tells you the checkpoint password ("say goodbye, english
  breakfast", which teleports you here with your inventory intact if you die).
  USE LOUVRE KEY unlocks the door east.

Underwater Room: a 60-second drown timer starts the moment you arrive, the
  door behind you is re-locked by the room's own script, and the way out is
  inside a chest.  TAKE BRASS KEY first (it is loose in the room and it is the
  only way into the bottom third of the map).  Then OPEN CHEST, which offers
  "a gold key, a silver key, and a pretzel key" and lets you take exactly one
  -- taking any one hides the other two.  Take the GOLD KEY: USE GOLD KEY
  unlocks the west door.  The other two are traps in the cruellest possible
  way; see "THE CHEST" below.

The Library: USE BRASS KEY unlocks the door south.

Independence Hall: SPEAK TO GEORGE WASHINGTON, then TAKE THING (out from under
  his hat) for the sixth note and the hint about "my wife's nickname according
  to Groupon Says".

The Science Lab.  Take calcium, pieces of aluminum, dog bones, cinnamon and
  the highlighter, then run the recipe the six notes spell out:
    COMBINE CALCIUM AND PIECES OF ALUMINUM        -> calcuminum
    COMBINE DOG BONES AND HIGHLIGHTER             -> fluorescent bone paste
    PUT FLUORESCENT BONE PASTE OVER BUNSEN BURNER -> science crystal
    COMBINE CALCUMINUM AND CINNAMON               -> cinnamonium
    COMBINE CINNAMONIUM AND SCIENCE CRYSTAL       -> unobtainium
    COMBINE GLITTER AND UNOBTAINIUM               -> less boring unobtainium
  Then GIVE LESS BORING UNOBTAINIUM TO REYNOLD, which unlocks the door east.
  Both the verb and the name matter here; see "TWO WAYS THE SCIENCE LAB LIES"
  below.  Giving him the plain unobtainium first is worth doing once for the
  rejection speech ("sure it's a new element and all but it's soooo boring").

The Oval Office: SAY BETSY ROSS drops the OLD TIMEY SLEEP HAT out of a secret
  ceiling panel.  Take it.  Do not push the big red button.

The Moon: the room description asks a yes-or-no question ("Was the game
  designer to lazy to set up a challenge in this room?").  Answer YES and a
  teddy bear appears.  Take it.

The Library, finally: GIVE GLASS OF MILK TO GUARD, GIVE OLD TIMEY SLEEP HAT TO
  GUARD, GIVE TEDDY BEAR TO GUARD.  He then poses the map riddle, and SAY E
  puts him to sleep and unlocks the door west -- "leaving this DOOR TO THE END
  OF THIS DUMB GAME unguarded".  That is as far as the game goes.

THE DEAD FINAL DOOR
-------------------
The Library declares its west exit as one line:

  west locked <...guard won't let you> locked <...> locked <...> msg <You've beaten the game>

Quest parses a directional exit tag by taking the direction, then an optional
`locked`, then -- if what remains starts with "<" -- the *first* <...> as the
tag's parameter and everything after that closing ">" as the exit's **script**
(RoomExits.AddExitFromTag, RoomExits.cs:141-186).  For a directional exit that
parameter is the lock message, never a destination.  So this exit is a locked
script exit whose lock message is the guard's refusal and whose script is the
single line

  locked <...> locked <...> msg <You've beaten the game>

and ExecuteScript splits a script on CRLF only, then dispatches one leading
keyword per line with BeginsWith (V4Game.Part2.cs:5686-5722).  The keyword here
is `locked`, which is not a script statement, so Quest logs "Unrecognized
keyword" and does nothing.  The `msg` is never reached.  The author wrote three
lock messages and the win text on one line; only the first survives, and the
win text is dead code.  There is no `playerwin` anywhere in the file.

geas reproduces this exactly -- `west` prints nothing and does not move -- and
fixtures/exitscript410.asl pins that behaviour along with the general
one-statement-per-line rule, so that nobody later "fixes" it into a loop.

TWO WAYS THE SCIENCE LAB LIES
-----------------------------
1. USE is a dead end; COMBINE is the real verb.  `calcium` carries an
   object-level `use <pieces of aluminum>` that reports success and does
   `give <calcuminium>` -- misspelled, with an extra i.  No object by that name
   exists, so USE CALCIUM ON PIECES OF ALUMINUM consumes both reagents and
   hands you nothing, permanently.  The game-level `combine #object1# and
   #object2#` command spells it `calcuminum` and works.  The notes from Michael
   all say "Combine", which is the only warning you get.

2. The robot's name decides whether you can win.  The game-level handler opens

     if ( #object1# = unobtainium ) and ( #object2# = reynold )
        or ( #object2# = R.E.Y.N.O.L.D. ) then { reject } else { accept... }

   Left-associated, that is `(A and B) or C`.  So *any* give addressed to
   "R.E.Y.N.O.L.D." takes the reject branch no matter what you are handing
   over, and the accept branch is unreachable through that name.  Addressing
   him as "reynold" makes C false, A false, and drops into the else.  This
   script therefore says GIVE LESS BORING UNOBTAINIUM TO REYNOLD.  Worth
   knowing that the same else branch holds all three of the guard's sleep-item
   handlers, which is why they work at all.

THE CHEST
---------
The Underwater Room's chest offers "a gold key, a silver key, and a pretzel
key" and lets you take one.  The silver key is an object named
`old timey sleep cap` and the pretzel key is an object named `teddy bear` --
namesakes of the two real sleep items, `old timey sleep hat` in the Oval Office
and `teddy bear1` on the Moon.  The guard's handlers test `got <old timey sleep
hat>` and `got <teddy bear1>`, so neither chest item can ever satisfy him.
Taking either one hides the gold key, which is the only thing that unlocks the
way out, and the room is on a 60-second drown timer.  There is also a
`skeleton key` behind the pirate skull whose use message is "Sorry, this key
don't to a damn thing", and a `key-shaped rock` that is "just a rock".  Four
decoy keys, one real one.

WAYS TO DIE
-----------
  * TOUCH COMPUTERS, TOUCH PHONES or TOUCH BREAKABLE THINGS in the Gloopon
    office -- the room description warns you off all three by name, and the
    computers one is the game explaining that "the real robots that we really
    installed in the real Groupon office come down and kill you".
  * TOUCH PLANT in the Library.
  * PUSH BUTTON in the Oval Office (every nuclear weapon on Earth).
  * USE CALCIUM ON CINNAMON, ON DOG BONES or ON HIGHLIGHTER -- "terrible poison
    that kills everything".  Note these are the object-level `use` handlers,
    i.e. the same wrong verb that silently eats your reagents in case 1 above.
  * Staying in the Underwater Room for 60 seconds (the `Drown` timer).  This
    harness does not advance timers, so the script is not racing it, but the
    route is written to leave promptly anyway.

AUTHORING QUIRKS
----------------
`say` always prints "No one can hear you. What a dweeb" first, whatever you
say, because the handler is `if (#text# = goodbye, english breakfast) then
{...} else msg <...>` followed by further un-elsed `if`s.  Those later `if`s
have the same broken precedence as the robot's: `say Betsy Ross` and `say E`
fire their branches regardless of the `flag <in oval>` / `flag <in library>`
guards the author meant to attach, because the `and flag <...>` binds only to
the last of four or-ed text comparisons.  The script still visits the right
rooms, since what the branches do is `show` an object that lives there.

Fourteen game-level `verb <x> msg <...>` fallbacks (burn, turn, touch, smash,
dig, hack, open, push, ride, "change channel on", "insert CD into", "turn
silver handle on", "type password123 on", and `ride ` again with a stray
trailing space) are answered by per-object `action <verb>` handlers -- the
stdverbs.lib idiom, and this game leans on it for nearly every puzzle, so the
script burns, turns, touches, smashes, digs, hacks and rides a lot of scenery.

Also deliberately included: BURN PAINTING, which is the only refusal with
feelings ("A single tear runs from the gentleman's eye. You monster!"); BURN
BED, whose refusal is "No! You can't burn this. Do your homework!"; RIDE
STRANGE BIKE; CHANGE CHANNEL ON TELEVISION ("good job reading the game
manual!"); LOOK AT A BOOK in the Underwater Room, which is the first paragraph
of Moby-Dick; and LOOK AT THE DECLARATION OF INDEPENDENCE, which is not the
Declaration of Independence but Bill Pullman's speech from Independence Day.
The three jungle animals all explode if you burn, smash or hack them.
