"The Former" (former.asl) -- Derek W., ASL 350
================================================================================
Command script for terps/geas/test/quest4/harness/run_walkthroughs.sh.

Verified: 231 turns, leftover 0, WON.  No flags needed -- there is not a single
timer or `rand` in the file.  The win marker is "have learned the whole truth",
from `define text <win>`.


WHY THIS GAME IS IN THE SUITE

It is a 74-room detective story built in QDK 3.53, and it is the largest
purely-deterministic ASL 350 game in the corpus.  You wake up in an abandoned
factory as robot 10-126 with your memory wiped, and spend the game interrogating
witnesses about two murders before discovering you committed them.  There are no
combat rolls, no chance factors and nothing on a clock, so every one of the 231
turns below is doing real work, and any divergence shows up immediately.

What it exercises that the rest of the corpus does not:

  * ten invisible marker objects tested in one enormous `if ... and ... and not
    ... then` (see below) -- the widest boolean condition in the corpus;
  * `place` rooms used as a conversation system.  Every witness is a room whose
    `description` is the menu of things you may ask, and every question is a
    room-scoped `command <...?>`, including four that only appear once a flag
    object exists.  Five witnesses, and two of them `goto` you back out of the
    conversation as their parting shot;
  * the show/hide-versus-reveal/conceal distinction, hard.  `exists` is the
    game's only state test, and the author is scrupulous about pairing
    `hide`+`conceal` and `show`+`reveal` -- except where he isn't, and those
    places are exactly where the interesting behaviour lives;
  * `Dead Body` -> `dead body 2` -> `Dead body 3` swap chains, all four bodies
    wearing the alias "Dead Body" so that X DEAD BODY means something different
    each time -- three deep in the landfill, four deep in the mansion office;
  * `give <Data Chips>` on three different NPCs with three different guards;
  * a room `script` that fires on entry and `goto`s you somewhere else (mine3);
  * one `create exit` firing from inside a condition evaluated in a *different*
    room -- the gate below opens the Science Center elevator while you are
    standing in the factory.

It also parses a fair amount of authoring debris without complaint: the `start`
room block ends with five bare `- Who am I?`-style lines (44-48) left over from
the author pasting his own menu into QDK, the mansion den contains a naked
`use <>` with no object and no body (1347), `define synonyms` is empty, and the
Landfill Compactor's `south` exit points at the Landfill Compactor (840).


THE TEN-MARKER GATE, AND WHY 15/15 CLUES CAN STILL LOSE

The game is in two acts.  Act 1 ends when you read the Journal in your bedroom
chest, which is a one-way `goto <Utility End>`; from there three forced moves
north put you back in the factory next to the PDA you killed, and examining it
runs the only decision in the game (room `end`, object `PDA2`, line 1639):

    if exists <clue-test9> and exists <clue- test 10> and not exists <test-clue>
      and exists <test-clue 3> and exists <test - clue 6> and exists <clue - test2>
      and not exists <clue -test 4> and exists <clue - test5>
      and not exists <clue - test 8> and exists <clue- test 7>
    then { create exit <scicenter S; Elevator> goto <end2> }
    else playerlose

Note the internal spacing: `clue-test9`, `clue- test 10`, `clue -test 4`,
`clue - test 8`.  Ten different objects, ten different ideas about where the
spaces go, and Quest object names are matched with the spacing intact -- so this
is also a decent test that the loader is not normalising whitespace inside
`<...>`.

Seven of them start `hidden invisible` (do not exist) and must be `show`n; three
of them -- `test-clue`, `clue -test 4`, `clue - test 8` -- start `invisible`
only (they *do* exist) and must be `hide`den.  That inversion is the whole trick:
the three you have to destroy are the three "prove a negative" beats of the
plot.  You hide `test-clue` by asking the repair droid what model the motherboard
came from, `clue -test 4` by reading the girl's diary, and `clue - test 8` by
reading her brother's ledger in his oil cellar.

Running alongside all of this is a completely separate counter, `CLUE`, displayed
as "Clues: !/15".  Nine of the ten markers bump it; six other examines bump it
too; and the total is exactly 15, so a full playthrough reads 15/15.  But the
counter is cosmetic -- the gate does not look at it.  The marker that does *not*
touch it is `test - clue 6`, and that is where the trap is.


THE TRAP: LIGHTING THE TORCH TOO EARLY MAKES THE GAME UNWINNABLE

`test - clue 6` is set by *failing*.  Walking east or west out of mine4 without a
light runs

    east {
        msg <The lack of light is becomng unbearable. ...>
        show <test - clue 6>
    }

and the marker exists purely as a record that you have bumped into the dark.  Its
visible purpose is to unlock the line "Do you sell flashlights here?" in the
repair droid's menu -- but that line is a plain room `command`, guarded only by
`if not exists <Wallet>`, so it can be typed by a player who has never set foot
in the mines.  Buy the torch first and then

    use <Flashlight> {
        create exit east <mine4; mine5>
        create exit west <mine4; mine6>
        msg <You click the flashlight on.>
    }

overwrites both scripted exits with plain text destinations -- at ASL < 410
`ExecuteCreateExit` assigns straight into the room's slot (`r.East.Data =
destRoom; r.East.Type = TextActionType.Text`, V4Game.cs:5433-5437), discarding
the script wholesale, exactly as in "Get out of the house!".  The
`show <test - clue 6>` is gone for good, the marker can never be set, and the
game is unwinnable from that moment on.

This is not theoretical, and it takes one edit to see: delete the single `east`
command that this script types in the dark (the one whose only output is "The lack
of light is becomng unbearable") and the run still collects all fifteen clues,
still reaches the PDA, and still ends in

    You have finished the game, but without learning the whole truth.
    Congradulations, I suppose.

So the script below deliberately walks into the dark once before buying the
flashlight, and then walks all the way back out to the repair shop and in again.
Those fourteen moves are not padding.


THE MONEY

`DC` ("Data Chips: !") has exactly two sources: the Chair Pocket in your living
room (+10) and the Wallet that falls out of the beureau in your back room (+50).
There are four sinks: the bartender's key (-5), the repair droid's briefing
(-20), the weapons lady's records (-20) and the flashlight (-10).  That is 60 in
and 55 out, with no slack for a second purchase, and `dec <DC; N>` never checks
whether you can afford it -- the counter would simply go negative.

The affordability test is not the counter, though.  It is

    give <Data Chips> if not exists <Wallet> then { ... } else msg <What, you
      think I'm going to tell you sensitive information for that little bit?>

on both the repair droid and the weapons lady, and the same guard on the
flashlight.  `take <Wallet>` does `conceal` + `hide` + `inc <DC; 50>`, so "not
exists <Wallet>" means "you have picked your wallet up".  Three of the four
purchases are therefore locked behind a room in the far corner of the map that
nothing in the game points you towards, which is why this script detours to the
office before going to the pub at all.  (The bartender has no such guard; his
five chips come out of the Chair Pocket.)


THE ROUTE

  1. Factory.  Ask Data all five questions off the opening menu, then south into
     fac S and GO TO PANTRY for the desk key and the box.  fac W: USE DESK KEY ON
     DESK, TAKE RUSTY KEY.
  2. Utility Region.  USE RUSTY KEY ON RECYCLING PLANT DOOR opens the west exit.
     In the plant, USE BOX ON KEY ON A STRING pops the box (the key is bolted to
     the wall, so the box has to come to it), USE DOOR KEY ON SMALL DOOR opens
     the parts closet, TAKE SERVICE DROID HEAD.  At the exit gate, USE SERVICE
     DROID HEAD ON UTILITY REGION GATE reads the dead droid's digital imprint and
     lets you out of the district.
  3. Industrial Region.  SPEAK TO CAR -- it opens its doors for you, which is how
     you learn it is yours, and `create exit east` gives you the rest of the
     island.  (`take` on the car refuses: stealing is outside your parameters.)
  4. Your house, 7th Street.  X FILES -> X LETTER is the map: eight `create exit`
     calls in one handler, wiring urban to street5, Commercial1, City1, the
     Science Center and the Housing Office.  X FILES again (it is now `Files2`
     wearing the same alias) turns up the Room Key.  Living room: GO TO CHAIR,
     X CHAIR POCKET, TAKE DATA CHIPS.
  5. Your office.  USE ROOM KEY opens the back room; X BEUREAU, TAKE WALLET.
     Everything expensive is now affordable.
  6. City Pub.  GO TO INTERROGATE BARTENDER, ask where the back door goes, then
     whether you may borrow the key, then GIVE DATA CHIPS TO BARTENDER.  OUT, GO
     TO INTERROGATE BARFLY, "have you seen me talking to anyone?" -- he names a
     short guy and passes out, which is `clue- test 10` and also
     `destroy exit <pub; interrogate - barfly>`, so it is one-shot.  USE BAR KEY
     in the pub itself opens the alley.
  7. Alleyway.  X LARGE DUMPSTER, TAKE MOTHERBOARD SHARDS (which hands you a fragment
     and creates the repair droid interrogation), X TINY MOTHERBOARD FRAGMENT --
     the flashback that `reveal`s the Dresser back in your bedroom.  The dresser
     is invisible until this examine, so the bedroom has to be visited *after*
     the alley, not before.
  8. Bedroom.  X DRESSER, TAKE RUSTY KEY.  (Both stairways in the house are
     objects whose `look` property does the moving, so the command is LOOK AT
     STAIRS, not UP.  Every staircase in the game works this way.)
  9. Landfill.  USE RUSTY KEY ON LANDFILL DOOR, east twice, X DEAD BODY twice.
     The first examine creates the weapons lady interrogation; the second reveals
     the word "Latter" carved into his arm, which is `Dead body 3` -- the object
     that unlocks a "Does the word latter mean anything to you?" line in every
     interrogation menu in the game and the `Latter` topic on your computer.
 10. Weapons Shop, via the Ghettos.  Ask about a weapon that could puncture a
     model 7 (a foiling lance), ask who bought one, GIVE DATA CHIPS TO WEAPONS
     LADY for `clue - test2`.  Only *then* ask her about the Latter: that handler
     ends with `goto <Ghettos2>`, so it throws you out of the shop and out of the
     conversation.
 11. Back room.  GO TO YOUR COMPUTER, search LATTER.  The flashback creates
     `create exit west <industrial2; Mine entrance2>` -- which overwrites the
     decoy `Mine entrance1` (a dead-end room that exists only to tell you not to
     go in without a reason).
 12. Mines, first pass.  west, west, south to mine4 and try EAST in the dark.
     See above; this is the whole reason for the trip.
 13. Repair Shop.  Ask what model the motherboard came from (hides `test-clue`),
     ask about the Latter, ask who they are, GIVE DATA CHIPS TO REPAIR DROID for
     `test-clue 3`, and DO YOU SELL FLASHLIGHTS HERE? for the torch.
 14. Mines, second pass.  USE FLASHLIGHT, then east to mine5: X MYSTERIOUS OBJECT
     -> X ROBOT ARM for `clue- test 7`.  Back west and around to mine7: X DEAD
     BODY reveals the girl's Diary, X DIARY hides `clue -test 4`.  Now walk back
     north into mine3, whose entry `script` sees that both the Diary and the Robot
     Arm exist, prints "The person who did it was... You.", sets `clue - test5`
     and `goto`s you to your office.
 15. office2 is a second copy of your office with a five-`wait` monologue, two
     paragraphs of which are conditional on your having bribed the lady and the
     droid.  It ends by creating the Housing Office interrogation.
 16. Housing Office -> "im looking for a model 5 on 3rd street" -> `create exit
     northwest <urban; street3>`, and 3rd Street is the mansion.
 17. Mansion.  X DOORBELL creates the front door.  The gold Key is on a pedestal
     in the upstairs bedroom; it unlocks the den's east door into the garage;
     LOOK AT WINDOW climbs out into the backyard; GO TO TERRACE, LOOK AT WINDOW
     again and you are in the locked office with the brother's body -- which is
     the point, because the door was locked from the inside and the killer went
     out the same window you came in.  X DEAD BODY three times: the first gives a
     clue and `show`s the Chest under your own dresser, the third turns up a
     skeleton key.  That key opens the den's cellar stairs (a `stairs3`/`stairs4`
     swap), and X LEDGER down there hides `clue - test 8`.
 18. Home.  X CHEST UNDERNEITH YOUR DRESSER (the object is `<Chest>`, but its
     alias is that whole phrase, misspelling and all -- former.asl:590-591),
     X LATTER H.Q. KEY (for `clue-test9`; the inscription reads
     "Elevator 5, Lobby in Science Center", which is the only hint about where
     the endgame is), TAKE LATTER H.Q. KEY, and only then X JOURNAL.  The Journal
     is four `wait`s of confession followed by `goto <Utility End>` and there is
     no way back; take the key first or act 2 is unwinnable.


ACT TWO IS ACT ONE AGAIN

Past the gate, `end2` drops you into a duplicated factory -- `fac W2`, `fac C2`,
`fac S2`, `fac pantry2`, `utility outside2`, `utility exit2`,
`rec plant entrance2` -- with a fresh desk key, box, key-on-a-string, door key
and service droid head, because the pantry says "all locks and keys have been put
back.  How strange.  Perhaps done by a maintainence droid?".  So the last twenty
commands are the first twenty again, and the second copy is worth having under
test: the duplicated objects carry the same aliases as the originals, and the
originals are still around (concealed, or hidden) rather than destroyed, so every
one of those commands is a resolution test.  `Desk2` even carries handlers for
both `use <desk key>` and `use <desk key2>`, the author evidently unsure which
name would win.

The route out is the same too: recycling plant, box, small door, droid head,
gate.  Then east, north and east across the island to the Science Center, GO TO
ELEVATOR 5 -- the exit the gate condition created twenty turns earlier while you
were standing in the factory -- and USE LATTER H.Q. KEY ON SMALL KEYHOLE.


THREE THINGS THIS SCRIPT HAD TO WORK AROUND

  * Every `place` has to be entered as GO TO <alias>; the bare alias is not a
    command.  That is five interrogations, two pantries, the chair, the terrace,
    the computer and the elevator.
  * Every noun has to be an object's full alias.  The game is ASL 350, and Quest
    matches a word taken from the middle of a name only for ASL 391 and up
    (Disambiguate, V4Game.cs:4762-4772; fixtures/abbrev.asl), so the two places
    where the alias is longer than the thing it names have to be typed out: the
    alley's `<Dumpster>` is `alias <Large Dumpster>` (former.asl:734-735) and the
    bedroom's `<Chest>` is `alias <Chest Underneith Your Dresser>` (590-591).
    Both have a plain-named twin -- `Dumpster2` is `alias <Dumpster>` (799-800),
    which is why X DUMPSTER is not simply an abbreviation of the first one but a
    different object, concealed until the alley has been searched.
  * USE BOX ON KEY is ambiguous -- the Recycling Plant Entrance holds both `key`
    ("Key on a String") and the `doorkey` you are about to receive ("Door Key"),
    and the disambiguation prompt eats the script's next line.  Spelled out in
    full as USE BOX ON KEY ON A STRING it resolves cleanly, which also confirms
    the two-argument `use` splits at the first " on " rather than the last.


WHAT THIS SCRIPT SKIPS

  * Most of each interrogation menu.  Only the questions that change state are
    typed; the bartender's model number, the barfly's boasting, the lady's
    "Whats behind the locked door?" (a joke: "All my dead bodies, of course")
    and the three questions Data answers in `end2` are flavour.  The script does
    ask all five opening questions, because the room text says to.
  * `Mine entrance1`, the Landfill Compactor and its self-referential south exit,
    the mansion's four-door fridge and its pool table, the `Messy Files`,
    the room called `mansion wine cellar` whose own description insists it is an
    Oil Cellar, `Broken motherboard2`,
    `Tiny Shard 2`, `chair2`, `dresser2`, `Dead Body7` and the other half-dozen
    "you already did that" replacement objects.
  * `command <hint> msg <Hints are for sissies.  Figure it out!>`, `command
    <jump>` and `command <hello>`, the game's entire help system.
  * The bar key, which the pub's handler claims you hand back ("You give the key
    back before heading out") but only `conceal`s -- it is in your inventory,
    invisibly, for the rest of the game.
