The Legend of Cyrn -- command script
==========================================

"The Legend of Cyrn" v1.0, by Graham Kelly, (c) 2002, ASL 300, "Created with
QDK 3.03".  Distributed as Magic-2.asl (1652 lines of plain ASL -- no .cas to
decompile), and one of the very few asl-version <300> games in this corpus, so
it is useful mainly as a Quest *3* feature test: `define selection` / `choose`,
`ask`, `enter`, `place`, `create exit`, `doaction`, `use <a> on <b>`,
`give <a> to <b>`, and object disambiguation resolved by `detail`.

It is explicitly a demo.  From its own intro text: "This is the demo of my
rather large, soon-to-be-released game ... I promise the full version out within
the year."  It never arrived, and the file contains no `playerwin` at all --
`define text <win>` and `define text <lose>` are both empty blocks.  The only
`playerlose` is the joke death for `wear fine cloak`.  So the furthest reachable
state is the author's own end-of-demo notice, printed from the room script of
Path9, the one room past the town's rear gate:

    define room <Path9>
        script msg <Ahead lies the Highway. Through it all of Cyrn is open to
                    you. Good Luck.|n|nEND OF DEMO>

This script reaches it in 72 turns with an otherwise clean transcript.  Marker:
    END OF DEMO


CHOOSE HUMAN, OR THE GAME IS OVER IMMEDIATELY
---------------------------------------------
The start room is `signup room`, a room with no exits at all, whose script asks
your name with `enter <playername>` and then hands off to `choose <Race>`.  Each
of the nine choices in `define selection <Race>` confirms with
`if ask <Do you wish to choose this race?>` and then `goto`s a racial starting
room -- but only Human and Half-Elf go to <Hall of Records>.  The other seven go
to Wood, Mountainside, Hole, Forest, Hut or House, and every one of those rooms
is declared with nothing but a `look` and an `indescription`: no exits, no
objects you can use, no way out.  Picking any race but 1 or 4 ends the demo on
turn zero, exactly as the intro warns.  Hence the script's first three lines:
a name, `1` (Human), `1` (Yes).

Two authoring slips in that selection are worth knowing about even though this
route dodges both.  The Dwarf branch has a redundant second `goto <Mountainside>`
after the if, so declining it still teleports you there.  And in every branch
except Human's, the `set <Race; ...>` sits *after* the if rather than inside it,
so declining a race (which recurses via `else choose <Race>`) sets Race to the
race you refused on the way back out of the recursion -- accept Elf-then-Human
and you end up a Human standing in the Hall of Records who is described as an
Elf.  Answering Yes to the first prompt avoids the whole tangle.


THE MEDALLION DEADLOCK, AND WHY THE TRAINING HALL IS MANDATORY
--------------------------------------------------------------
The first half of the demo is a three-part errand for the Curator, each part
paying out one medallion:

    speak to Curator          -> Scroll
    give Scroll to Scholar6   -> Fine Cloak       (in Hall of Records5)
    give Fine Cloak to Curator-> Medallion  (bronze), and Curator becomes Curator2
    speak to Curator2, Yes    -> moves Head Librarian2 to the Study Hall
    speak to Head Librarian, choose 1, `study books` -> the Dream
    any direction in the Dream-> wake with the Writ of Knowledge
    give Writ to Curator2     -> Medallion2 (silver), Curator becomes Curator3
    speak to Curator3, Yes    -> Medallion3 (gold)

The gold medallion is what opens the silver gates:

    define room <Gateway>
        east if got <Medallion3> then goto <outside the great gates>
             else msg <The gates will not let you pass.>

and then the courtyard's exit to the outside world tests the same flag the other
way round:

    define room <outside the great gates>
        north if got <Medallion3> then msg <You try, but it seems a magical
              barrier is blocking the way.> else goto <Path>

Read literally that is a deadlock: you can only leave northwards while lacking
the very medallion that let you in, and Medallion3's `drop` is refused ("I think
the Curator wants you to keep that"), while the only container that could hold
it -- the knapsack -- is for sale in a shop on the far side of the barrier.

The escape hatch is the Training Hall, which looks like optional flavour and
isn't.  The Magic Chamber's third lesson does all of this at once:

    command <cast shield spell> ... {
        goto <Training Hall3>
        lose <Medallion> ... lose <Medallion2> ... lose <Medallion3> ...
        create exit east <Hall of Records; outside the great gates>
        create exit west <outside the great gates; Hall of Records>
        set <Gold; 200>
        timeron <food timer>  timeron <drink timer>
    }

Confiscating the medallions is what unlocks `north`, and the same script is the
game's only source of money.  The two lessons also have to be taken in the order
combat-then-magic, because each one moves you to a different copy of the hall:
`attack dummy` drops you in Training Hall2, whose only inner door is east to the
Magic Chamber, and `cast shield spell` drops you in Training Hall3, which has no
inner doors at all.  Take magic first and the Combat Chamber is unreachable for
the rest of the game.  (All it costs is +1 agility, which nothing reads, but the
one-way room shuffle is the interesting part.)


TWO SCHOLARS THAT LOOK IDENTICAL
--------------------------------
Hall of Records5 holds Scholar5 and Scholar6, with identical `prefix <a>`,
`suffix <taking notes>` and `displaytype <human>`, so the room lists "There is a
Scholar taking notes and a Scholar taking notes here."  They differ only by
`detail <Sleeping>` and `detail <Awake>`, and only Scholar6 -- the awake one,
whose `look` mentions a red-orange cloak -- has the `give to` handler that trades
the Scroll for the Fine Cloak.  So `give scroll to scholar` raises

    Which scholar do you mean?
    1) Sleeping
    2) Awake

and the script answers `2`.  Forget that answer and the menu silently eats the
following command (menu input goes through atoi, so a direction word parses as 0
and clamps to option 1), which is how the first draft of this script gave the
scroll to a sleeping man and then walked into a wall for sixty turns.

The five Hall of Records rooms are likewise all `alias <Hall of Records>` with
one shared `look`, so the map has to be walked by exits rather than recognised
by description:

    HoR   E->Gateway   W->HoR2
    HoR2  N->HoR4      S->HoR3   E->HoR
    HoR3  N->HoR2      W->HoR5            (HoR5 also NW->Hall of Learning)
    HoR4  N->Hall2 (the Curator's study)  S->HoR2

The Dream is nine identical blocks: all eight compass directions run the same
wake-up script, hand over the Writ, and `goto <Study Hall>`, so any direction
works.


THE OIL CHAIN
-------------
Everything after the gates is one four-item craft:

    speak to Merchant (Path4), choice 1  -> bag of clay, 50 of your 200 gold
    mold clay                            -> a game-level command that
                                            `doaction <bag of clay; mold clay>`,
                                            setting clayshape to "lopsided pot"
    use bag of clay on kiln (Potting Room, SW of Town Square then south)
                                         -> Lopsided Pot (the clay is not spent)
    take olive tree (Path5)              -> Olives (its `look` hands them over
                                            too, so looking at the tree twice
                                            is a free olive dispenser)
    use olives on mortar and pestle (Apothecary, W of Town Square) -> Oil
    use oil on lopsided pot              -> Oil-filled Pot
    give oil-filled pot to guard (Path8) -> create exit north <Path8; Path9>

The Guard states the price himself if you `speak to` him ("You must submit one
jar of oil as an Offering of Passage"), via a one-choice `define selection`.
Note the clay must be moulded before it goes in the kiln -- the `use on <kiln>`
handler tests `if ( #clayshape# = lopsided pot )` and otherwise refuses.


DEAD ENDS AND DEAD CODE (none of it on the critical path)
--------------------------------------------------------
  * The gold bar is a money pump: 35gp from the shopkeeper, then `give gold bar
    to Smelter` for a 5gp fee returns 100gp, for +60 per lap.  It also carries
    `type <valuable>`, whose `action <steal>` yanks the bar to the alcove off
    Town Square.  With 200 gold from training and one 50gp purchase needed, none
    of it matters.
  * Two of the inn's three rooms cannot be entered.  `Hall`'s doors test
    `if ( Smalllock = 1 )` and `if ( Medlock = 1 )` -- the bare variable names,
    not `%Smalllock%` / `%Medlock%`, so Quest 3 compares the literal strings
    "Smalllock" and "Medlock" against "1" and the condition is never true.  The
    Small Key's own `use on <East Door>` compounds it by setting Smalllock to 1
    in *both* arms of its if, and the Medium Key has no `take` at all.  Only
    Hall3's west door uses `%Grandlock%` correctly, so the Grand Room is the one
    reachable bedroom -- and the `inn` selection never checks or deducts gold, so
    the 100gp suite is free.  Sleeping only bumps Daystatus, which gates the
    merchant's buy-back option and the Potter's excuse line.
  * All four timers are real-time (`interval <60>` for light and shield,
    `interval <1200>` for food and drink) and the harness does not advance them
    without --tick, so this script runs without it.  Just as well: `Health` is
    never defined anywhere in the file, so the food and drink timers decrement a
    variable that does not exist.
  * The game block ends every turn with `afterturn do <merchant_proc>`, and no
    `define procedure <merchant_proc>` exists -- the only procedure in the file
    is `statusproc`.  QuestViva's ExecuteDo reports the miss as
    `LogASLError("No such procedure " + procedureName, LogType.WarningError)`
    (V4Game.cs:5809-5813), which is a log-channel warning and not player-visible
    text, so geas's silent skip leaves the transcript identical.
  * `command <wear fine cloak>` is the whole game's only death: the Head Scholar
    appears, tells you off, and `playerlose`.  Carrying the cloak to the Curator
    unworn is the "first test".
  * The Bakery is defined with a description and no exits and is never linked to
    anything, and the Spear in the Goblin/Troll hut can only be taken by a race
    that cannot leave the room it is in.
