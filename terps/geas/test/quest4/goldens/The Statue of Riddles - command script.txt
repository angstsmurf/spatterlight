"The Statue of Riddles" -- Andrew "Thanatos" Trewin, QDK Pro 4.04, finished 9 February 2009
Command script for geas_walkthrough_runner.  57 turns; wins.

WHY THIS GAME IS IN THE SUITE

Because for years it was not playable at all.  The Dome of Wits -- the last room, the one
with the ending in it -- is described as "This massive dome is completely blank. Yep, 100%
empty."  That stray percent sign has no partner, and geas used to `throw` out of
GeasFile::static_eval when it hit one.  The throw was caught all the way out in set_game,
which quietly abandoned the rest of startup, so the game printed no title, no intro and no
room description, and every command afterwards was a no-op.  One typo in a joke, 500 lines
away from the start, and the whole game was a black screen.

Quest never had that problem: each pass of ConvertParameter (V4Game.cs:6697-6701) logs
"Line parameter <...> has missing %" as a WarningError -- which is a log-channel entry the
player never sees -- and substitutes the literal text "<ERROR>" for the whole parameter,
then carries on.  geas now does the same on the runtime paths; see
fixtures/unmatchedpercent.asl for the one place it still can't (a definition-level property,
because the marker's angle brackets don't survive re-serialization into a
`properties <name=value>` line).

THE SHAPE OF THE GAME

Nine Stone Faces are scattered across the map.  Speaking to one teleports you into a Room of
Riddles, where the Statue of Riddles asks a riddle and `choose`s from a twelve-way selection;
eleven answers kill you outright ("Unfortunetly you didn't notice the giant axe attached to
the roof") and the twelfth hands over an item, teleports you back out, and shuts that face's
mouth.  So the map only opens up in the order the riddles are answered, and the walkthrough is
essentially a fixed sequence of Face -> Statue -> number.

The nine answers, by riddle number:

  1  no beginning, no end, symbol of the world's cycles ......  6  A circle
  2  hammer to break myself, melted by fire ..................  2  A pebble
  3  spirited jig, wind feeds me, water is my bane ...........  6  Fire
  4  weighs nothing, but a box with it is lighter ............ 11  A hole
  5  a man stepped on me, sat to find me, left with me .......  1  A splinter
  6  captures a picture, kept forever, gone when you're dead ..  9  A memory
  7  not dirt nor grime, struggle and I'll pull you deep .....  5  Quicksand
  8  "Qaz Wsx Edc Rfv Tgb Yhn Ujm Ik Ol?" ....................  5  The letter P
  9  alive without breath, dressed in mail but never a clink .  4  A fish

Riddle 8 is the one worth pausing on: those are the columns of a QWERTY keyboard read
downwards, and the missing column is P.

THE OPENING DEADLOCK

The temple's own front door is the last thing you can open.  Stone Face1 at the Ruin Entrance
is declared `hidden`, buried behind thorny Vines that can only be cut with the Machete; the
Machete is riddle 4's reward on the Island; the Island can only be reached by canoe; and the
canoe's two oars are riddles 2 and 3, out on the Cliff and down in the Cave.  So the whole
first act runs backwards through the numbering -- 2, 3, 4, then 1 -- and only riddle 1 swaps
the Huge Door for "Huge Door (Open)" and `create exit north` into the Entrance Hall.

The canoe is assembled from three separate objects (Canoe, CanoeRightOar, CanoeLeftOar) so
that either oar may be fitted first, plus a fourth copy (CanoeBothOars2) parked on the Island
so the trip home works.  Both oars carry `alt <Oar>`, so "use oar on canoe" is ambiguous --
this script always names them in full.  Note also that `command <use canoe>` in the Lake is
guarded by `if here <CanoeBothOars>` with no `else`: row before the canoe is finished and the
game says absolutely nothing.

THE FOUR ELEMENTAL ROOMS AND THE SKELETON

The Entrance Hall has four doors, one per element, each holding a Stone Face (riddles 5-8) and
each yielding one piece of Thanatos's kit: Helm (fire), Armour (water), Sword (wind), Shield
(earth).  The Skeleton of Thanatos is `parent <Sarcophagus>`, so the sarcophagus must be
opened before he is in scope at all, and his `speak` gates on `got` all four pieces at once --
with anything less he just repeats the couplet.  Hand over all four and he crumbles, dropping
the Ghost Key, which is `use`d on the Strange Door to be "sucked inside" the Madness Room.

THE MADNESS ROOM

Three headless/bodiless/legless ghosts, and they must be spoken to in order: Aeven refuses
until `not here <Estaq>` and Monty until `not here <Aeven>` (a solved ghost is `move`d to the
Storeroom, which is how "not here" doubles as "already answered").  Answers: Estaq 4 (A
sword), Aeven 3 (The stars), Monty 12 (Silence).  Monty's script has a visible slip -- the
scolding line is emitted unconditionally before the gate is tested, so even on the turn he
finally asks his riddle he opens with "Aeven is the second one thy must speak to."  Monty's
success `create exit north` into the Circular Room.

THE ENDGAME

Riddle 9 in the Circular Room creates the `up` exit (and drops a Ladder that is pure scenery --
the room's own description promises "If you had a ladder you could climb it", but the exit is
made by the riddle, not by the ladder).  Up is the Bridge, where the now-armoured Thanatos
blocks the north exit with a script exit rather than a flag: `north if here <Thanatos> then msg
<...> else goto <Dome of Wits>`.  His three riddles are chained selections -- answering one
`choose`s the next inside the same turn -- so three answers are consumed by a single "speak to
thanatos": 3 (the prince is 30 and the princess is 40), 1 (Freedom!), 10 (Bacteria).  Then
north, and speaking to the Overlord -- who is a Hobbit -- runs the Chuck Norris gag and the
credits, and `playerwin`.  The win marker is the last credit line, "One Hobbit was forced into
slave labour in the making of this game."

AUTHORING QUIRKS WORTH KNOWING

* Riddle 6, choice 3 ("Shadows") prints the *wrong*-answer message -- axe, roof, and all --
  and then runs `playerwin` instead of `playerlose`.  Answering the sixth riddle wrongly in
  exactly one specific way therefore wins the game outright, roughly a third of the way in.
  This script deliberately takes the long road; keep the marker on the credits text so the
  regression covers the whole game and not the typo.
* Riddle 2's success script forgets to place Shut Stone Face2, and moves Stone Face2 to the
  Cliff it is already standing on.  The face on the Cliff therefore never closes: its riddle
  can be answered again and again, spawning another Right Oar each time.
* Riddle 5's success uses `background <...>` where every other riddle uses `msg <...>`, so the
  "Correct. You have survived this round." line arrives through a different channel.
* `define selection <playthegame>` is never `choose`n by anything.  It belongs to a completely
  different, item-passing Imp game, and answering "No" to it would melt the player into an Imp.
  It is dead code that survived a copy-paste.
* The Storeroom is the author's offstage junk drawer -- every "used up" object is `move`d there
  -- and it has no exits, so it is unreachable in play.  Its description ("A riddle to hard for
  you? By all means, look around... Well, I can. But meh.") is aimed at players reading the ASL
  rather than playing the game, and it holds a Scroll whose `look` prints a CONGRATULATIONS
  banner and calls `playerwin`: a second, entirely unreachable ending.  Also in there: a
  baseball bat marked "Property of Tr0n", blunt scissors, and a jar of red dye, none of which
  any script ever mentions again.
