Something 'Bout A Hex (Paul Flum, © Paul Flum Games 2007, QDK 4.0; ASL 400,
`gametype singleplayer', start <START>) -- 6612 lines of autobiographical
Baltimore memoir told out of chronological order, and one of the largest Quest 4
games in the corpus.  This transcript plays BOOK ONE and BOOK TWO end to end.

What the game is.  The narrator recorded twenty minutes of organ music on a
Casio SK-1 through a reverb pedal and a Fender amp in November 1997, decided it
was a soundtrack, and drove Baltimore -> Louisville -> Memphis -> New Orleans
filming a video for each track.  A brick he pocketed at Saint Louis Cemetery
Number One in New Orleans becomes the "hex" of the title, and the game's
structure is a colour-coded index of the years the brick follows him through:
1 9 r e d 9 1, 1 9 o r a n g e 9 7, 1 9 y e l l o w 9 8, 2 0 g r e e n 0 1,
2 0 b l u e 0 2, 2 0 i n d i g o 0 3, 2 0 v i o l e t 0 4.  Each of those is a
`define room' AND an `invisible' object; picking the object up is how you jump to
the year.  The seven jumps are the spine of Book Two, and they are not in
chronological order -- the transcript below runs
1998 -> 2001 -> 1991 -> 1998 -> 2002 -> 1997 -> 2004 -> 2001 -> 1997 -> 2003 ->
2004 -> 1998 -> 2001 -> 1991 -> 1997 -> 2004, which is the order the author
wrote and the only order the flags permit.

--tick is required and the timers are the whole game.  Almost every scene
transition in Book Two is a chain of `timeron' steps that only advance while
turns pass, so the long runs of `look' in this transcript ARE the route.  The
chains, with the filler counts this script uses:

  redlionsleep / 2 / redlionslleep3    2/3/4    14 turns   Sea-Tac, Aug 1991
  redsoxtimer .. redsoxtimer8          8 x 3    38 turns   St Charles Motel dream
  threeyearsleep .. threeyearsleep5    5 x 3    23 turns   the three-year nap
  vinny timer .. vinny timer8   15,7,7,7,7,7,7,1  70 turns  Vinny dies
  nobal1 .. nobal9                     9 x 3    43 turns   New Orleans -> 2003
  seattledrive .. seattledrive5        5 x 3    23 turns   sightseeing Seattle
  zzz .. zzz8                          8 x 4    46 turns   the closing dream

Those counts are larger than interval x steps because Quest's `timeron' does not
reset the tick counter -- `SetTimerState' only flips TimerActive and raises
BypassThisTurn (V4Game.Part2.cs:561-574) -- so each step of a chain costs
interval + 1 turns, and because a step only ever `timeroff's its PREDECESSOR
(nobal2 turns off nobal1, not itself) the earlier links keep firing alongside the
later ones.  The repeated "...38 miles..." and "...1117 miles..." lines are that,
faithfully; they are not a geas bug.  Do not trim the filler runs.

`place' matching wants the destination's ALIAS.  At ASL >= 311 `PlaceExist'
compares the typed name against the alias of the room being entered, not its
`define room' name (V4Game.Part2.cs:6543-6590), so `go to middle of living room'
fails and `go to the middle of the living room' works, and the desk at the end is
`go to cluttered desk' rather than `go to mt vernon desk'.  Every `go to' line
below is an alias.

`use' resolves its two blanks in two different scopes.  `ExecUse' disambiguates
the item against the inventory alone and the use-on target against the current
room first, falling back to the inventory only on a miss
(V4Game.Part2.cs:5289, 5376-5390).  That is why every vehicle in this game has to
be picked up before it can be driven -- `take truck' then `use truck', `take
Honda Accord' then `use Honda Accord', `take Mini-van' then `use Mini-van' -- and
it is also what makes Dougherty's Pub work at all: Derrick pours three drinks
that ALL answer to "Jim-n-Ginger" (`Jim-N-Ginger', `Jim 2', `Jim 3', each with
that alias) and leaves the ones you have not been handed yet sitting in the bar
room, so `use Jim-N-Ginger' has exactly one candidate.  geas used to search
inventory and room together here and raise a disambiguation menu Quest never
shows, which silently ate the next line of this script.

Type the iguana's ALIAS, not its name.  The author spelled the object
`define object <iquana>' and aliased it `iguana'.  Quest's `Disambiguate' matches
the alias and the alt-names, never the real name unless the command came through
`exec' (V4Game.cs:4704-4727), so `iquana' is not a word this game knows -- and
the fake brick sitting in the same cage is `define object <fake brick iquana>',
which the ASL >= 391 abbreviation pass WOULD match on " iquana".  `take iguana'
is unambiguous; `take iquana' is not.

Named-object footguns.  Three objects in this file carry trailing spaces in the
name or alias, and they matter: `define object <Journal >' is handed over as
`give <Journal >' and tested as `if got <Journal>', the 1998 brick is
`define object <fake brick iquana>' with `alias <The Brick >', and the 2004 one is
`another fake brick' with `alias <The Brick  >' (two).  They never collide in
practice because each take moves its predecessor to `KILLED OBJECTS' first.

Book One, in brief.  November 1997: wire the reverb pedal (9-volt battery from
the desk), record the organ tape in the music room, put the tape in the walkman,
and the game moves you to the roof to start filming.  Then the drive south --
the OhioInn in Louisville (DO NOT DISTURB sign on the doorknob so you can type,
brochure at the checkout counter to release the truck), Christ Church, Memphis,
Saint Louis Cemetery Number One where you take the brick, Bourbon Street,
the French Quarter, the NOMA.  Book One ends with a cellphone call to the
courier company on September 11, 2001 and `choose <DOYAWANTBOOKONE>'; answering
either way reveals `BEGIN BOOK TWO', which is the object that starts the second
half.

Book Two, scene by scene, with the puzzle in each.

  1998, 30th Street apartment.  You arrive with the fake truck; the front door
  refuses to let you into the foyer until you have run the hallway to the
  bathroom (`gotbathroom'), and doing that destroys the foyer exit and turns the
  lobby into 2 0 g r e e n 0 1.
  2001, midafternoon, Sept 11.  The TV is on and the phone rings: `take
  telephone' opens the `mortalarthur' chain (2/2/2/1/1/1).  The answering machine
  has two messages and has to be taken twice -- the first is Morphius, the second
  is Sea-Tac and it teleports you to August 1991.
  1991, Red Lion Hotel, Sea-Tac.  Take the concert T-shirt (which is what reveals
  the bed), read the journal's FINAL ENTRY, sleep, and the redlionsleep chain
  reveals 1 9 y e l l o w 9 8.
  1998 again, and the sick iguana.  Colin the roommate is in the kitchen
  (`colinsavannah', 2/2/2/1) and telling him about Savannah is what makes Vinny
  ill.  `look at iguana' while he is ill reveals the refrigerator; the crisper has
  romaine lettuce; feeding him reveals the Gargoyle Statue, which reveals the blue
  heatrock, which electrocutes you into 2 0 b l u e 0 2.
  2002, Dougherty's Pub.  The longest conversation in the game and the only place
  a wrong answer strands you.  Derrick pours; Sam is revealed by the first drink
  and Raissa by the second; the two of them alternate, and Raissa's fifteenth
  node ("You gotta take that brick back!") is what opens `leave02'.  The order is
  Derrick 3/1 + drink, Sam 1/1, Derrick 1/1 + drink, Raissa 2/1/1/1,
  Derrick 1/1 + drink, Raissa 2/2/1/1/2/1, Sam 2/1, Raissa 2/2/2/1/1.  Then the
  restroom sink is 1 9 o r a n g e 9 7.
  1997, St Charles Motel, New Orleans.  The typewritten page is the Nov 13
  itinerary; taking it reveals the bed; the redsox dream is 2 0 v i o l e t 0 4.
  2004, Mt Vernon, the night the Red Sox beat the Yankees in Game Five.  Shut the
  TV off with the remote, and the window sill hands you the brick -> the Rodan
  puzzle -> the PC, whose closed MSN window reveals the Toyota Camry.  Driving to
  Holy Frijoles drops you on I-95 on the morning of September 11, 2001.
  2001, Philadelphia.  The airport is closed; `use cellphone' -> work -> Sally
  (2/2/2) sends you home and opens the 30th Street exit.  The Mojo's Pizza
  doorhanger on the front door is 1 9 o r a n g e 9 7 and creates the two St
  Charles Motel exits.
  1997, the last morning in New Orleans.  Two songs still need video.  Sync the
  walkman to the camcorder, drive the truck to the NOMA (`gettonoma' choice 3),
  film the geese on the pond in Color (choice 1 of `camerafilter'; any other
  filter is refused), pick up the spanish moss and throw it in the bedliner --
  which is what lets `gettonoma' choice 1, Baltimore, work -- then take the road
  sign and sit out nobal1..nobal9.
  2003, Casa Des Arachnid.  A single beat: walk up to the locked rancher
  (`sawcasa') so the mini-van will let you in, then drive downtown.
  2004, moving in.  The legal paper box needs the kitchen knife; then snow globe
  -> virgin mary candle -> dried rose -> crocheted doilies -> tubes of incense ->
  THE BRICK, and taking it throws you back to 1998.
  1998, third act.  The trash bag of Savannah greenery decorates the master
  bedroom -- ficus tree, hanging plants, aquarium, all three -- and only then does
  re-entering the room reveal the bed.  The nap lasts three years.
  2001, Nov 15.  Vinny has laid eggs and is dying.  Nothing you do saves him:
  `take iguana' reveals the refrigerator, the refrigerator arms `vinny timer', and
  the eight-step chain kills him no matter which of the romaine/cantelope/spinach
  you offer.  His death is what reveals the lens cap on the roof.
  1991, Aug 27, AKA Books, Seattle.  The Lollapalooza poster reveals the band
  flyer, the flyer opens the checkout line and moves Eli into it.  Eli will not
  talk to you ("He is helping other customers") until the pretty girl has left,
  and she only leaves if you speak to her -- that arms `prettygirl timer', and
  ELI: "See you later, wondergirl" is the cue.  Then `eliaka' (2/1/2/1/1) buys the
  Vonnegut and gets you the directions to Enumclaw.
  1997, Nov 15, the Georgia -- South Carolina border.  The final shot.  `oncoming
  traffic' is revealed by a timer on interval 3 and concealed again by another on
  interval 2, so it is only in scope three turns in every five: sync the walkman
  first, wait three turns, and film on the fourth.  Then ZZZ..., and the zzz chain
  recites all seven colours back at you.
  2004, Oct 19, 11:45 PM.  You woke at the desk.  `take computer' re-opens the MSN
  window, you press SEND, and that is the end of the story.

No `playerwin', no `playerlose', no `stop'.  The win marker is the congratulation
printed by `choose <octoberiffic>' in its `finalscene' branch (asl 5894),
"completed BOOK TWO of Something 'Bout a Hex"; the last line of the transcript
answers `DOYAWANTBOOKTWO' with yes so the BOOK TWO SCRIPT and BONUS FEATURES land
in the inventory, which is where the author's own fifteen-scene synopsis
(`define text <BOOK TWO SCRIPT>', asl 5103-5185) can be read.  The game keeps
running afterwards.
