Easy Money -- Keyz Karanza, v1.0, ASL 210
=========================================

Two rooms, no objects, no puzzles: this is a commodities-trading game built
entirely out of Quest's collectables, and it is the corpus's test case for them.
You start in the Stock Exchange with 100 dollars and 1000 points, and you win
the moment you hold more than 4999 dollars.  Going OUT to the street loses.

    afterturn {
    if has <Money; +4999> then do <winner>
    if is <#quest.currentroom#;the street> then playerlose
    }

Note "+4999", not "=5000": `has <name; +N>` is *strictly* greater than N, so the
target is 5000 dollars and the intro's "Parle your 100 dollars into 5000" is
exactly right.

Collectables
------------
The whole game state is one line of the game block: 34 collectables, three per
company (stock held, price per share, last change) plus Money, Score and five
scratch values the Buy and Sell dialogues pass around.

    collectables <Money dp=100 [! Dollars]; Score p=1000 [! Points];
                  BCBBstock dp=0 [! BCBB shares]; BCBBstat n=10 [];
                  BCBBchange n=0 []; ... BuyChoice dp=0 []; ...>

"dp" is a "p" range -- never below zero -- with the "d" meaning "leave this line
out of the status pane while it is zero", which is how the pane shows only the
shares you actually own.  The eight "[]" price and change entries have an empty
display string and so are never shown at all; the game prints them itself in the
stock report.  All eight companies start at 10 dollars a share.

Two things in here only work at all because the game declares ASL 210.  Below
2.80 there are no numeric or string variables, so a bare `set <X; +1>` *is* a
collectable set; and below 3.20 `~X~` in a parameter is the collectable's value,
which is how the game does arithmetic on them:

    setvar <Cost; ~BuyNumber~*~BCBBstat~>
    if has <Money; -%Cost%> then msg <You don't have enough money!>
        else do <RingMeUpBCBBFinal>

`setvar` is the one place a real numeric variable appears, and it is used purely
as a scratch pad, because `%Cost%` can then be substituted back into the next
`set`.  DetermLow and DetermHigh do the same thing in reverse -- after clamping a
price they copy it into a same-named numeric variable with
`setvar <BCBBstat; ~BCBBstat~>` -- so that the report's `%BCBBstat%` has
something to read.

Note also `if has <BCBBstock; -SellNumber>`: the operand is not a number, so
Quest reads it as the name of another collectable, and SellNumber is one.  That
is the "you don't have that much stock" check, and it works.  (Contrast
lovesong.asl, which writes `if has <chrlock;1>` with no +/-/= at all: the
operator is then the character "1", which matches nothing, and the condition is
false for ever.)

How the market moves
--------------------
NEXT costs 8 points and redraws the market:

    setvar <ratel; $rand(-7;-1)$>
    setvar <rateh; $rand(1;7)$>
    set <BCBBchange; =$rand(%ratel%;%rateh%)$>      (and seven more)

so every company in a given turn draws its change from the same range, and that
range is itself random -- a turn can be capped at -1..+7 or at -7..+1.  Prices
are then clamped to 1..50.  Buying and selling do not move the market at all, so
under a fixed seed the price path depends on nothing but the number of NEXTs
issued, and a script can be written against it.  This one is written against
GEAS_SEED=1, which run_walkthroughs.sh sets; the whole corpus is seeded that way.

The trading dialogue
--------------------
BUY and SELL each open a `choose` menu of the eight companies (1 = Bloated Colon
Baked Beans, 2 = Laughin Jacks Antidepressants, 3 = Pizza Face Pimple Cream,
4 = Psycho Mutt Dog Treats, 5 = Sea Bass Feminine Deodorant, 6 = Skull Crusher
Pain Reliever, 7 = Sinead O Conner Hair Tonic, 8 = Tough Luck Bail Bonds) and
then `enter` the number of shares.  So each trade is three script lines: the
command, the menu number, the share count.  There is no confirmation and no
output on success -- a trade that works prints nothing at all, which is why the
transcript below is mostly stock reports.

The affordability test is `if has <Money; -%Cost%>`, i.e. "money strictly less
than the cost", so spending your last dollar to the penny is allowed.  That
matters: every buy below is for the largest whole number of shares the cash
covers.

The route
---------
Seven round trips, seven NEXTs, and the win lands on the seventh -- which is as
early as this seed allows.  The market path depends on nothing but the number of
NEXTs, so the whole price table can be read off a run of bare NEXTs and the best
route solved for exactly: at every turn, hold the one stock that buys the most
dollars back later.  Prices are per share, at the turn named:

    turn  0  buy  10 Bloated Colon   @10  -- 100 dollars, all of it
    turn  1  sell                    @11  ->  110
    turn  1  buy  13 Laughin Jacks   @ 8
    turn  2  sell                    @12  ->  162
    turn  2  buy  23 Sea Bass        @ 7
    turn  3  sell                    @10  ->  231
    turn  3  buy  46 Sinead O Conner @ 5
    turn  4  sell                    @ 9  ->  415
    turn  4  buy 415 Pizza Face      @ 1  -- the clamp floor
    turn  5  sell                    @ 2  ->  830
    turn  5  buy 830 Bloated Colon   @ 1  -- and again, which decides the game
    turn  6  sell                    @ 6  -> 4980
    turn  6  buy 996 Tough Luck      @ 5
    turn  7  sell                    @ 6  -> 5976, and the win fires

Two stocks hitting the clamp floor of 1 on consecutive turns is what makes seven
turns enough: a dollar a share turns 415 dollars into 415 shares, and 830 into
830, and the two doublings-and-then-some compound into a sixfold turn.  Turn 6
still lands 20 dollars short of the target -- 4980 is the most any route holds
there -- so the last round trip is forced rather than a flourish.

The ending
----------
`winner` prints "Your final score is ~Score~ points!" -- 944, since seven NEXTs
cost 8 points each -- and then `playerwin`, which shows the game's `define text
<win>`: "YOU DID IT! Congratulations! Now you can put your kids in college...for
a week, maybe...at a community college."
