"What do you do?" (FHA.asl) -- OrenjiJusu, v0.1, ASL 410
========================================================

A fifteen-minute QDK Lite sketch, and mostly a death trap: of the twelve rooms,
five kill you on entry and one more kills you if you take any exit at all.  You
are Jeff, caught in bed with the crime boss's wife; Harry has run off to tell
Frank; Frank is on his way with a gun; your sister Alice is face-down drunk at
the bar with the car keys in her handbag.

There are two winning endings.  This script takes the one that actually prints
something, which is not the obvious one.

    define object <Honda Civic>            (Outside the Pub)
        use <Car keys> {
            playerwin
            msg <You quickly start up the car and speed off, ...|nYou havce won.>
        }

Steal Alice's keys, USE CAR KEYS ON HONDA CIVIC, and the game is won -- in
silence.  Note the order: `playerwin` comes first and the message second, and in
Quest that message never runs.  `ExecuteScript` returns immediately for every
line once the game has finished (V4Game.Part2.cs:5695-5698, the
`if (_gameFinished) return;` sitting right after the empty-line check), so
`playerwin` does not merely end the game, it abandons the rest of the enclosing
script.  The game declares no `define text <win>` either, so the car ending is
a completely silent win: the last thing on screen is the description of Outside
the Pub.  All of the ending text -- including the game's only use of the word
"won", typo and all -- is unreachable.

The author got the order right in the other ending, six lines further down the
same file, which is why this script ends with the knife instead.

The keys
--------
    define object <Alice's bag>   container  opened
        take <You find the keys inside the bag and drop the 600lbs of make up...>
        open <You manage to find the keys inside the bag and pocket them.>
    define object <Car keys>      parent <Alice's bag>
        take give <Car keys>
        drop nowhere <>

Three different authored ways to end up with the keys, and the bag is already
`opened`, so the simplest -- TAKE CAR KEYS -- works straight away.  `take give
<Car keys>` prints nothing, which is why the transcript shows no response at all
to the one command that decides the game; an I is in the script below to make it
visible.  `drop nowhere <>` means the keys cannot be dropped anywhere.

The ending this script uses
---------------------------
Frank is fought in Street with frank, which is west of Street East and which the
author wired as a point of no return: every one of its ten exits, OUT included,
leads to a room called Death whose script is `playerlose`.  So you arrive there
with a weapon or you do not arrive there.

    define object <Knife>                  (in the Bin, Street East)
        use <Frank> msg <...You have survived.>
        use on <Frank> {
            msg <...You have survived.>
            playerwin
        }

    "...you manage to catch him off guard and stab him in the gut. he manages to
     get off a shot into your kneecap... Luckily for you, frank bled out...
     You have survived."

Message first, `playerwin` second, so the text is printed and the win marker has
something to match.  Both spellings of the use handler are defined and carry the
same prose; only the two-argument `use on <Frank>` form has the `playerwin`, so
USE KNIFE ON FRANK is the phrasing that ends the game -- a bare USE KNIFE with
Frank present prints the same paragraph and leaves you standing there.

The Pistol from the Alleyway corpse in Street
west loses instead ("Frank already had his drawn"), and the Machete from behind
the Titanic Poster in Frank's office has no `use on <Frank>` at all -- only a
`use on <Harry>`, which reduces him to lumps of meat and is entirely optional.
Harry can also be killed bare-handed via a `properties <Kill = ...>` /
`action <Kill>` pair, the only verb definition in the game.

Everything that kills you
-------------------------
  * Hamleaf Town Pub ,f  -- north from Outside the Pub.  A second copy of the
    starting room, `alias <Hamleaf town Pub>`, whose look text is Frank shooting
    you between the eyes and whose `script` is `playerlose`.  Its south exit
    points at "Outside the bar", a room that does not exist.
  * Country road -- east from Street East.  Frank runs you down with the car.
  * Rip in reality -- the `place <Rip in reality>` object in Street South, and
    the room behind it.  A velociraptor in a tweed suit and top hat.
  * Death -- reached from any exit of Street with frank.
  * Street South itself is survivable; only entering the rip is not.

The bin's look script is `msg <Knife>` followed by the real description, so
LOOK AT BIN prints a bare line reading "Knife" before it prints anything else --
the author debugging in public.

The map
-------
                Hamleaf Town Pub ,f (death)
                        |  (north)
    Frank's office -- Outside the Pub -- Street East -- Country road (death)
                     /      |       \                        (west back)
        Street west         |        Street South
             |              |             |
        Street with frank --+-------------+      -> all exits: Death

    Hamleaf Town Pub -- east --> The bar -- west --> HTP, after bar -- south -->
    Outside the Pub

The pub is really three rooms sharing two aliases: the start room, "HTP, after
bar" (same description plus "You hear shouting coming from up stairs"), and the
lethal ",f" copy.  Going east then west from the start therefore does not return
you where you began -- it advances the plot one notch.

The car keys are collected on the way anyway: they are the one command in the
game that decides an ending, and carrying them past Frank costs nothing.
