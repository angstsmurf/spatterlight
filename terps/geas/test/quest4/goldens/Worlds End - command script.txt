World's End! (worldsend/world's end.asl, v1.0, by GAMER!, QDK Pro 4.03,
ASL 400) - walkthrough.
Win marker: "slumps to the ground dead", the opening of the game's `define
text <win>`; the run ends on its "(to be continued)".

The first input is the "tester" that answers the startscript's `enter <name>`
prompt, not a command.

Run with --tick, for the dynamite: `define timer <dynamite>` has interval <5>
and starts disabled; lighting the stick planted by the spaceship turns it on,
and the explosion hides the spaceship, which is what lets "examine crop
circle" in the fields reveal the sleepville security chip the game needs
later.  The six LOOKs after lighting it are the fuse burning down.  Without
ticks it never explodes.

The two fights, and why this script has no --save-scum
------------------------------------------------------
This script used to be the one walkthrough that needed the runner's
--save-scum and --fight extras: the game decides its two fights with
$rand(1;2)$ / $rand(1;3)$ draws, and a losing streak kills you outright.  But
under GEAS_SEED=1 the draw stream is a fixed xoshiro128** sequence -- the
same sequence the oracle's qv4 replays (QVH_SEED=1) -- and this game makes
*no draw at all* before the desert, so every draw either fight will ever see
can be computed in advance.  The fight turns below were solved offline
against that stream (geas's $rand(a;b)$ is lower + draw % range,
geas-runner.cc), then verified by replay; they are not luck, and rerolling is
never needed.

  * The hovering cube (sahara desert): each vial takes 5 of its 10 health on
    a $rand(1;2)$ coin, while `define timer <cube>` (interval <1>) fires a
    laser back on the same coin.  Both vials are given by the desert room
    script -- there is nothing to take -- and both are `alias <vial>`, so the
    first USE VIAL ON CUBE is a two-way disambiguation menu; the 1 after each
    of the first two throws answers it (vial1, defined first, is entry 1).  A
    missed vial1 is caught and can be thrown again; a missed vial2 prints
    nothing at all.  Under seed 1 the fight is: vial1 miss, vial1 hit (the
    cube's laser hits back), vial2 hit -- and once vial1 is gone the third
    throw needs no menu answer.
  * The alien warlord (arena): your laser does 10 of its 100 health on
    $rand(1;2)$, and an afterturn script fires back for 10 of your 100 on
    $rand(1;3)>1$ -- a 2/3 chance, so trading shot for shot loses: spamming
    the laser from this stream position is dead in 17 turns, 8 hits to its
    10.  The script instead idles on four turns -- 7, 9, 12 and 15 of the
    fight, as SPEAK TO WARLORD, which draws nothing -- so that the afterturn
    draw eats the values that would have been losing shots and every laser
    lands on a favourable one.  19 turns: six hits, two misses, an idle, a
    hit, an idle, two misses, an idle, a miss and a hit, an idle, four hits.
    The arena's room script heals you to 100 on entry, and the afterturn does
    not fire on the entry turn -- the first draw of the fight is the first
    shot's.

Aliases: what this script may and may not type
----------------------------------------------
Quest's matcher accepts an object's alias and never its definition name once
an alias replaces it (ObjectAlias is *initialised* to the name and
*overwritten* by `alias', V4Game.cs:4738-4760), and geas now agrees, so:

  * All six teleporter chips -- heatville, sleepville, awakeville, bloodbath
    city, dumbville, darkness -- are `alias <security chip>`, and TAKE/USE
    SECURITY CHIP is the only way to name any of them.  The device hides
    each chip as it is inserted, so the phrase is unambiguous at every use
    but one: when the sleepville chip is inserted the awakeville chip (given
    by snowville's computer) is already in inventory, so that USE SECURITY
    CHIP ON DEVICE is a two-way menu, answered 1 (sleepville, defined
    first); the next insertion is the awakeville chip, alone again.
  * The heatville screwdriver seller is `trader' aliased "salesman": SPEAK
    TO SALESMAN.
  * `paper2' (the pen-marked paper) and `triangle2' (the clay triangle) are
    aliased "paper" and "triangle", and their originals are hidden by the
    time they exist, so the plain nouns reach them.
  * The two fields aliens share the alias "alien": the first USE SMG ON
    ALIEN is a two-way menu, answered 1; the second is unambiguous.

The other menus are the game's own `ask'/`choose' prompts (teleport
destinations, shop offers, the bus fare, the suitcase's 1873 code) and each
is answered by the line after it.  GO TO ELEVATORROOM really is the room's
definition name -- `go to' matches rooms by name, not through the object
matcher -- and TAKE ICE GEM was dropped from older revisions of this script:
alien2's death script already does `give <ice gem>`, so the take was a
scripted "I can't see that here.".
